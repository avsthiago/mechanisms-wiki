No. 452: Double-acting pump

A pump that moves water on the way down and on the way up. The cylinder is closed at both ends, so the piston has water on both sides. One side draws water in while the other side pushes it out.

How it works

Watch the piston go down. Above it the space gets bigger, so water is drawn in through the top right flap. Below it the space gets smaller, so water is pushed out through the bottom left flap.

On the way up it is the other way round. The bottom right flap lets water in under the piston, and the top left flap lets it out above. The flaps only open one way, so water always comes in on the right and leaves on the left. No stroke is wasted.

Try pausing at the very top or bottom of a stroke. All four flaps are shut there, because the piston stops for a moment. Then watch the delivered count climb on both strokes. Choose Closed to see the pump as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Double-acting pump. Cylinder closed at each end, and piston-rod passes through stuffing-box on one end, and the cylinder has four openings covered by valves, two for admitting water and like number for discharge. A is suction-pipe, and B discharge-pipe. When piston moves down, water rushes in at suction-valve, 1, on upper end of cylinder, and that below piston is forced through valve, 3, and discharge-pipe, B ; on the piston ascending again, water is forced through discharge-valve, 4, on upper end of cylinder, and water enters lower suction-valve, 2.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The front half of the pump is cut away so the inside shows. The body is drawn as a flat box, with a square piston to match, so all the passages show at once. The handle on the rod, the shape of the flaps and all the sizes were made up. The litres count assumes a made-up size for the piston.
