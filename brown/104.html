No. 104: Worm, wheel and slide

A long screw, called a worm, lies across the top of a gear. Turn the worm and the gear turns slowly. Turn the gear instead and it walks along the worm, carrying its slide with it. This is how lathes move a cutting tool in small, exact steps.

How it works

Turn the worm. Its thread slides sideways under the worm wheel, and each thread pushes one tooth along. The worm wheel turns, but only one tooth for each turn of the worm. The slide does not move.

Now turn the worm wheel instead. The worm cannot turn, so the worm wheel has to climb along its thread, like a gear rolling on a rack. The slide goes with it. One full turn of the worm wheel moves the slide the length of its rim.

Try turning the worm, then the worm wheel, and watch the slide and the red tooth each time. When the slide reaches the end of the screw, the turning goes the other way.

In Brown's words

In this, rotary motion is imparted to the wheel by the rotation of the screw, or rectilinear motion of the slide by the rotation of the wheel. Used in screw-cutting and slide-lathes.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The bed, the two posts that hold the worm, the hand wheel, the marks on the bed, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. Brown does not say which part is held still; here the slide is held when the worm turns, and the worm is held when the worm wheel turns. The worm wheel has plain straight teeth, as drawn. A real one has slanted, hollowed teeth.
