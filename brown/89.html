No. 89: Eccentric

A round disc is fixed on a shaft, but set off its centre. This is called an eccentric. As the shaft turns, the disc wobbles round and pushes a rod to and fro. Steam engines used this to open and close their valves.

How it works

The shaft turns, and the disc turns with it. The disc is not centred on the shaft, so its centre swings round in a small circle. Watch the red peg.

The strap fits round the disc like a loose collar. It cannot turn, because the rod holds it, so it just follows the centre of the disc. When the peg is on the right, the rod is pushed out. When it is on the left, the rod is pulled back. It works like a crank, but the shaft can pass straight through.

The rod travels twice as far as the disc's centre sits from the shaft. Try Small, then As drawn, and watch the travel and the tilt grow.

In Brown's words

An eccentric generally used on the crank-shaft for communicating the reciprocating rectilinear motion to the valves of steam engines, and sometimes used for pumping.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The rails and the sliding block at the end of the rod, the bearing behind the shaft, the base and the sizes were made up. Brown's rod runs off the edge of the drawing. The smaller offsets were added for comparison.
