No. 373: Roberts's friction test

A loaded wagon stands on top of a big turning wheel, tied to a spring balance with a dial. The dial shows how hard the wheel drags the wagon along. It proves that the drag depends on the load, not on the speed.

How it works

The belt turns the big wheel, and its top runs under the wagon's wheels. The wagon's wheels spin, but the wagon does not go anywhere. The rod holds it back.

Wheels never roll for free. Their axles rub and their rims squash a little. So the big wheel drags the wagon to the left. The rod pulls back on a spring inside the dial, and the pointer shows how hard.

Speed the big wheel up or slow it down, and watch the pointer. It stays where it is. Now switch to a heavy load. The pointer swings round at once, because the drag grows with the weight, not with the speed.

In Brown's words

Robert's contrivance for proving that friction of a wheel carriage does not increase with velocity, but only with load. Loaded wagon is supported on surface of large wheel, and connected with indicator constructed with spiral spring, to show force required to keep carriage stationary when large wheel is put in motion. It was found that difference in velocity produced no variation in the indicator, but difference in weight immediately did so.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing and description. The small driving pulley and its crank, the cord inside the dial, the sizes and how hard the wheel drags the wagon were made up. Brown draws a screw through the post behind the dial; it is left out. The frame's sloping legs are drawn upright.
