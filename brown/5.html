No. 5: Belt with a tightening pulley

Two pulleys are joined by a belt that is a little too loose. A small pulley on a lever can be pressed against it. Pressed in, it pulls the belt tight and the upper pulley turns. Let go, the belt hangs loose and the upper pulley stops.

How it works

The lower pulley pulls the belt round, and the belt pulls the upper pulley. That only works if the belt grips both pulleys. A loose belt slides over them.

The belt is made a little too long. When the small pulley is pressed in, it bends the belt inwards and takes up the extra length. Now the belt is tight and grips. When it is let go, the extra length hangs loose on the other side.

Try let go, then half way, then pressed in. Half way, the belt is still a bit loose and slips, so the upper pulley turns slowly. Watch the bulge on the right side shrink as the speed goes up.

In Brown's words

Resembles 1, with the addition of a movable tightening pulley, B. When this pulley is pressed against the band to take up the slack, the belt transmits motion from one of the larger pulleys to the other; but when it is not, the belt is so slack as not to transmit motion.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing and description. The sizes, the frame and the lever that holds the small pulley are made up. Brown does not show where the lever is pivoted. A real loose belt sags on both sides; here all the slack is shown on the right side.
