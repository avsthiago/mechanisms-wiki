No. 372: White's dynamometer

A dynamometer measures how much power a machine takes to turn. Here the power passes through four bevel gears held in a hoop. The pull on the hoop shows how hard the machine is to drive, and a hanging weight measures it.

How it works

Turn the crank and the shaft turns the left bevel gear. That gear turns the top and bottom gears, and they turn the right gear the opposite way. The right gear drives the drum, and the brake on the drum stands for the machine being tested.

The top and bottom gears ride in the hoop. The harder the machine is to turn, the harder they push the hoop round with the shaft. The strap and weights hold it back. When the hoop stays still, the weight on it measures the push. With equal gears, the push on the hoop is twice the turning force passing through.

Try a light brake and then a heavy one, and watch the weights on the hoop change. Then change the speed. The weight stays the same, but the power goes up, because power is the push times the speed.

In Brown's words

White's dynamometer, for determining the amount of power required to give rotary motion to any piece of mechanism. The two horizontal bevel-gears are arranged in a hoop-shaped frame, which revolves freely on the middle of the horizontal shaft, on which there are two vertical bevel-gears gearing to the horizontal ones, one fast and the other loose on the shaft. Suppose the hoop to be held stationary, motion given to either vertical bevel-gear will be imparted through the horizontal gears to the other vertical one ; but if the hoop be permitted it will revolve with the vertical gear put in motion, and the amount of power required to hold it stationary will correspond with that transmitted from the first gear, and a band attached to its periphery will indicate that power by the weight required to keep it still.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The crank, the drum and brake, the strap and weights, the pins and hub inside the hoop, the base, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. Brown's hoop fits closer round the gears; here it is a little larger so the top and bottom gears clear it as they spin. The power readout takes a made-up size for the hoop and leaves out rubbing in the gears.
