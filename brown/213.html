No. 213: Watch stop, second kind

A stop that lets a watch spring be wound only so many turns, then blocks the key. A pin on the winding wheel moves a stop wheel one tooth per turn. The stop wheel has only a few teeth, and its plain edge is what stops the pin.

How it works

Turn the key and the lower wheel turns with it. Once each turn its pin dips into a gap between two teeth of the ring. It pushes the ring round by one tooth, then lifts out. The rest of the time the ring stays where it was left.

The ring has only a few gaps. When the last one has gone by, the pin meets the plain edge of the ring instead of a gap. It presses straight in towards the ring's centre, which cannot turn it. So the key stops.

Let it run and watch the turns count up until the key stops. Then it turns back the other way, as the spring unwinds, and the plain edge on the other side stops it again. Count the turns between the two stops.

In Brown's words

Another kind of stop for the same purpose.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown only calls it another kind of stop. Here the small circle he draws between the teeth is read as a pin on the lower wheel. The cut in the ring is read as a way to make it grip its stud, so rub holds it still between pushes. The key, the stud, the plate that holds the shafts and the sizes were made up. The click for the saw teeth is left out, as in the drawing. The spring is left out too, so the model just turns back to show unwinding.
