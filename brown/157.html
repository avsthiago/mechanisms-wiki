No. 157: Bell crank and connecting rod

A turning disc pulls a rod, and the rod rocks a lever bent at a corner, called a bell crank. The other arm of the bell crank pushes a second rod up and down. So a turning motion becomes a straight to-and-fro motion, at a spot well away from the disc.

How it works

The pin on the disc goes round in a circle. It pulls and pushes the long rod. The rod rocks the top of the lever, so the lever swings to and fro on its fixed pin.

The lower arm of the lever swings up and down with it. It carries the sliding rod along, and the guide keeps that rod moving in a straight line. One turn of the disc gives one trip up and one trip down.

Move the pin on the disc. Try Near the centre, then Far, and watch the rod reading. A pin further from the centre swings the lever further, so the sliding rod goes further.

In Brown's words

A modification of the movement last described; a connecting-rod being substituted for the slot in the bell-crank.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The bell crank is moved a little to the right of where Brown draws it. Otherwise the connecting rod would jam when the pin comes round close to the lever. The shaft, the posts, the base and the guide were made up. The lower arm ends in a pin that slides along a slot in the head of the rod, because the arm tip swings along a small arc. The pin positions on the disc were added for comparison.
