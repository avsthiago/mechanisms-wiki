No. 310: Three-legged gravity escapement

A weight tries to turn a wheel with three thin legs. Two hanging frames, called pallets, take turns to stop a leg. The wheel lifts each pallet a little, and the pallet then pushes the pendulum with its own weight as it falls back. The wheel never touches the pendulum, so every push is the same.

How it works

Start with the pallet whose stop has a leg resting on it. The wheel cannot turn. A green pin near the middle holds this pallet pushed out a little way.

The pendulum swings out and meets the pin at the bottom of that pallet. It carries the pallet further out, and the stop slides off the leg. The wheel jumps on. As it turns, another green pin pushes the other pallet out, and the next leg lands on that pallet's stop. Then the pendulum swings back and the first pallet falls with it. Its lifting pin has moved away, so it falls further than it was carried. That extra fall is the push that keeps the pendulum swinging.

The push comes from the weight of the pallet, not from the wheel. Try Weak, then Strong. The wheel jumps faster, but the pendulum swings just as far as before. A clock like this keeps good time even when the pull on its wheel changes.

In Brown's words

Three-legged gravity escapement. The lifting of the pallets, A and B, is done by the three pins near the center of the escape-wheel, the pallets vibrating from two centers near the point of suspension of the pendulum. The escape-wheel is locked by means of stops, D and E, on the pallets.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The lifting pins are larger and further from the middle than Brown shows, so you can see them work. The wheel sits on the middle line, where Brown draws it a little to one side. The pendulum hangs in front, where Brown shows it behind. The fly is shorter than drawn and is fixed to the wheel. The spurs on the ends of the legs, the back plate, the rest pins, the bob, the base and all the sizes were made up. The weight that turns the wheel is left out. Nothing rubs in this model. Only the air slows the pendulum and the fly.
