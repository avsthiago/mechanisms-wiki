No. 428: India-rubber rotary engine

This steam engine has no pistons. A rubber lining sits inside the casing, and three rollers on arms press it against the wall. Steam gets in behind the rubber and squeezes the rollers round.

How it works

Look at the yellow band. It is a rubber lining, fixed to the casing only at the two pipes. Three rollers press it flat against the wall. Where a roller presses, no steam can get past.

Steam comes in between the rubber and the wall, shown as dark blue dots. It pushes the rubber inwards behind a roller. The bulging rubber squeezes the back of the roller and drives it forward. The roller runs on round the wall, and its arm turns the shaft.

The top half and the bottom half each have a way in and a way out. When a roller reaches the far pipe, the steam behind it escapes and the rubber springs back to the wall. Watch the red dot. Each roller spins backwards as it rolls along the rubber. Then swap the pipes and see the shaft turn the other way. Choose Closed to see the engine as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

The india-rubber rotary engine in which the cylinder has a flexible lining, E, of india-rubber, and rollers, A, A, are substituted for pistons, said rollers being attached to arms radiating from the main shaft, B. The steam acting between the india-rubber and the surrounding rigid portion of the cylinder presses the india-rubber against the rollers, and causes them to revolve around the cylinder and turn the shaft.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The front cover is left off, and the steam is drawn as small blue balls so you can see it move. How far the rubber bulges inwards was made up. The model does not work out the real stretch of the rubber. The round casing, the straight pipes, the sizes and the choice of pipe were also made up.
