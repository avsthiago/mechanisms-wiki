No. 90: Eccentric with a yoke

A round disc is fixed on a shaft, but set off its centre. This is called an eccentric. It turns inside a long, flat frame called a yoke, and pushes it from side to side. The rods on the yoke slide in straight guides and never tilt.

How it works

The shaft turns, and the disc turns with it. The disc is not centred on the shaft, so its centre swings round in a small circle. Watch the red peg.

The yoke's inner sides are straight and upright. When the disc's centre moves up or down, the disc just slides along them. When it moves left or right, it pushes the yoke with it. So the yoke and its rods go only left and right, and the rods never tilt.

The rods travel twice as far as the disc's centre sits from the shaft. Try Small, then Large, and watch the travel change.

In Brown's words

A modification of the above; an elongated yoke being substituted for the circular strap, to obviate the necessity for any vibrating motion of the rod which works in fixed guides.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The guides for the rods, the bearing behind the shaft, the base and the sizes were made up. The yoke is made tall enough for the largest offset. The other offsets were added for comparison.
