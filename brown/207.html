No. 207: Feed rolls with two worms

One long shaft carries two screws. Each screw turns a gear above it, and a roll sits on each wheel shaft. Both rolls turn the same way at the same speed, so they feed stock steadily.

How it works

Turn the long shaft and both screws, called worms, turn with it. Each thread slides sideways and pushes the teeth of the wheel above it. Both worms wind the same way and sit under their wheels, so both wheels turn clockwise.

A roll is fixed to each wheel and turns with it. The two rolls turn together, at exactly the same speed. Stock held against both is fed straight, without twisting. The worms also stop the rolls being pushed back by the stock.

Some worms have two threads wound side by side. Then each turn moves the wheels on by two teeth, so the rolls go twice as fast. Try One thread, then Two threads, and watch the red marks on the rolls.

In Brown's words

A modification of 195 by means of two worms and worm-wheels.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The frame, the posts that hold the shaft, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The drawing shows teeth only along the bottom of each wheel. Here the teeth go all the way round so that the wheels can keep turning. The matching rolls that would pinch the stock are not drawn, and neither is the stock. The worms with two threads were added for comparison.
