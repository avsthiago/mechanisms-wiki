No. 434: Fourneyron turbine

Water is fed into the middle of a wheel. Fixed curved plates turn it sideways, and it rushes outward into the curved buckets of the wheel. The buckets are pushed round, and the water leaves at the rim.

How it works

Water comes down into the middle and spreads outward. First it runs between the fixed plates. They are curved, so the water comes out sideways, going round as well as out.

Then it runs through the buckets of the wheel. The buckets bend the other way, so the water has to change direction. That change pushes on each bucket, and the wheel turns. The water leaves at the rim with little speed left.

Try a low flow, then a high one. More water means a faster wheel.

In Brown's words

A plan view of the Fourneyron turbine water-wheel. In the center are a number of fixed curved "shutes" or guides, A, which direct the water against the buckets of the outer wheel, B, which revolves, and the water discharges at the circumference.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing, which is a view from above. The floor under the guides, the arms and shaft below, and the number and curve of the plates are made up. The speed of the wheel follows the flow by a simple rule.
