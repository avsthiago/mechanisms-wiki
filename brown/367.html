No. 367: Graduated parallel ruler

Two blades are joined by swinging arms, so the lower blade always stays parallel to the upper one. A curved brass arc on the lower blade crosses a scale on the upper blade and shows how far apart the blades are. This lets you draw lines the right distance apart without measuring each one.

How it works

The two blades are joined by two arms of the same length. As the arms swing, the lower blade slides down and sideways, but it never turns. So its edge always points the same way as the upper blade.

The brass arc is fixed to the lower blade. As the blades come closer, the arc moves along the scale on the upper blade. Each mark on the scale matches a gap, so you can read the gap without a separate ruler.

The ruler closes a little before each line is drawn. Try Close spacing, then Wide, and watch how far the arc moves along the scale each time.

In Brown's words

A parallel ruler with which lines may be drawn at required distances apart without setting out. Lower edge of upper blade has a graduated ivory scale, on which the incidence of the outer edge of the brass arc indicates the width between blades.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown drew curly arms; plain bars are used here. The sheet of paper and the pencil were added to show the lines. The scale marks are placed to match the arc as it is drawn here. In real use you hold the upper blade and push the lower one. All the sizes are made up.
