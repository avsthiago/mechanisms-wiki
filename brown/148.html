No. 148: Gears rocking a crank

A small gear turns a big spoked gear. A pin on the big gear pushes a link, and the link swings a long arm to and fro. The gears only go round and round, but the arm rocks back and forth. A rocking motion like this is useful where a lever must move up and down.

How it works

The small gear turns the big gear. The pin on the big gear goes round in a circle and pushes the link. The link pushes the end of the long arm. The arm is held at one end, so it swings up and down.

The big gear goes round again and again, always the same way. The arm never goes round. It swings up, then down, then up again, once for each turn of the big gear. The arm moves fastest in the middle of each swing and stops for an instant at the top and the bottom.

Try the three places for the pin. Watch how much the arm swings, and read the swing in the box at the top.

In Brown's words

Continuous circular motion of the spur-gears produces alternate circular motion of the crank attached to the larger gear.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The plate behind the gears, the base, the post for the arm, the tooth counts and the thickness of every part were made up. Brown draws an oval groove in the big gear and his link is shorter. The groove was left out, and the link was made a little longer so that the big gear can turn all the way round. The choice of pin place was added to show what it changes.
