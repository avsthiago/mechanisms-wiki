No. 13: Fixed and movable pulley

The weight hangs from a pulley that rides up and down on the rope. Two ropes now hold the weight, so you only pull half as hard. In return, you pull in twice as much rope.

How it works

Follow the rope from the hook on the beam. It goes down under the movable pulley, up over the fixed pulley, and down to the winch. So two ropes hang below the movable pulley, one on each side.

Those two ropes share the weight. With 100 kg, each one holds 50 kg, so the winch only pulls 50 kg. But to lift the weight, both ropes must get shorter by the same amount. So the winch pulls in twice as much rope as the weight rises.

Half the pull, twice the rope. Watch the two readouts: the rope pulled is always twice the weight raised.

In Brown's words

In this the lower pulley is movable. One end of the rope being fixed, the other must move twice as fast as the weight, and a corresponding gain of power is consequently effected.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown leaves the end of the rope hanging; a small winch was added to pull it. The 100 kg weight and the sizes are made up. The pulleys and rope are taken to weigh nothing, and real pulleys rub a little, so a real lift needs a bit more pull than shown here.
