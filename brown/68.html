No. 68: Single tooth and notched wheel

A wheel with one tooth turns a notched wheel one notch at a time. Between pushes, the round edge of the first wheel holds the notched wheel still. So no extra catch is needed to stop it.

How it works

Turn the crank and the left wheel goes round steadily. Once each turn, its tooth slips into a notch of the right wheel. It pushes that wheel round by one notch, then slides out again.

The rest of the time the right wheel cannot move. One of its hollows fits closely round the edge of the left wheel, which keeps turning inside it. Beside the tooth the edge is cut back a little. That lets the corners of the notched wheel swing past while it moves.

Watch the two readouts. Each turn of the crank moves the wheel exactly one notch. Slow the speed right down to see the tooth go in and come out.

In Brown's words

The single tooth, A, of the driving-wheel, B, acts in the notches of the wheel, C, and turns the latter the distance of one notch in every revolution of C. No stop is necessary in this movement, as the driving-wheel, B, serves as a lock by fitting into the hollows cut in the circumference of the wheel, C, between its notches.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The crank, the plate that holds the shafts and the sizes were made up. The cut-backs beside the tooth and the curve of the hollows were worked out so the tooth moves the wheel exactly one notch and nothing rubs. As in a real one, the tooth gives the wheel a small knock as it goes in.
