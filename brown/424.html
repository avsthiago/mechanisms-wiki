No. 424: Root's square piston engine

A steam engine with a square box in place of a round cylinder. One piston slides from side to side in the box, and a second piston slides up and down inside the first. Together they carry a pin round in a circle, and the pin turns a crank.

How it works

Watch the two pistons. The tall frame only goes left and right. The block inside it only goes up and down. Yet the pin in the middle of the block goes round in a circle.

The block rides in the frame, so it goes across with the frame and up and down by itself. Moving across and up and down at the same time, with the right timing, makes a circle. Steam pushes each piston in turn, in the direction it is already going.

Look for the moment one piston stops at the end of its slide. The other is then moving at full speed. So the crank always has a push and never sticks at a dead point. Slow the engine down to see it. Choose Closed to see the engine as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Root's double-reciprocating or square piston engine. The "cylinder," A, of this engine is of oblong square form and contains two pistons, B and C, the former working horizontally, and the latter working vertically within it; the piston, C, is connected with the wrist, a, of the crank on the main shaft, b. The ports for the admission of steam are shown black. The two pistons produce the rotation of the crank without dead points.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The front cover is left off so the pistons show, and the crank is drawn in front of the box so it can be seen. The post that holds the shaft, the base and all the sizes were made up. The steam pipes and valves are left out; the blue dots show the steam. Dark dots are fresh steam pushing, and pale dots are used steam leaving.
