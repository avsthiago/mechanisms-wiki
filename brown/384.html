No. 384: Helicograph

A small wheel runs round a pin stuck in the paper and draws a spiral. The wheel sits on a screw, so each time it rolls it also creeps along the screw. That slowly moves it further from the pin, or nearer to it.

How it works

Push the rod round the pin and the wheel rolls on the paper. The wheel is screwed onto the rod like a nut on a bolt. So while it rolls, it also moves along the rod. The line it leaves is not a circle but a spiral.

The further the wheel is from the pin, the longer its trip round, and the more times it spins. More spins mean more thread, so it moves out further each time. That is why the gaps between the lines grow wider towards the outside. Turn the rod the other way and the wheel walks back in along the same spiral.

The thread sets how far the wheel moves for each spin. Try Fine, then Coarse, and compare how close the lines lie.

In Brown's words

Helicograph, or instrument for describing helices. The small wheel, by revolving about the fixed central point, describes a volute or spiral by moving along the screw-threaded axle either way, and transmits the same to drawing paper on which transfer-paper is laid with colored side downward.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The sizes of the wheel, the rod and the thread are made up. The rod is turned round for you, out to the end of the thread and then back. The drawn line is shown where the wheel touches; the transfer paper itself is left out.
