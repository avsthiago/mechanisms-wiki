No. 464: Hero's fountain

Water poured into the upper vessel runs down a pipe into the lower vessel. It squeezes the air there, and the squeezed air pushes water out of the middle vessel. The water shoots up a tube as a jet.

How it works

Water poured into the upper vessel runs down the wide pipe into the lower vessel. The water rises there and squeezes the air above it. That air cannot escape. It goes up the thin pipe into the middle bowl.

The pressed air pushes down on the water in the middle bowl. The only way out is up the jet tube, so the water shoots out above the upper vessel. It falls back in, and runs down again. The jet can only rise about as high as the water level above the lower vessel.

Try the Short jet tube, then the Tall one. A tall tube needs more pressure, so the jet starts later and is shorter. Choose Closed to see the fountain as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Hiero's fountain. Water being poured into upper vessel descends tube on right into lower ; intermediate vessel being also filled and more water poured into upper, confined air in cavities over water in lower and intermediate vessels and in communication tube on left, being compressed, drives by its elastic force a jet up central tube.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing, which is a cut-away. The sizes, the pouring and the way the air squeezes are simplified and made up, with the pressure worked out from how much room the air has. When the middle bowl runs dry, the model refills it and starts again.
