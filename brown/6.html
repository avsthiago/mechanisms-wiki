No. 6: Rocking segment and crossed belts

A lever rocks a half wheel up and down. A belt tied to the half wheel turns two pulleys below it, first one way and then back. The pulleys turn further than the lever does.

How it works

Push one knob down and the half wheel rocks. One belt end is wound onto its rim and the other end is let off. Both pulleys turn. Rock the lever back and they turn back.

The belt is one band, so every part of it moves the same distance. The half wheel is bigger than the pulleys. So when the lever rocks a little, the pulleys swing further. Both pulleys sit on the same side of the belt loop, so they turn the same way.

The belt never goes slack. Whatever one end lets off, the other end winds in, so the loop keeps the same length. Try 10°, then 40°, and watch the red spokes swing further than the lever.

In Brown's words

By giving a vibratory motion to the lever secured to the semi-circular segment, the belt attached to the said segment imparts a reciprocating rotary motion to the two pulleys below.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown does not say how the belt is fixed. Here it is one band with both ends tied to the half wheel near the lever, and it grips each pulley without slipping. In a real machine it could also be nailed to each pulley rim. The sizes, the back board and the shafts are made up.
