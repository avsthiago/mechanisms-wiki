No. 205: Wheel driven by a two-tooth pinion

A wheel has two rings of pegs, one on its front face and one on its back, set in between each other. A shaft below it carries two cams, one in front of the wheel and one behind. Together they work like a gear with only two teeth.

How it works

The shaft turns steadily. Its front cam swings up, catches a peg on the front of the wheel and pushes it along. Then the wheel rests for a moment. Half a turn later the back cam comes up behind the wheel and pushes a back peg the same way.

The back pegs sit halfway between the front ones. So each cam only has to push the wheel half the gap between two front pegs. The two cams take turns, like the two teeth of a very small gear. The wheel goes round much more slowly than the shaft.

Watch the Pushing readout. It goes front cam, neither, back cam, neither. Each turn of the shaft moves the wheel on by one front peg. Slow the speed right down and follow the red peg.

In Brown's words

Represents a wheel driven by a pinion of two teeth. The pinion consists in reality of two cams, which gear with two distinct series of teeth on opposite sides of the wheel, the teeth of one series alternating in position with those of the other.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The shape of the cams, the size and number of pegs, the shafts, the plate and the base were made up. The wheel's motion is worked out from the outlines, so a cam only moves the wheel as far as it pushes a peg, and nothing passes through anything.
