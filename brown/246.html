No. 246: Pantograph

Four jointed bars let you copy a drawing at a different size. You trace the outline with one point and a pencil draws the same shape, bigger. Draughtsmen use it to enlarge or shrink plans.

How it works

Trace the small star with the tracing point. The pencil draws the same star, larger, in its own place. The dark line is where the point has been and the red line is the pencil's work.

The four bars always make a diamond with opposite sides parallel. That keeps the fixed point, the tracing point and the pencil in one straight line. The pencil stays twice as far from the fixed point as the tracing point, so every line it draws is twice as long.

Slide the fixed point and the pencil along their bars to change this. Try 1.5 times, then 3 times, and compare the size of the red star with the dark one.

In Brown's words

Represents a pantograph for copying, enlarging, and reducing plans, etc. One arm is attached to and turns on the fixed point, C. B is an ivory tracing-point, and A the pencil. Arranged as shown, if we trace the lines of a plan with the point, B, the pencil will reproduce it double the size. By shifting the slide attached to the fixed point, C, and the slide carrying the pencil along their respective arms, the proportion to which the plan is traced will be varied.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The board, the paper, the star and the bar thickness were made up. The bars are drawn just long enough for each setting, and the bars lie in two layers so the joints can turn. The slides move by themselves when you pick a new size.
