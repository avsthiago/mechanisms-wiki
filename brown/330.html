No. 330: Forked connecting rod

The piston rod is made longer, and its top end slides in a fixed guide above the cross-head. The guide keeps the rod straight. The connecting rod splits into a fork at its lower end, so the piston rod can pass between its two legs.

How it works

The crank pin goes round and pulls the forked rod up and down. The rod also swings to and fro, towards you and away from you. Left alone, that swing would push the piston rod sideways.

The piston rod runs through a guide fixed to the frame, above the fork. The guide holds the top of the rod in line with the cylinder, so the rod can only go up and down. The two legs of the fork pass either side of the guide, so nothing touches.

Try a short throw, then a long one. With the long throw the fork swings further, but the piston rod stays upright. Choose Closed to see the cylinder as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

The piston-rod is prolonged and works in a guide, A, which is in line with the center of the cylinder. The lower part of the connecting-rod is forked to permit the upper part of the piston-rod to pass between.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The crank turns across the picture, so the rod swings towards and away from you. Brown also shows a cylinder hanging on the right; it does nothing here and is left out. The cylinder, its cover, the base, the arm that holds the guide and all the sizes were made up. The arm runs behind the fork so the legs can swing past it. The cylinder is see-through so the piston can be seen.
