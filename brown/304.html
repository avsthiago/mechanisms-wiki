No. 304: Pin-wheel escapement

This escape wheel has a ring of small pins on its face instead of teeth. Two pallets on a pair of swinging arms let the pins past one at a time. If a pin is damaged, a new one can be fitted. A broken tooth would spoil a whole toothed wheel.

How it works

The wheel is always trying to turn, so the pins on the right move downward. A pin comes down onto the top face of the upper pallet and rests there. The wheel cannot move.

The pallets swing with the pendulum. The top face of each is part of a circle drawn round the pin at the top. So the pallet can slide along under a resting pin without pushing the wheel back or letting it creep forward. When the end of the face slides out from under the pin, the pin runs down the sloping end and pushes the pallet aside. That push keeps the pendulum swinging. The wheel moves on a little, until another pin lands on the lower pallet. On the swing back the same thing happens there.

The pendulum sets the pace. A long pendulum swings more slowly, so the beats come further apart and the wheel turns more slowly. Try Short, then Long, and count the beats.

In Brown's words

Pin-wheel escapement, somewhat resembling the stud escapement shown by 292. The pins, A, B, of the escape-wheel are of two different forms, but the form of those on the right side is the best. One advantage of this kind of escapement is that if one of the pins is damaged it can easily be replaced, whereas if a tooth is damaged the whole wheel is ruined.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing and description. Brown says only that the pins have two forms and that the form on the right is the better one. Here the round side of every pin leads. The pins on the left of his drawing are half-round, and the pins on the right are thin slices, which leave the pallets more room. The exact shape of the pallets, the pendulum, the back plate and the sizes were made up. The pallets are small blocks behind the arms, so the arms themselves pass in front of the pins. The pendulum hangs in front so that it can be seen. The weight that turns the wheel is left out and its pull is taken to be steady. The pins slide on the pallets without rubbing, and only the pendulum and the wheel are slowed a little.
