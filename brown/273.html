No. 273: Toggle rods

Four rods slide in guides, and four links join their ends into a diamond. When the two side rods are pushed together, the diamond gets taller and pushes the top and bottom rods apart. Pulling the side rods apart does the opposite.

How it works

The four links are all the same length, so the corners always make a diamond. The side corners can only move left and right. The top and bottom corners can only move up and down.

If the side corners move together, the diamond has to get taller. The top and bottom corners then move apart. The reverse also holds. The distance moved is not always equal. A tall diamond moves its top and bottom corners less than its sides.

Try the three diamond shapes. In the square shape both pairs of rods move the same distance. In the others, watch which pair moves more.

In Brown's words

Rectilinear into rectilinear motion. When the rods, A and B, are brought together, the rods, C and D, are thrust further apart, and vice versa.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The guides, the cross-shaped stand behind them, the length of the rods and how far they push were made up. The links are drawn in two layers so that they can cross at each pin.
