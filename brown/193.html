No. 193: Mangle wheel with two speeds

A small gear turns steadily one way, yet the big wheel turns almost a whole turn one way and then almost a whole turn back. It goes back faster than it goes forward. Machines used this to move a table or a roller to and fro.

How it works

Turn the crank and the small gear, called a pinion, turns with it. In the picture it runs along the inside of the big ring of teeth. It pushes the wheel round the same way it turns itself.

At the end of the gap the pinion rolls round the bend and onto the small ring. Now it pushes on the outside of the teeth, so the wheel turns back. The small ring is much smaller, so each turn of the pinion moves the wheel further. The wheel goes back faster than it went forward. The pinion's shaft rides up and down in the guide, and its joints let it tilt to follow.

Watch the wheel speed readout. A minus sign means the wheel turns the other way. Compare how long each trip takes.

In Brown's words

Another kind of mangle-wheel with its pinion. With this as well as with that in the preceding figure, although the pinion continues to revolve in one direction, the mangle-wheel will make almost an entire revolution in one direction and the same in an opposite direction; but the revolution of the wheel in one direction will be slower than that in the other, owing to the greater radius of the outer circle of teeth.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The crank, the guide, the jointed shaft, the post, the base, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. Brown says the shaft has a universal joint; here it has two, so the pinion stays square to the wheel and turns at the same speed as the crank.
