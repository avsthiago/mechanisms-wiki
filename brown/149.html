No. 149: Cams and levers

Two oval cams turn together on one shaft. Each one pushes a long lever up and down, and the lever moves a rod. The shaft turns round and round, while the rods slide up and down.

How it works

Look at the wheel on each lever. It sits on the edge of an oval cam. The shaft is off to one side of each oval, so as the cam turns, its edge moves up and down under the wheel.

When the edge rises, it lifts the wheel and the lever swings up. When the edge falls, the lever sinks back by its own weight. The rod hangs from the lever, so it rises and falls with it. The two cams point in different directions, so the two rods do not move together.

Turning in one direction at a steady speed gives rods that go up and down, again and again. Try Nearly round, Medium and Long, and watch how far each rod moves.

In Brown's words

Uniform circular converted, by the cams acting upon the levers, into alternating rectilinear motions of the attached rods.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The sizes, the exact oval shapes, the shaft, the pin, the back plate, the guides and the base were made up. The two cams sit side by side on the shaft so that each lever has its own. The levers are held down on the cams only by their weight. Brown draws the rods as if fixed to the levers, but a rod that stays upright cannot be fixed to a lever that swings in an arc, so each rod's pin slides in a slot. The lever heights are worked out exactly from the shapes of the cams. With the long cams, the edge sometimes rises beside the wheel, and then the lever rests on the cam for a moment. The choice of cam shape was added for comparison.
