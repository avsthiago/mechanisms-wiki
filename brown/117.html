No. 117: Cam between two rollers

A lumpy cam turns on a shaft between two small wheels, called rollers. The rollers are held in a frame, and the frame moves up and down as the cam turns. Steam engines used this to open and close their valves.

How it works

The shaft turns, and the cam turns with it. The cam is lumpy, so its sides are at different distances from the shaft. Watch the red peg go round.

One roller sits above the cam and one below. The frame keeps them the same distance apart, and the cam is shaped so that it always touches both. A bulge on the top pushes the frame up. A bulge on the bottom pushes it down. The rods go with the frame.

Watch the readout while the cam turns. The travel rises and falls once for each turn. Press pause and look at the two rollers. Both are touching the cam.

In Brown's words

A cam acting between two friction-rollers in a yoke. Has been used to give the movement to the valve of a steam engine.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The guides for the rods, the bearing behind the shaft, the base and all the sizes were made up. The shape of the cam was worked out so that it touches both rollers at every angle. The two rollers turn at the speed the cam surface carries them.
