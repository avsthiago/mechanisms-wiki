No. 122: Two cranked gears and a bar

Two gears of different sizes turn together. A short pin on each gear pushes a rod, and both rods push one upright link. A bar joined to the middle of that link slides to and fro. Its strokes are not all the same length, because the two pins pull together at some moments and against each other at others.

How it works

Turn the gears and the two pins go round in circles. Each pin pushes its rod, and the two rods swing the upright link. The bar joined to the link's middle cannot swing, so it slides to and fro.

The gears are different sizes, so they do not turn together once for once. The pin on the driven gear gets a little ahead of the other pin each turn. The pins therefore meet in a new way each time, and the bar's strokes change in length. It takes several turns before the pattern repeats.

Try the three places for the pin on the driven gear. Watch how the bar's strokes grow longer or shorter.

In Brown's words

The rotation of the two spur-gears, with crank-wrists attached, produces a variable alternating traverse of the horizontal bar.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The plate behind the gears, the base, the guides for the bar, the tooth counts, the lengths of the rods and the link, and the thickness of every piece were made up. The pins were set closer to the centres than drawn, so that the rods never jam during a full turn. The choice of pin place was added to show what it changes.
