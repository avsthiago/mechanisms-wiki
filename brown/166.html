No. 166: Crank and slotted rod

A disc turns a pin round in a circle. The pin sits in a long slot in the end of a rod. The rod pulls a mould to and fro, but at each end of the trip the pin slides along the slot and the mould waits. A brick press uses this to give the clay time to drop in.

How it works

The disc turns and the red pin goes round in a circle. The rod has a long slot in its end, and the pin sits in it. The far end of the rod is pinned to a mould that slides on a table.

Near the middle of the trip the pin presses on an end of the slot and drags the rod and mould along. Near each end of the trip the pin is moving across more than along, so it just slides down the slot and the mould stands still. In a brick press the mould waits at one end while clay drops in, and at the other while the brick is taken out.

Try Short, then Long. A short slot gives a long trip and short rests. A long slot gives a short trip and long rests. Watch the stroke reading change between Moving and Resting.

In Brown's words

The rotation of the disk carrying the crank pin gives a to-and-fro motion to the connecting-rod, and the slot allows the rod to remain at rest at the termination of each stroke; it has been used in a brick-press, in which the connecting-rod draws a mold backward and forward, and permits it to rest at the termination of each stroke, that the clay may be deposited in it and the brick extracted.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The table, the mould, the plate behind the disc and all the sizes were made up. The short and long slots were added for comparison.
