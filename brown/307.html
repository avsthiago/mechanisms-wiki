No. 307: Three-legged escapement with stops

A weight tries to spin a wheel with three long teeth. Two small stops on the pendulum take turns to catch a long tooth and hold the wheel still. Each time a stop lets go, a short tooth near the hub gives the pendulum a small push.

How it works

The wheel tries to turn clockwise. The tip of a long tooth is caught by a stop on the pendulum, so the wheel stands still. The face of the stop is part of a circle round the pin of the pendulum. So the stop can slide along the tip without moving the wheel.

As the pendulum swings through the middle, the stop slides off the tip and the wheel is free. A short tooth near the hub runs into a pallet and pushes it the way the pendulum is already going. That push keeps the pendulum swinging. Then the short tooth slips past, and the next long tooth lands on the other stop. On the swing back the other pallet gets the push.

The pendulum sets the pace. A long pendulum swings more slowly, so the beats come further apart. Try Short, then Long, and count the beats.

In Brown's words

A modification of the above with long stopping teeth, D and E. A and B are the pallets.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The top of the pendulum, the bob, the back plate and the sizes were made up. The pendulum here is much shorter than a real one would be. Because of that the two stops are tilted, where the drawing shows them level, and the long teeth are bent forward at the end so that only their tips touch the stops. In the drawing the teeth are straight. The weight that turns the wheel is left out and its pull is taken to be steady. The teeth slide on the stops and pallets without rubbing, and only the pendulum and the wheel are slowed a little.
