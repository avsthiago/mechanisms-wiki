No. 176: Uncoupling engines

A pin on one crank arm pushes round a second crank arm through a slot in a ring. Turn the ring so the slot points along the arm and the two arms turn together. Turn it across the arm and the pin slides through the slot, so the second arm stands still.

How it works

The driving arm turns all the time. Its pin sits in the slot of the ring, at the far end of the driven arm. With the slot lying along the arm, the pin leans on the side of the slot and pushes the arm round. Both arms turn together.

Now turn the ring a quarter turn. The slot lies across the arm, in the same direction that the pin is travelling. The pin runs along the slot and out of the other end. The driven arm does not move, so the engine is uncoupled.

Try Uncoupled and watch the driven arm slow and stop. Then try Coupled. The ring can only turn while the pin is inside it, so it waits for the pin to come round.

In Brown's words

Contrivance for uncoupling engines. The wrist which is fixed on one arm of the crank (not shown) will communicate motion to the arm of the crank which is represented, when the ring on the latter has its slot in the position shown in 176. But when the ring is turned to bring the slot in the position shown in 177, the wrist passes through the slot without turning the crank to which said ring is attached.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The driving arm is not shown by Brown; a plain one was added. The shaft, the bearing, the stand, the disc under the ring and all the sizes were made up. Brown does not say how the ring is held, so it simply sits on the disc. The ring is allowed to turn only while the pin is inside it.
