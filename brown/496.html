No. 496: Drawing and twisting rollers

A loose rope of cotton fibres is squeezed between pairs of rollers. The front rollers turn faster than the back ones, so they pull the rope thinner. Then a spinning arm twists it into a strong thread and winds it onto a reel.

How it works

The thick rope goes in between the back rollers. The front rollers are a little further on and turn faster. They pull the fibres along faster than the back rollers let them in. The fibres slide past each other and the rope gets thinner. This is called drawing.

The thin rope slips down into the flyer, a bent arm that spins round the reel. Every time the arm goes round, it twists the rope once. Twisting makes the fibres grip each other, so the thread is strong. The reel turns a little slower than the flyer and winds the thread on.

Try Small, Medium and Big. With a bigger pull-out the front rollers are much faster, the rope after them is much thinner, and the twists are spread further apart.

In Brown's words

Drawing and twisting in spinning cotton, wool, etc. The front drawing-rolls, B, rotate faster than the back ones, A, and so produce a draught, and draw out the fibers of the sliver or roving passing between them. Roving passes from the front drawing-rolls to throstle, which, by its rotation around the bobbin, twists and winds the yarn on the bobbin.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The frame, the sizes of the rollers and the reel, the speed of the flyer and the exact path of the rope inside the flyer were made up. The spinning arm is shown going round much more slowly than a real one, so you can follow it. The twist is not drawn on the rope. The readout gives the length of thread that gets one twist.
