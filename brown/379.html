No. 379: Cramp drill

A C-shaped frame holds a hand drill above a plate. A crank spins the drill, and a screw below pushes the plate up into it. It drills a hole where there is no room for a drilling machine.

How it works

Turn the crank and the drill spins. The screw at the bottom turns slowly and lifts the plate. When the plate touches the spinning bit, the bit starts to cut a hole.

The screw does the pushing. One turn of the screw moves the plate a small, fixed distance, so the bit cuts in by the same small amount every time. This is why the drill can press very hard without slipping. When the hole is through, the screw winds back and a fresh plate is clamped on.

Try Light, Medium and Heavy. A heavy bite means the screw turns faster for each turn of the crank, so the hole gets deeper sooner.

In Brown's words

Portable cramp drills. In 379 the feed-screw is opposite the drill, and in 380 the drill spindle passes through the center of the feed-screw.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The frame, plate, thread and drill bit are drawn simply, and all the sizes were made up. In real use a person turns both the crank and the screw by hand. Here the screw turns by itself at the right speed to match the crank. The plate stays still on the screw's end and is replaced when the hole is through.
