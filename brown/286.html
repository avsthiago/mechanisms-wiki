No. 286: Toe and lifter

A shaft rocks to and fro. A curved arm on it, called the toe, pushes up on a flat lifter fixed to an upright rod. The rod lifts a valve in a steam engine, then lets it drop as the toe swings back.

How it works

The shaft turns a little way, and the curved toe turns with it. The tip of the toe rises and pushes on the flat underside of the lifter. The lifter, the rod and the valve all go up together.

The tip of the toe moves along a curve, but the lifter can only go straight up. So the tip also slides sideways along the lifter as it lifts, a short way. The guide keeps the rod upright. When the shaft swings back, the weight of the valve brings the lifter down onto the toe again.

Try a small swing of the rocking shaft, then a big one. Watch the valve lift off its seat. Compare how high it goes and how far the toe slides.

In Brown's words

Toe and lifter for working puppet valves in steam engines. The curved toe on the rock-shaft operates on the lifter attached to the lifting-rod to raise the valve.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows only the toe, the lifter and the rod. The valve and its seat, the guide, the bearing, the base and all the sizes were made up. The weight of the valve is assumed to keep the lifter resting on the toe.
