No. 376: Tread-wheel horse-power

A horse walks inside a big wheel, like a hamster in its wheel, but it never gets anywhere. Its weight turns the wheel, and the wheel turns a shaft that can drive a machine.

How it works

The horse walks up the sloping floor on the left side of the wheel. Each step pushes the floor backwards and down, so the wheel turns and the horse stays in about the same place. Watch the red stripe on the floor go round.

The turning force comes from the horse's weight. At the very bottom of the wheel the weight pushes straight down and turns nothing. The higher up the side the horse stands, the more the weight pulls sideways, and the harder the wheel turns. The wheel turns as fast as the horse walks.

Move the horse low, then part way up, then high, and watch the turning force change. Then raise the wheel speed and see the horse's legs keep up.

In Brown's words

Tread-wheel horse-power turned by the weight of an animal attempting to walk up one side of its interior ; has been used for driving the paddle-wheels of ferry-boats and other purposes by horses. The turnspit dog used also to be employed in such a wheel in ancient times for turning meat while roasting on a spit.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows only the wheel, its bars and the horse. The floor boards, the rim bolts, the shaft, its two bearings, the gear and the base were made up. The horse is a simple blocky shape, and its size, weight and walking pace are made up too. The horse always stays in the place you choose and walks steadily.
