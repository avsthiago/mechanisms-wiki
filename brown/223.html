No. 223: Sectors on different planes

The upper shaft carries two toothed sectors, slices of gears of different sizes, one in front of the other. Each drives a matching sector on the lower shaft. The upper shaft turns steadily, but the lower one turns fast for part of each turn and slowly for the rest.

How it works

The upper shaft turns at a steady speed. In each turn its front slice and its back slice take turns at driving. Only one pair of slices is in mesh at a time, because the two pairs lie in different planes.

For a quarter turn the big front slice drives the small front slice. A big gear turning a small one makes it go fast, so the lower shaft does half a turn in that quarter. For the other three quarters the small back slice drives the big back slice. That is slow work, and the lower shaft does only the other half turn.

Watch the Driven readout jump between fast and slow. The red mark hurries through its half turn, then crawls through the other half. The upper shaft never changes speed at all.

In Brown's words

An arrangement for obtaining variable circular motion. The sectors are arranged on different planes, and the relative velocity changes according to the respective diameters of the sectors.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown draws the slices in quarters and parts of quarters; here each shaft has just two slices, one in each plane, sized so they share out one whole turn. The plate, the base, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. Where one pair hands over to the other, real teeth would need careful shaping to pass the drive on smoothly.
