No. 14: Blocks and tackle

A rope runs back and forth between two sets of pulleys. It lets you lift a heavy weight with a small pull. In return, you have to pull in a lot more rope.

How it works

Count the ropes between the two blocks. Each pulley in the lower block has one rope going up on each side. So 4 pulleys give 8 ropes, and all 8 hold the weight up together.

It is really one long rope, so every part of it pulls equally hard. With 8 parts sharing 100 kg, each part carries 12.5 kg. The winch only has to hold one part. That is Brown's rule: divide the weight by twice the number of lower pulleys.

There is a catch. To lift the weight 1 cm, all 8 parts must get 1 cm shorter, so 8 cm of rope has to be pulled in. Less force, more rope. Try 1 pulley, then 4, and compare.

In Brown's words

Blocks and tackle. The power obtained by this contrivance is calculated as follows: Divide the weight by double the number of pulleys in the lower block; the quotient is the power required to balance the weight.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows the end of the rope hanging loose; the winch was added so it has somewhere to go. Real pulleys rub and real rope is heavy, so a real tackle needs a bit more pull than shown here.
