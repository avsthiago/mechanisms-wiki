No. 408: Centrolinead

A long blade has two flat legs fixed to its end at a set angle. The legs rest against two pins on the paper. However the tool is moved, the blade keeps pointing at one far-off spot that is off the page. A pencil along the blade draws lines that all meet at that spot.

How it works

The legs are fixed to the disc, and their inner edges press on the two pins. This leaves the tool only one way to move. It slides along a curved path while it turns.

As it moves, the blade turns just enough to keep pointing at the same spot. That spot is far off to the left, beyond the edge of the paper. Each line the pencil draws is part of a line that runs through it, so the lines fan out from there.

The pins set where that spot is. Try the pins near the joint, then far from it, and see how the lines fan out more or less.

In Brown's words

Centrolinead for drawing lines toward an inaccessible or inconveniently distant point ; chiefly used in perspective. Upper or drawing edge of blade and back of movable legs should intersect center of joint. Geometrical diagram indicates mode of setting instrument, legs forming it may form unequal angles with blade. At either end of dotted line crossing central, a pin is inserted vertically for instrument to work against. Supposing it to be inconvenient to produce the convergent lines until they intersect, even temporarily, for the purpose of setting the instrument as shown, a corresponding convergence may be found between them by drawing a line parallel to and inward from each.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown draws the blade, disc, legs and clamp knobs; the curved slots and knobs are left out and the legs are fixed. The paper, the two pins and the pencil were added to show how it works. In the drawing the pins are a diagram only. The sizes are made up.
