No. 211: Intermittent pinion with a locking curve

A big gear turns all the time, but the small gear beside it, called a pinion, turns only now and then. Each time the big gear goes round once, the small gear makes exactly one turn and then stops. While it waits, it is locked so it cannot creep.

How it works

The big gear turns steadily. For most of its turn the small gear stands still. Then the red pin on the big gear hits the curved arm on the small gear. The small gear starts to move, the teeth meet, and they carry it round once. Then it stops again.

Behind the small gear is a green plate. Its edge is hollowed into the same curve as the big gear's plain rim. While the plain rim passes, it slides round inside that hollow. The plate cannot turn without cutting into the rim, so the small gear is locked. Where the big gear has teeth, the plain disc behind them is cut back, so the green plate is free to swing round.

Watch the Turns readout. Each turn of the big gear adds exactly one. Slow the speed right down and watch the pin start the small gear before the first tooth arrives.

In Brown's words

A continuous rotary motion of the large wheel gives an intermittent rotary motion to the pinion-shaft. The part of the pinion shown next the wheel is cut of the same curve as the plain portion of the circumference of the wheel, and therefore serves as a lock while the wheel makes a part of a revolution, and until the pin upon the wheel strikes the guide-piece upon the pinion, when the pinion-shaft commences another revolution.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The plate and base that hold the shafts, the sizes, the number of teeth and the shape of the guide piece were made up. Brown's locking piece and guide are hard to make out, so here the locking plate sits behind the small gear and the gear's plain rim is a disc behind its teeth. The motion was worked out so the pin and the teeth push the small gear exactly one turn and nothing rubs. As in a real one, the small gear starts and stops with a small knock.
