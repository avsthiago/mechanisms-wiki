No. 163: Water-wheel governor with a belt

Two heavy balls spin on an upright shaft. When the shaft speeds up, the balls swing out and slide a belt onto a lower pulley. When it slows, the belt moves to a higher pulley. A drum on the gate shaft is turned one way, left still, or turned the other way, to shut or open the water gate.

How it works

The balls hang from arms on the spindle. As the spindle turns, the balls are thrown outward. The faster it turns, the further out they swing, and the higher the sleeve slides up the spindle.

A collar under the sleeve lifts one end of a bent lever. The other end drops and pushes the belt down onto the lower pulley. The belt then turns the gate shaft one way. The middle pulley is loose, so the belt on it drives nothing. On the top pulley the belt is crossed, so the gate shaft turns the other way.

A small change in speed is enough, so slide it slowly from low to high. Watch the balls, then the belt, then the gate shaft. A minus sign means the gate shaft turns the other way. Pause to see the balls drop.

In Brown's words

Another arrangement for a water-wheel governor. In this the governor controls the shuttle or gate by means of the cranked lever, which acts on the strap or belt in the following manner:—The belt runs on one of three pulleys, the middle one of which is loose on the governor spindle and the upper and lower ones fast. When the governor is running at the proper speed, the belt is on the loose pulley, as shown; but when the speed increases the belt is thrown on the lower pulley, and thereby caused to act upon suitable gearing for raising the gate or shuttle and decreasing the supply of water. A reduction of the speed of the governor brings the belt on the upper pulley, which acts upon gearing for producing an opposite effect on the shuttle or gate.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing and description. The sizes, the table and frame, the bearings, the stops that limit the arms, the pulley widths and the whole gate shaft were made up. Brown does not draw the far end of the belt, so a drum was added. The belt is drawn crossed when it sits on the top pulley, so that the gate shaft reverses. The weights of the balls and sleeve were chosen so the belt rests on the loose pulley at a middle speed. The gate and its gearing are left out.
