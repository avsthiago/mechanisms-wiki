No. 83: Toothed arcs on a rocking shaft

A rod slides back and forth and rocks a shaft. Two toothed arcs on the shaft take turns to push a flat toothed wheel. So the wheel keeps turning one way, with only short pauses.

How it works

The rod slides and rocks the shaft to and fro. Both arcs swing with it, so their lowest teeth move left and right over the wheel. The front arc sits over the front edge of the wheel and the back arc over the back edge.

When the arcs swing right, the front arc's teeth catch the wheel's teeth and push the front edge right. When they swing left, the back arc catches and pushes the back edge left. Both pushes turn the wheel the same way. The arc that is not pushing rides up over the slanted teeth, and its springs press it back down.

Watch which arc turns red, and watch the other one lift. Try Tiny. The arcs swing less than one tooth, so they never catch a new tooth and the wheel stops. Then try Short and Long, and compare how far the wheel turns.

In Brown's words

A nearly continuous rotary motion is given to the wheel, D, by two ratchet-toothed arcs, C, one operating on each side of the ratchet-wheel, D. These arcs (only one of which is shown) are fast on the same rock-shaft, B, and have their teeth set opposite ways. The rock-shaft is worked by giving a reciprocating rectilinear motion to the rod, A. The arcs should have springs applied to them, so that each may be capable of rising to allow its teeth to slide over those of the wheel in moving one way.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's description and drawing. Brown draws only the front arc; the back one is added as he describes. The frame over the wheel, the guide for the rod, the holders that let each arc rise, the springs, the number of teeth and all the sizes were made up. An arc swings in a circle while the wheel's teeth lie flat, so the two sets of teeth cannot match perfectly. The arc's teeth were made a little narrower, a little shorter and spaced a little wider, so each arc drops right down into the teeth before it pushes. A real wheel would coast a little between pushes. Here it stops at once.
