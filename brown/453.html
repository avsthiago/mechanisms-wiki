No. 453: Double lantern-bellows pump

Two round bellows stand side by side on a hollow base, worked by one lever. As one bellows is pulled open it sucks water up the pipe below. At the same time the other is squeezed and pushes its water out of the pipe at the top.

How it works

Watch the left bellows open. The flap under it lifts and water rushes up from the suction pipe to fill the space. Meanwhile the right bellows is squeezed. Its water cannot go back down, because its bottom flap is shut, so it pushes open a flap into the middle pipe.

When the lever rocks back, the two bellows swap jobs. One is always filling while the other is emptying. So water leaves the top pipe on every stroke of the lever, not just every other one.

Try pausing just as the lever is level. Two flaps are open and two are shut. Look at which way each one opens and follow the blue dots through it. Choose Closed to see the pump as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Double lantern-bellows pump. As one bellows is distended by lever, air is rarefied within it, and water passes up suction-pipe to fill space ; at same time other bellows is compressed, and expels its contents through discharge-pipe ; valves working the same as in the ordinary force pump.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The front half of the base, pipes and bellows is cut away so the inside shows. The base is drawn as a flat box so all the passages show at once. The post is cut off at the top, as in the drawing. The bend at the top of the delivery pipe, the shape of the flaps and all the sizes were made up. The litres count assumes a made-up size for the bellows.
