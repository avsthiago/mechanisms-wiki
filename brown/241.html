No. 241: One-tooth wheel driving

A small gear with one tooth turns steadily. Once each turn, its tooth catches the big gear and moves it on by one tooth. A bent spring then holds the big gear still until the next push.

How it works

Turn the crank and the small gear goes round steadily. Once each turn, its one tooth swings into a gap between two teeth of the big gear. It pushes that wheel round by one tooth, then leaves the gap.

For the rest of the turn the big gear has no push. The round edge of the small gear fits against the big gear and keeps it from turning. The spring arm helps. Its tip slides up a sloping tooth while the wheel moves, then drops into the next gap. A tooth then lies in front of the spring, so the wheel cannot go backwards.

Watch the two readouts. The big gear gains one tooth for every turn of the small gear. Slow the speed right down to see the tooth go into the gap and the spring tip drop.

In Brown's words

Intermittent circular motion is imparted to the wheel, A, by the continuous circular motion of the smaller wheel with one tooth.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The crank, the plate and base, the shafts, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The drawing shows a curved tooth on the small gear. Here it is a straight, thin tooth, and the gaps of the big gear were cut to fit it. The motion of the big gear was worked out so the tooth moves it exactly one tooth and nothing passes through anything. The spring arm is drawn as a stiff curved arm that swings on its pin. A real one would bend.
