No. 463: Self-acting weir

A dam across a stream made of two hinged boards that open by themselves in a flood. When the water rises too high, they tip over and let it rush out along the bottom, washing away the mud. When it falls again, they close.

How it works

In an ordinary stream the two boards stand upright and hold the water back. The water spills out through the notch in the top of the upper board. Mud slowly settles on the bottom in front of the weir.

Each board turns on a pin a little below its middle. Water presses harder the deeper it is, so most of the push is low down, and the upper board stays shut. When the water rises over its top, there is more push above the pin than below. The upper board tips over and shoves the lower one the other way. A gap opens along the bottom, and the water rushes out and scours away the mud. When the water falls, the push low down wins and both close.

Move the water to Flood and watch the level climb until the boards tip. Then go back to Ordinary and watch them close.

In Brown's words

Self-acting weir and scouring sluice. Two leaves turn on pivots below centers ; upper leaf much larger than lower, and turns in direction of stream, while lower turns against it. Top edge of lower leaf overlaps bottom edge of upper one and is forced against it by pressure of water. In ordinary states of stream, counteracting pressures keep weir vertical and closed, as in the left-hand figure, and water flows through notch in upper leaf; but on water rising above ordinary level, pressure above from greater surface and leverage overcomes resistance below, upper leaf turns over, pushing back lower, reducing obstructions and opening at bed a passage to deposit.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's description and his two drawings, which show the weir shut and open. The pins, the wall that holds them, the stop pins, the depth of the water below the weir and the mud are made up. The boards are taken as floating just enough that their own weight does not count. Each turn is worked out from the pressure of the water on both faces of both boards. How fast the pond fills is chosen so the change can be watched.
