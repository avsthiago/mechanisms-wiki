No. 329: Gear guide for a piston rod

A small gear rolls round inside a fixed ring of teeth twice its size. One point on the edge of the small gear goes straight up and down, with no sideways wobble at all. The piston rod is pinned to that point, so it needs no other guide.

How it works

The plate on the shaft carries the small gear round in a circle. Its teeth roll on the teeth of the fixed ring. As it is carried round one way, the rolling makes it spin the other way, at the same speed.

The ring is exactly twice as wide as the small gear. Because of that, a point on the edge of the small gear does not go round in a loop. It slides up and down along a straight line across the middle of the ring. The piston rod is pinned to that point, so it moves straight up and down. Nothing pushes it sideways, so it needs no slide.

Try the red dot on the edge, then halfway in, then at the centre. Only a point on the edge draws a straight line. The others draw an oval or a circle. Choose Closed to see the cylinder as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

A piston-rod guide. The piston-rod, A, is connected with a wrist attached to a cog-wheel, B, which turns on a crank-pin, carried by a plate, C, which is fast on the shaft. The wheel, B, revolves around a stationary internally toothed gear, D, of double the diameter of B, and so motion is given to the crank-pin, and the piston-rod is kept upright.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The cylinder, its cover, the base, the bearing behind the flywheel, the arm that carries the pin on the small gear, the tooth counts and all the sizes were made up. The cylinder is drawn see-through so the piston shows. The red dot and its line were added to show the path.
