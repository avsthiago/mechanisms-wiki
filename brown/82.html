No. 82: Treadles and ratchet

Two foot boards, called treadles, are pressed down one after the other. Each one pulls an arm that drags a toothed wheel round. One treadle works while the other rises, so the wheel keeps turning one way almost without a stop.

How it works

Press the front treadle down. Its rod pulls the front arm down, and the arm's pawl drags a tooth, so the wheel turns. At the same time the belt over the pulley lifts the back treadle. That pushes the back arm up, and its pawl slides back over the slanted teeth.

Now press the back treadle. The two swap jobs. The back pawl drags the wheel and the front pawl slides back for a fresh grip. Both strokes turn the wheel the same way, with only a short pause when the treadles change over.

Watch which pawl turns red. Try Tiny. The arms swing less than one tooth, so the pawls never reach a new gap and the wheel soon stops. Then try Short and Long, and count the teeth that pass on each stroke.

In Brown's words

On motion being given to the two treadles, D, a nearly continuous motion is imparted, through the vibrating arms, B, and their attached pawls, to the ratchet-wheel, A. A chain or strap attached to each treadle passes over the pulley, C, and as one treadle is depressed the other is raised.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The plate behind the wheel, the hinge block, the stand round the pulley, the shape of the pawls, the number of teeth and all the sizes were made up. A real wheel would coast a little between pulls. Here it stops at once.
