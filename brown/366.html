No. 366: Drilling machine

A crank turns a big bevel gear, which turns a small gear, called a pinion, on an upright drill shaft, so the drill spins fast. The drill shaft can slide through the small gear, and a foot treadle pushes it down into the work while it spins.

How it works

Turn the crank. The driving gear turns with it, and its teeth push the teeth of the small gear. The small gear has half as many teeth, so it turns twice as fast. It sits on the upright drill shaft, so the drill spins twice as fast as the crank.

The drill shaft goes through the small gear. A groove along the shaft keeps the gear and the shaft turning together, but the shaft is free to slide. Press the treadle and the levers push the shaft down, while the gears keep turning it.

Try pressing the treadle half way, then all the way down, and watch the drill go down while it spins.

In Brown's words

Drilling machine. By the large bevel-gear rotary motion is given to vertical drill-shaft, which slides through small bevel-gear but is made to turn with it by a feather and groove, and is depressed by treadle connected with upper lever.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The frame, the floor and the stand for the treadle were added so it stands up. The number of teeth, the sizes and how far the drill can go down were made up. The groove and key inside the small gear are hidden, so only a stripe on the shaft shows the groove.
