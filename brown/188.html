No. 188: Unhooking the eccentric rod, third form

A rod pushed to and fro by the engine ends in an open hook, called a gab, that rests on a pin. The pin is on an arm that rocks a shaft. A big curved handle lifts the hook off the pin, so the rod no longer moves the shaft.

How it works

The rod slides to and fro. Its hooked end sits round the red pin, so the arm swings with it and rocks the shaft above.

Lift the curved handle and the hooked end swings up on its hinge. The hook leaves the pin. The rod keeps sliding, but the arm and the shaft stay still.

Press Lifted off and watch the handle swing. Then press Hooked on. The hook waits until it is over the pin, then drops onto it.

In Brown's words

Modifications of 186.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The stand, the bearing, the hinge in the rod and the sizes were made up. In the drawing the arm and the shaft are hidden, so they are shown here as in the other pictures. The notch marked in the drawing is left out. The engine's eccentric is off the picture, so the rod simply slides to and fro.
