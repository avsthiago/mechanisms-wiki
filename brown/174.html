No. 174: Bench clamp

Two curved jaws turn on screws fixed to a bench. When a board is pushed in between them, the jaws turn and close on its sides. The harder the board is pushed, the tighter it is held.

How it works

Push the board in from the right. Its end meets the round lobe on each jaw and pushes the lobes apart. Each jaw turns on its screw, so its left end swings in towards the board.

The jaws turn only while the end of the board rides over the lobes. After that the board slides under them. Soon the board reaches the curved left ends of the jaws and cannot go farther. It is now held, and pushing harder holds it tighter. Pull the board out and the jaws swing back.

Try Thin, then Thick. Watch the jaw angle and how far the board goes in before it is held.

In Brown's words

Carpenters' bench-clamp. By pushing the clamp between the jaws they are made to turn on the screws and clamp the sides

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows the two jaws, the two screws and the board. The exact shape of the jaws, the back plate, the base, the stops that hold the jaws open and the board thickness choice were made up. The jaws sit one in front of the other where their tips cross, so they do not touch. A real clamp would need a light spring to open the jaws again, and rubbing is left out.
