No. 505: Planet pinion and annular gear

A small gear on an arm runs between a centre gear and a ring with teeth on its inside. Hold either one still and turn the arm, and the other is driven round faster than the arm.

How it works

The ring is held still and the arm turns. The small gear rolls round the inside of the ring. As it rolls it spins on its pin, and its teeth push the centre gear round.

The centre gear gets one turn from the arm carrying the small gear round it. It gets more turns from the small gear's spin. The ring has many more teeth than the centre gear, so that extra part is large, and the centre gear turns several times for each turn of the arm.

Now hold the centre gear and let the ring go. The small gear rolls on the centre gear and pushes the ring. The ring is big, so it only turns a little faster than the arm. Try The ring, then The centre gear, and compare the readouts.

In Brown's words

Another simple form of the epicyclic train, in which the arm, D, carries a pinion, B, which gears both with a spur-wheel, A, and an annular wheel, C, both concentric with the axis of the arm. Either of the wheels, A, C, may be stationary, and the revolution of the arm and pinion will give motion to the other wheel.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows only the two gears, the small gear and the arm. The disc behind the ring that holds it on the centre pin, the back plate, the base, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up.
