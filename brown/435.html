No. 435: Warren's central discharge turbine

Water rushes inward from all round the outside. Fixed curved plates turn it sideways, and it pushes through the buckets of a wheel inside them. The water leaves through the open middle.

How it works

The water starts outside and moves toward the middle. The fixed plates of the outer ring send it round the wheel at a slant.

Next it runs through the small curved buckets of the wheel. They turn the water again, so it pushes the buckets round. The water then drops out through the open middle, between the spokes.

Try a low flow, then a high one. The wheel speeds up as more water pushes through.

In Brown's words

Warren's central discharge turbine, plan view. The guides, a, are outside, and the wheel, b, revolves within them, discharging the water at the center.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing, which is a view from above. The floor under the plates, the spokes and shaft, and the number and curve of the plates and buckets are made up. The wheel speed follows the flow by a simple rule.
