No. 314: Lever chronometer escapement

A balance wheel swings to and fro on a coiled spring and keeps the time. A rocking lever with two hooks holds the escape wheel still between swings. When the lever lets go, a tooth of the wheel pushes the balance itself, not the lever.

How it works

The escape wheel is always trying to turn, but one pallet of the lever sits in the way of a tooth and holds it. For most of each swing the balance touches nothing at all.

When the balance swings anticlockwise, the pin on its roller runs into the notch of the fork and knocks the lever over. The upper pallet slides off its tooth and the wheel is free. A tooth near the roller catches the red finger and pushes it round. That push keeps the balance swinging. Then the lower pallet stops the wheel.

On the swing back the pin knocks the lever over again. The wheel only slips a short way, from the lower pallet to the upper one, and gives no push. So the balance gets one push for every two beats. Try Soft, then Stiff, and count the beats.

In Brown's words

Lever chronometer escapement. In this the pallets, A, B, and lever, look like those of the lever escapement 296: but these pallets only lock the escape-wheel, having no impulse. Impulse is given by teeth of escape-wheel directly to a pallet, C, attached to balance.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The balance wheel and its coiled spring, the back plate, the number of teeth and the sizes were made up. The two pins that stop the lever are in the drawing. The finger on the roller is straight here and curved in the drawing. The mainspring is left out and its push on the wheel is taken to be steady. The parts knock and slide on each other without rubbing, and only the balance and the wheel are slowed a little.
