No. 140: Toggle-joint punch

Two links joined at a knee are pushed until they lie in one straight line. As they straighten, they shove a punch down with a huge force. This is how machines punch holes in metal with only a hand lever.

How it works

Pull the handle down. The short arm of the lever shoves the flat link to the right. The flat link pushes the knee towards the line between the two ends of the toggle, and the two links begin to straighten.

At first the punch moves a good way for each bit of knee movement. Near the end the links are almost in a line, and the punch creeps down. A small handle movement now makes a huge push, which is just what a punch needs at the moment it cuts.

Try Short, then Full. Look at the knee reading. With Full the links come close to a straight line, and the punch moves the least in the last part of the swing.

In Brown's words

The toggle-joint arranged for a punching machine. Lever at the right is made to operate upon the joint of the toggle by means of the horizontal connecting-link.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The frame, the base, the guide, the die and the metal, the thickness of the links and all the sizes were made up. The links lie in layers, one in front of the next, so that they can pass each other. The lever is drawn swinging down and back up. The short swing was added for comparison.
