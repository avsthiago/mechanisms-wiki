No. 461: Swinging gutters

A frame of pipes hangs like a pendulum with its foot in the water. Each swing makes the water inside run one step higher. After a few swings it pours out at the top, with no pump and no wheel.

How it works

The pipes make two zigzag paths that climb from the pool to the top. When the frame hangs still, every pipe slopes uphill, so the water inside stays where it is.

When the frame swings one way, the level pipes tip down to the left. Water runs along them into the left boxes. When it swings back, the sloping pipes tip downhill instead, and the water runs on into the right boxes. A flap in each box stops it running back. So each full swing lifts the water two pipes higher.

Try Too small. The pipes never tip past level, so no water climbs. Then try Big and watch the flaps open.

In Brown's words

Pendulums or swinging gutters for raising water by their pendulous motions. Terminations at bottom are scoops, and at top open pipes ; intermediate angles are formed with boxes (and flap valve), each connected with two branches of pipe.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows the frame at one end of its swing, and the model starts the same way. The pin it hangs from, the stand, the two catch trays, the pool and the size of the swing are made up. A hand would have to push the frame to keep it swinging, and that is not shown. The pipes and boxes are cut open at the front, and the water is drawn as dots.
