No. 465: Balance pumps

Two pumps worked by one person standing on a seesaw. As the person leans from one foot to the other, one pump lifts water while the other gets ready. The heavy balls on the beam ends balance it, so it rocks easily.

How it works

Watch the person lean to the left. The left end of the beam goes down and the right end comes up. The right rod lifts its piston, and the water above that piston pours out of the spout.

Then the person leans to the right and the other pump lifts. In each pump the lid on the piston is shut on the way up, so it carries the water. On the way down the lid opens and the piston slides through the water. The valve at the foot stops the water running back into the pit.

The pumps take turns, so water comes out on every lean, first on one side, then the other. Try pausing when the beam is level and see which pump is lifting. Choose Closed to see the pumps as they look from outside.

In Brown's words

Balance pumps. Pair worked reciprocally by a person pressing alternately on opposite ends of lever or beam.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The pumps are cut open at the front so the inside shows. In the drawing the two pump pipes join in the middle and rise through the platform; here each pump pours out of its own spout onto the bank, so the water can be seen. The person is drawn as simple blocks. The rail, the clamps that hold the pumps, the inside of the pumps and all the sizes were made up. The litres count assumes a made-up size for the pumps.
