No. 283: Air-pump racks and pinion

A curved handle rocks a small gear, called a pinion, to and fro. The wheel pushes two toothed bars, called racks, one on each side. One rack rises while the other sinks, so two small air pumps work in turn. People used this to pump air out of glass jars in science experiments.

How it works

Rock the handle and the pinion turns one way, then back. Its teeth push on the teeth of both racks at once. The rack on the left goes down while the rack on the right goes up. Then they swap.

Each rack is joined to a rod and a disc, called a piston, that fits a tube. When a piston goes down it pushes air out of its tube. When it goes up it draws air in. The two pumps always work in opposite turns, so one of them is always pushing air.

Try Short, then Long. Watch how far the discs slide inside the tubes. Choose Closed to see the pumps as they look from outside.

In Brown's words

By a vibratory motion of the handle, motion is communicated by the pinion to the racks. This is used in working small air pumps for scientific experiments.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows the pinion, the curved handle and the two racks, the right one taller than the left. The back plate and base, the guide rails behind the racks, the rods, discs and tubes for the pumps, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The choice of how far the handle rocks was added to show what it changes.
