No. 450: Force pump

A pump that pushes water out instead of lifting it. The piston is solid, so it draws water in on the way up and squeezes it out on the way down. It can send water up a pipe as high as you can push.

How it works

Watch the piston rise. It leaves an empty space below it, and water from the well pushes up through the flap in the floor to fill it. The lid under the dome stays shut, so nothing comes back down the outlet pipe.

Now the piston comes down. The flap in the floor falls shut, so the water cannot go back to the well. It has only one way out: along the passage, up past the lid and into the tall pipe. The water only leaves on this stroke, in spurts.

The harder you push, the higher the water can go. Try a slow speed, then a fast one, and watch the spurts at the top of the pipe. Choose Closed to see the pump as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Ordinary force pump, with two valves. The cylinder is above water, and is fitted with solid piston ; one valve closes outlet-pipe, and other closes suction-pipe. When piston is rising suction-valve is open, and water rushes into cylinder, outlet-valve being closed. On descent of piston suction-valve closes, and water is forced up through outlet-valve to any distance or elevation.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing, which is a cut-away. The front half of the pump is left off so you can see in. The passage from the tube to the dome is drawn as one simple pipe. The sizes, the shape of the valves and the made-up size used for the litres are not from the book. The outlet pipe is cut short, and the model starts with the pump full of water.
