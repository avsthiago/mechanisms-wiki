No. 110: Double screw for spooling

A roller turns steadily and a thread guide slides along it, first one way and then back. The roller has two fine screw threads, one wound each way. Two half-nuts take turns gripping them, so the guide can lay thread evenly across a spool.

How it works

The roller turns the same way all the time. Each thread works like a bolt that is held still: if a nut grips it, the nut is pushed along the roller. A thread wound one way pushes the nut left. A thread wound the other way pushes it right.

The spindle holds a half-nut for each thread. Rocking the lever drops one half-nut onto its thread and lifts the other off. When the guide reaches the end of its sweep, the lever rocks over. Watch the two half-nuts swap, with a short pause when neither one grips.

A fine thread moves the guide only a little for each turn, so the thread is laid down evenly. Try a short sweep, then the full sweep, and watch how many turns of the roller each one takes.

In Brown's words

Uniform circular into uniform rectilinear motion; used in spooling-frames for leading or guiding the thread on to the spools. The roller is divided into two parts, each having a fine screw thread cut upon it, one a right and the other a left hand screw. The spindle parallel with the roller has arms which carry two half-nuts, fitted to the screws, one over and the other under the roller. When one half-nut is in, the other is out of gear. By pressing the lever to the right or left, the rod is made to traverse in either direction.
