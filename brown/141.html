No. 141: Band saw

A saw blade is made into one endless loop and stretched round two wheels. As the wheels turn, the blade runs in a straight line, always the same way. This gives a cut that never has to stop and go back.

How it works

The upper wheel turns and pulls the band with it. The band turns the lower wheel. Between the wheels the band does not bend. It runs along two upright straight lines, one going down and one going up.

The teeth point the same way all the time, so the saw keeps cutting. The wood sits on the table and is pushed against the straight part of the band. The turning of the wheels has become straight sawing.

The band moves as fast as the rim of a wheel. A bigger wheel has a longer rim, so it moves the band further with each turn. Try small, then big, with the same turning speed, and watch the red mark.

In Brown's words

Endless-band saw. Continuous rotary motion of the pulleys is made to produce continuous rectilinear motion of the straight parts of the saw.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing and description. The size of the wheels, the table, the frame and the teeth are made up. Brown draws a curved frame. Here it is a plain upright with two arms. The wood and the guides that steady the band are left out.
