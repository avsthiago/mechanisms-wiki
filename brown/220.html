No. 220: Offset cranks with a slot

Two shafts lie side by side, not in a line. A pin on one crank slides up and down a slot in the other. The first shaft turns steadily, but the second one speeds up and slows down.

How it works

Watch the red pin. The driving crank carries it round in a circle. The pin sits in the slot of the second crank, so it pushes that crank round with it.

As the pin goes round, it slides along the slot. Close to the second shaft it has only a short lever, so the second crank swings fast. Far from the shaft the lever is long and the crank turns slowly. So the second shaft speeds up and slows down in every turn, while the first stays steady.

Try the gap slider. With no gap the shafts are in a line and both turn at the same speed. Then try a big gap and watch the Driven reading swing.

In Brown's words

The two crank-shafts are parallel in direction, but not in line with each other. The revolution of either will communicate motion to the other with a varying velocity, for the wrist of one crank working in the slot of the other is continually changing its distance from the shaft of the latter.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown draws the shafts in the air, so no bearings are drawn. The two cranks lie in neighbouring flat layers and the pin is made long enough to reach through the slot. All sizes are made up. Brown shows one fixed gap, and the slider is added.
