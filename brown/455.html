No. 455: Old rotary pump

A drum turns inside a round casing and carries two hinged flaps, called valves. Each valve sweeps water round from the pipe at the bottom to the pipe at the top. A fixed block folds each valve shut so it can pass and start again.

How it works

Look at the two green valves. Each is a curved flap hinged on the drum. It swings out until its edge touches the casing wall. Then it works like a door across the space between drum and wall.

The drum turns and each valve pushes the water in front of it, shown as blue dots. The water goes up the left side, over the top and out by the upper pipe. Behind the valve the space grows, and that pulls new water up the bottom pipe.

The yellow block fills the space between the two pipes, so the water cannot go round again. But the valve must get past it. Follow the red valve. The sloping top of the block folds it flat into a hollow in the drum. Once past the block it swings out again and takes the next load. Choose Closed to see the pump as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Old rotary pump. Lower aperture entrance for water, and upper for exit. Central part revolves with its valves, which fit accurately to inner surface of outer cylinder. The projection shown in lower side of cylinder is an abutment to close the valves when they reach that point.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The front cover is left off, and the water is drawn as small blue balls so you can see it move. Brown does not show what swings the valves out, so here they simply open as far as the wall and the block let them. The exact slope of the block, the hollow drum with its web and shaft, and all the sizes were made up. A little water between the top pipe and the block has to slip past the valve as it folds, and the model does not show that.
