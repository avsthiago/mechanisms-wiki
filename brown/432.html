No. 432: Breast-wheel

Water runs in from the side, about level with the axle, and fills the buckets. A curved wall hugs the wheel so the water cannot slip out as the buckets go down. It works where the water does not fall very far.

How it works

A stream comes in from the right and flows into the buckets at the height of the axle. The buckets on the right fill up and become heavy.

The curved wall under the wheel keeps the water in the buckets as they go down. At the bottom the wall ends and the water pours out into the stream on the left. The empty buckets rise on the other side.

Try Low flow, then High flow. Watch how many buckets are full and how fast the wheel turns.

In Brown's words

Breast-wheel. This holds intermediate place between overshot and undershot wheels ; has float-boards like the former, but the cavities between are converted into buckets by moving in a channel adapted to circumference and width, and into which water enters nearly at the level of axle.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The stone bank, the curved wall, the back wall that holds the shaft and the water are made up. The tall pipe on the right of the drawing is left out. The water is drawn as drops and blobs, and the wheel speeds are chosen to look right, not worked out from real flow.
