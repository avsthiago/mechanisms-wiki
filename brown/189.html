No. 189: Unhooking the eccentric rod, fourth form

A rod pushed to and fro by the engine ends in an open hook, called a gab, that rests on a pin. The pin is on an arm that rocks a shaft. An upright rod and a bent lever, called a bell crank, lift the hook off the pin from a distance.

How it works

The rod slides to and fro. Its hooked end sits round the red pin, so the arm swings with it and rocks the shaft above.

Pull the upright rod and the bent lever turns. Its link lifts the tail of the hooked end, and the hook leaves the pin. The rod keeps sliding, but the arm and the shaft stay still.

Press Lifted off and watch the upright rod lean. Then press Hooked on. The hook waits until it is over the pin, then drops onto it.

In Brown's words

Another modification of 186.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The stand, the bearing, the hinge in the rod, the post for the bent lever and the sizes were made up. The bent lever is shown the other way round from the drawing so that it stays clear of the shaft. The engine's eccentric is off the picture, so the rod simply slides to and fro.
