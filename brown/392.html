No. 392: Gig saw with a spring

A thin saw blade goes up and down through a table. A crank wheel under the table pulls the bottom of the blade. A springy arm above holds the top, so the blade stays tight without any heavy frame.

How it works

The wheel turns. The crank pin goes round in a circle, and the rod pushes and pulls the bottom of the blade. The blade is held in guides, so it can only move straight up and down.

The blade cuts on the way down and rubs on the way up, so it must never go slack. The long arm above is a spring. It pulls the top of the blade up gently and bends a little each stroke. That keeps the blade straight and tight, and there is no moving frame to carry.

The blade travels twice as far as the crank pin is from the middle of the wheel. Try the Short stroke, then the Long one, and watch how much the arm has to bend.

In Brown's words

Gig-saw, the lower end connected with a crank which works it, and the upper end connected with a spring which keeps it strained without a gate.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The base, the upright post that holds the arm, the table slot and the guides are made up so it stands. The blade is drawn plain, without teeth. A real arm would also pull the blade a tiny bit sideways as it bends. That is left out.
