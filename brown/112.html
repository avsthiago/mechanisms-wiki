No. 112: Persian drill

A rod with a steep screw thread on it spins when a nut is pulled up and down along it. A drill bit at the bottom spins with it, first one way, then the other. One hand makes a plain up and down pull into a quick turning.

How it works

Pull the nut up and the rod spins one way. Push it down and the rod spins the other way. The nut cannot turn, so as it slides along the thread it makes the rod twist under it.

The thread is very steep, so one turn of the rod needs a long slide of the nut. Slide the nut that far and the rod has turned exactly once. The red stripes on the head and the chuck show this. At the end of each pull the nut stops for a moment, and so does the rod, before it turns back.

The bit at the bottom turns with the rod and drills where it touches. Try Steep, then Very steep, and compare how far the rod turns on each pull.

In Brown's words

Persian drill. The stock of the drill has a very quick thread cut upon it and revolves freely, supported by the head at the top, which rests against the body. The button or nut shown on the middle of the screw is held firm in the hand, and pulled quickly up and down the stock, thus causing it to revolve to the right and left alternately.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's description. There was no picture to copy. The rest above the head, the back plate, the block under the bit and all the sizes were made up. The nut is moved smoothly up and down, as a hand would. A real nut has a small peg or groove that rides in the thread.
