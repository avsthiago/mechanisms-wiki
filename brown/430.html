No. 430: Overshot water-wheel

Water falls into buckets at the top of the wheel. The full buckets on one side are heavy, so they pull the wheel round. It turns machines such as a mill without any engine.

How it works

Water runs along a wooden channel and pours into the buckets at the top of the wheel. The buckets on the right are full, so they are heavy. The buckets on the left are empty, so they are light.

The heavy side sinks and pulls the wheel round. Near the bottom each bucket tips out its water and starts up again, empty. The wheel turns because one side is always heavier than the other.

Try Low flow, then High flow. Watch how full the buckets get and how fast the wheel turns.

In Brown's words

Overshot water-wheel.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing, which shows only the wheel and the pit. The wooden channel, the stone walls, the back wall that holds the shaft and the water are made up. The water is drawn as drops and blobs, and the wheel speeds are chosen to look right, not worked out from real flow.
