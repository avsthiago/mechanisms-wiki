No. 323: Rolling parallel ruler

A straight ruler rides on a rod with a small wheel at each end. The wheels roll over the paper and always turn the same way, so the ruler can only roll straight ahead. A pencil along its edge draws lines that never meet.

How it works

The two wheels sit on one rod, so they turn together at the same speed. Both ends of the ruler move forward by the same amount. That is why the ruler cannot swing round.

The ridges bite into the paper, so the wheels roll instead of sliding. The pencil runs along the same edge each time, and every line is parallel to the one before.

Try Close spacing, then Wide. The ruler rolls farther for wider gaps, and the wheels turn more.

In Brown's words

Parallel ruler consisting of a simple straight ruler, B, with an attached axie; C, and pair of wheels, A, A. The wheels, which protrude but slightly through the under side of the ruler, have their edges nicked to take hold of the paper and keep the ruler always parallel with any lines drawn upon it.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The sheet of paper and the pencil were added to show the lines. Brown shows the wheels in small frames at the ends; these are left out. The ridges on the wheels are drawn in simply, and all sizes are made up.
