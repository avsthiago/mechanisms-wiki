No. 326: Slide guide for a piston rod

A block slides up and down in a tall slot cut in the engine frame. It carries the piston rod, so the rod can only move straight. A rod from the crank pushes the block, and the flywheel keeps everything turning smoothly.

How it works

The crank pin goes round in a circle. The connecting rod joins it to the slide, and the slide can only go up and down. So the turning pin pushes and pulls the slide along a straight line.

The rod is never quite upright. It leans one way, then the other. A leaning rod pushes the slide against the side of the slot. The slot wall is planed flat and true, so it takes that push and the piston rod is not bent.

The slide goes fastest when the crank is level and stops for a moment at the top and bottom. Try a short throw, then a long one. Watch how far the slide goes and how much the rod leans. Choose Closed to see the cylinder as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

A simple means of guiding or obtaining a parallel motion of the piston-rod of an engine. The slide, A, moves in and is guided by the vertical slot in the frame, which is planed to a true surface.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows only the frame, the slot, the slide, the crank and the flywheel. The cylinder, its cover, the base, the bearing, the crank arm, the rod and all the sizes were made up. The cylinder is drawn see-through so the piston can be seen.
