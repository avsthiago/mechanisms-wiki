No. 208: Pin wheel with three speeds

A wheel carries three rings of pins. A cage with slots slides along its shaft until it meets one of the rings. Each ring makes the drum turn at a different speed, with no gears to swap.

How it works

The wheel turns and its pins pass under the drum. Each pin drops into a slot and pushes the drum round by one slot. Then the pin leaves and the next one arrives.

All the pins are the same distance apart. So the drum always moves one slot for each pin that goes by. The outer circle has the most pins, so the drum turns fastest there. The inner circle has the fewest pins, so the drum turns slowest.

Slide the drum to another circle while the wheel turns. It lifts a little on the way so the pins do not catch on it. Try Inner, then Middle, then Outer, and watch the drum speed up.

In Brown's words

A pin-wheel and slotted pinion, by which three changes of speed can be obtained. There are three circles of pins of equal distance on the face of the pin-wheel, and by shifting the slotted pinion along its shaft, to bring it in contact with one or the other of the circles of pins, a continuous rotary motion of the wheel is made to produce three changes of speed of the pinion, or vice versa.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows only the wheel, the pins and the drum on its shaft. The posts that hold the shaft, the back plate, the base, the number of pins and slots, and the shape of the slots were made up. Brown does not show how the drum is moved. Here the shaft rises a little while it slides so the drum clears the pins.
