No. 1: Pulleys and an open belt

A belt runs round two wheels called pulleys. When one pulley turns, the belt drags the other round with it. Both turn the same way, and the shafts can be far apart.

How it works

The lower pulley turns and pulls the belt with it. The belt runs up one side and down the other. It drags the upper pulley round in the same direction.

The belt does not slip, so both rims move just as fast as the belt. A pulley twice as wide must turn half as fast to keep up.

Try the upper pulley at half size, then twice the size. Compare its speed with the lower one each time.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The sizes, the distance between the shafts and the frame behind are made up. Brown draws both pulleys the same size; the other two sizes were added to show the speed change. A real belt stretches and slips a little, so the upper pulley would lose a few turns.
