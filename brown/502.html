No. 502: Epicyclic train

An arm swings round a centre and carries gears with it. The gears roll on a gear at the centre, so they spin on their own pins while they travel. Trains like this add two turning motions into one.

How it works

The arm swings round the centre pin. The big centre gear is held still. The back gear of the middle pair touches it, so the pair has to roll round it and spin on its pin.

The front gear of the pair passes that spin on to the upper gear and to the small centre gear. Each of them turns partly because the arm carries it round and partly because the pair spins it. The readouts show the two motions added together.

What counts is how the big gear moves compared with the arm. Try Turning with the arm: nothing rolls, and the whole set goes round as one piece. Then try Turning against the arm, and every gear spins twice as fast on its pin.

In Brown's words

An "epicyclic train." Any train of gearing the axes of the wheels of which revolve around a common center is properly known by this name. The wheel at one end of such a train, if not those at both ends, is always concentric with the revolving frame. C is the frame or train-bearing arm. The center wheel, A, concentric with this frame, gears with a pinion, F, to the same axle with which is secured a wheel, E, that gears with a wheel, B. If the first wheel, A, be fixed and a motion be given to the frame, C, the train will revolve around the fixed wheel and the relative motion of the frame to the fixed wheel will communicate through the train a rotary motion to B on its axis. Or the first wheel as well as the frame may be made to revolve with different velocities, with the same result except as to the velocity of rotation of B upon its axis. In the epicyclic train as thus described only the wheel at one extremity is concentric with the revolving frame ; but if the wheel, E, instead of gearing with B, be made to gear with the wheel, D, which like the wheel, A, is concentric with the frame, we have an epicyclic train of which the wheels at both extremities are concentric with the frame. In this train we may either communicate the driving motion to the arm and one extreme wheel, in order to produce an aggregate rotation of the other extreme wheel, or motion may be given to the two extreme wheels, A and D, of the train, and the aggregate motion will thus be communicated to the arm.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown describes the upper gear and the small centre gear as two different ways to end the train; his drawing shows both, and so does this model. The post, the base, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. So was the choice of how the big centre gear moves, which follows Brown's remark that it may turn as well as the arm.
