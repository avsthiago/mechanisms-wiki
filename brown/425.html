No. 425: Eccentric rotary engine

This steam engine has no piston going up and down. A round piston sits off-centre on the shaft and touches the cylinder wall at one place. Steam pushes on one side of it and turns the shaft round and round.

How it works

Look at the orange piston. The shaft does not go through its middle, so the piston swings round off-centre. It always touches the cylinder wall at one place, and that place travels round the wall.

A yellow plate slides down from the top and rests on the piston. The plate and the touching place split the space into two rooms. Steam comes in beside the plate and fills one room, shown as dark blue dots. It cannot get past the plate, so it pushes the piston round. The pale dots in the other room are used steam, leaving by the other pipe.

The plate must rise and fall once in every turn to let the piston pass under it. The readout shows how far it is lifted. Now let the steam in by the left pipe. The rooms swap over and the shaft turns the other way. Choose Closed to see the engine as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

One of the many forms of rotary engine. A is the cylinder having the shaft, B, pass centrally through it. The piston, C, is simply an eccentric fast on the shaft and working in contact with the cylinder at one point. The induction and eduction of steam take place as indicated by arrows, and the pressure of the steam on one side of the piston produces its rotation and that of the shaft. The sliding abutment, D, between the induction and eduction ports moves out of the way of the piston to let it pass.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The front cover is left off, and the steam is drawn as small blue balls so you can see it move. Brown does not show what holds the plate down on the piston, so here it simply follows it. The sizes, the shape of the foot and the choice of pipe were made up.
