No. 195: Feed rolls driven by one worm

Two gears, one above the other, both turn from one screw that lies between them. The gears turn opposite ways, so the two rolls on their shafts pinch a rod and push it along. One screw feeds the rod through steadily and cannot be pushed back.

How it works

Turn the worm and its thread slides sideways, like the thread of a bolt that is turning. The thread sits in a gap of the upper gear and in a gap of the lower gear. It pushes both teeth the same way along the shaft.

The upper gear touches the worm from above and the lower gear touches it from below. So the gears turn in opposite directions. That is just what two feed rolls need. Their touching sides both move the same way and carry the rod with them.

Some worms have two threads wound side by side. Then each turn moves the gears on by two teeth, so the rod is fed twice as fast. Try One thread, then Two threads, and watch the red marks on the rod.

In Brown's words

A mode of driving a pair of feed-rolls, the opposite surfaces of which require to move in the same direction. The two wheels are precisely similar, and both gear into the endless screw which is arranged between them. The teeth of one wheel only are visible, those of the other being on the back or side which is concealed from view.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The frame, the posts that hold the worm, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The lower gear sits behind a round cover, so its teeth are hidden as in the drawing. The worm with two threads was added for comparison.
