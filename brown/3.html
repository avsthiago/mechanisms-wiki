No. 3: Belt round a corner on guide pulleys

A belt carries turning from one shaft to another that points a different way, at a right angle. Two small guide pulleys lead the belt round the corner. Each one carries one half of the belt.

How it works

The top pulley pulls the belt. One half runs left over the front guide pulley and drops straight down. It goes under the bottom pulley, comes up to the back guide pulley, and runs back to the top pulley.

On the way down, each half of the belt makes a quarter turn. That lets it lie flat on the bottom pulley, whose shaft points another way. The two halves move in opposite directions, so the guide pulleys turn opposite ways. That is why there are two.

Try forwards, then backwards. The belt runs just as well both ways. A belt twisted straight from one shaft to the other, with no guide pulleys, runs off its pulleys when turned backwards.

In Brown's words

A method of transmitting motion from a shaft at right angles to another, by means of guide-pulleys. There are two of these pulleys, side by side, one for each leaf of the belt.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing and description. The sizes, the distance between the shafts and the frame are made up. Brown does not say which pulley drives; here the large one does. The two guide pulleys share one pin, so the belt runs to the top pulley at a slight slant. A real drive would tilt them a little so the belt runs straight on.
