No. 218: Wool-comb roller catch and notch wheel

A swinging lever turns a notched wheel on a roller through a hooked catch. The catch drives the wheel back a little, then forward twice as far, then lets go while the lever swings back. So the roller feeds combed wool out in steps.

How it works

The lever turns loose on the roller shaft. The grooved disc behind it swings it to and fro once each turn. The notched wheel is fixed to the roller, so the roller only moves when the wheel does.

A catch is hinged to the top of the lever, and a spring presses it down. While its nose sits in a notch, the wheel must follow the lever. First the lever swings back a little, then forward twice as far, and the roller goes with it. Then a raised piece on the disc lifts the catch out. The lever swings back, and the nose slides over the plain rim until it drops into the next notch. All that time the roller rests.

Watch the Roller reading. It goes below zero, then up to its highest, then stays there while the Now reading says Resting. Each turn of the disc leaves the roller one notch further on. Pick the catch to follow its nose in and out of the notches.

In Brown's words

These are parts of the same movement, which has been used for giving the roller motion in wool-combing machines. The roller to which wheel, F (218), is secured is required to make one third a revolution backward, then two thirds of a revolution forward, when it must stop until another length of combed fiber is ready for delivery. This is accomplished by the grooved heart-cam, C, D, B, e (217), the stud, A, working in the said groove; from C to D it moves the roller backward, and from D to e it moves it forward, the motion being transmitted through the catch, G, to the notch-wheel, F, on the roller-shaft, H. When the stud, A, arrives at the point, e, in the cam, a projection at the back of the wheel which carries the cam strikes the projecting piece on the catch, G, and raises it out of the notch in the wheel, F, so that, while the stud is traveling in the cam from e to C, the catch is passing over the plain surface between the two notches in the wheel, F, without imparting any motion; but when stud, A, arrives at the part, C, the catch has dropped in another notch, and is again ready to move wheel, F, and roller as required.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing and description. Brown shows the lever and the cam on two separate drawings, so how they sit together was made up. The cam is placed behind the lever, with its centre below and to the right of the roller. On this page the cam is drawn faint so the wheel and lever can be seen; pick it to see it solid. Brown puts the lifting projection on the back of the cam's wheel; here it is a raised piece on the cam's front face, and the catch has a tail with a pin for it to strike. The spring on the catch, the roller, the shafts and the stands were made up. Brown says the roller goes back a third of a turn and forward two thirds. With as many notches as his drawing shows, the lever cannot swing that far, so here it goes back one notch and forward two, which keeps his one to two share.
