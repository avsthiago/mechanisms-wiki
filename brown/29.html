No. 29: Spiral disc wheel

A disc with a raised spiral on its face turns a gear on a shaft at right angles to it. Each turn of the disc moves the gear on by just one tooth. It is a simple way to make a fast shaft drive a very slow one.

How it works

Watch the place where the spiral passes under the gear. As the disc turns, the rib there slides outward, away from the middle. It pushes the tooth in front of it, so the gear turns slowly.

The rib is a spiral, so in one turn of the disc it moves out by the gap between two turns. That gap is the same as the gap between two teeth. So one turn of the disc moves the gear on by one tooth. When the rib reaches its outer end, its inner end has already come round to catch the next tooth.

A gear with more teeth needs more turns of the disc to go all the way round. Try the small gear, then the big one. Compare how many disc turns each one needs for one gear turn.

In Brown's words

Transmission of rotary motion from one shaft at right angles to another. The spiral thread of the disk-wheel drives the spur-gear, moving it the distance of one tooth at every revolution.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. As in the drawing, the spiral makes a little over one turn, so its two ends overlap where one tooth is handed on to the next. The stand, the bearings, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. Brown shows one gear; the other two sizes were added for comparison.
