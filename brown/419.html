No. 419: Self-rocking cradle

A small wheel turns round and round, and a cradle rocks gently from side to side. A rod makes a bigger wheel swing to and fro. Two belts from that wheel pull the cradle one way, then the other.

How it works

Watch the small wheel at the bottom. It turns all the way round. The rod joins its crank pin to the big wheel, but the big wheel cannot follow it round. It swings one way, then back, once for each turn of the small wheel.

The two belts cross over the top of the big wheel. When it swings one way, it winds one belt on and lets the other belt off. The winding belt pulls its post towards the middle, and the cradle rolls over a little on its curved bottom. When the big wheel swings back, the other belt pulls the other way.

The further the crank pin is from the middle of the small wheel, the more the big wheel swings, and the more the cradle rocks. Try a short crank, then the crank as drawn, and watch how far the cradle tips.

In Brown's words

Continuous circular motion converted into a rocking motion. Used in self-rocking cradles. Wheel, A, revolves, and is connected to a wheel, B, of greater radius, which receives an oscillating motion, and wheel, B, is provided with two flexible bands, C, D, which connect each to a standard or post attached to the rocker, E, of the cradle.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown does not say what turns the small wheel; in a real cradle it would be a spring or a weight, like in a clock. The stand that holds the two wheels, the floor and all the sizes were made up. The cradle itself is not drawn, only its rocker and posts.
