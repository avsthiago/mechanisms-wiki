No. 261: Weight with a short down-stroke

A turning disc makes a weight bob up and down on a cord. The cord is also wound onto a drum on the disc. So each rise is longer than each fall, and the weight climbs a little with every turn.

How it works

Turn the disc and the link rocks the arm up and down. The pulley on the arm lifts the cord and lets it down again. The weight bobs up and down, much farther than the pulley moves.

When the pulley rises, it takes up cord on both sides of it at once. So the weight rises twice as far as the pulley. At the same time the drum keeps winding in a little cord on every turn. That adds to each rise and takes away from each fall, so the weight creeps upward.

Try Held still and the drum stops, as if the cord were tied to a nail. Now the rise and the fall are equal. Then go back to On the turning drum and compare the readouts.

In Brown's words

A combination movement, in which the weight, W, moves vertically with a reciprocating movement; the down-stroke being shorter than the up-stroke. B is a revolving disk, carrying a drum which winds round itself the cord, D. An arm, C, is jointed to the disk and to the upper arm, A, so that when the disk revolves the arm, A, moves up and down, vibrating on the point, G. This arm carries with it the pulley, E. Suppose we detach the cord from the drum and tie it to a fixed point, and then move the arm, A, up and down, the weight, W, will move the same distance, and in addition the movement given to it by the cord, that is to say, the movement will be doubled. Now let us attach the cord to the drum and revolve the disk, B, and the weight will move vertically with the reciprocating motion, in which the down-stroke will be shorter than the up-stroke, because the drum is continually taking up the cord.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The sizes, the wall and the brackets were made up. Held still stops the drum, which is the same as Brown's idea of tying the cord to a fixed point. Wound on the drum, the weight would soon reach the pulley, so the model turns the disc backwards when it gets close. Then the falls are longer than the rises until the weight is low again.
