No. 363: See-saw

A long plank rests on a post in the middle. Two riders sit on its ends and push off the ground by turns, so the plank rocks up and down. It is the simplest way to turn a push into a swing back and forth.

How it works

When one end touches the ground, the rider there pushes off. That end rises and the other end sinks until it touches the ground. Then the other rider pushes off, and the plank swings back.

Gravity pulls down on each rider, and each pull turns the plank about the post. Two equal riders at equal distances turn it equally in opposite ways, so the plank is balanced. Even a small push can then swing it right across.

Make the right rider heavier and the plank is no longer balanced. The heavy end stays down and the push may lift it only part of the way. Try Same weight, then A bit heavier, then Twice as heavy.

In Brown's words

See-saw, one of the simplest illustrations of a limited oscillating or alternate circular motion.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows no riders; the two figures, the ground, the base and all the sizes were made up. The plank stops where its end meets the ground, which is a little lower than in the drawing. The riders' pushes are timed to follow each landing. The weights and the swing come from real balance and gravity. The choice of rider weight was added for comparison.
