No. 56: Lever that throws a lathe gear in and out

A lathe can turn its work fast or slow. A lever slides a big gear into mesh with a small one for slow, strong turning. Pull the lever down and the big gear slides back out of the way.

How it works

The belt keeps the pulley and the small gear turning. When the lever is up, the big gear is pressed against the small gear. Their teeth lock, so the big gear turns too, slowly and the other way.

The lever swings on a pin. The shaft of the big gear goes through a curved slot in the lever and through a straight slot in the frame. The curved slot is not centred on the pin. One end is farther from the pin than the other. As the lever swings down, the slot slides past the shaft and pulls it closer to the pin. The straight slot lets it move only backward, so the big gear leaves the small one.

Try Down, out of gear. Watch the shaft slide back and the big gear slow to a stop. Then put the lever up and see the teeth find each other again.

In Brown's words

Used for throwing in and out of gear the speed-motion on lathes. On depressing the lever, the shaft of the large wheel is drawn backward by reason of the slot in which it slides being cut eccentrically to the center or fulcrum of the lever,

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The exact shape of the frame and lever, the sliding block, the tooth counts and the sizes were made up. The far bearing of the pulley shaft and the belt are left out. Real gears might need a small nudge to slide in tooth to gap; the model lines them up as they close.
