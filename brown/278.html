No. 278: Hoist safety stop

A lift platform runs up and down between two posts lined with saw teeth. If its rope ever breaks, two catches, called holding pawls, spring out into the teeth. The platform drops only a short way and then hangs safely.

How it works

The rope holds up the platform by a pin. The pin pulls up against a curved spring. Its lower end holds up the inner ends of two bell cranks. So the outer ends keep the two holding pawls pulled in, clear of the teeth.

If the rope breaks, nothing pulls the pin up any more. The spring pushes it down. The levers turn, and their lower ends push the holding pawls out sideways into the teeth. The platform starts to fall, but each pawl lands on the flat top of the next tooth below it.

The fall is never longer than the gap between two teeth. Try breaking the rope at different moments and read how far it drops. Then mend the rope and the holding pawls pull back in.

In Brown's words

C. R. Otis's safety-stop for the platform of a hoisting apparatus. A are the stationary uprights, and B is the upper part of the platform working between them. The rope, a, by which the platform is hoisted, is attached by a pin, b, and spring, c, and the pin is connected by two elbow levers with two pawls, d, which work in ratchets secured to the uprights, A. The weight of the platform and the tension of the rope keep the pawls out of gear from the ratchets in hoisting or lowering the platform, but in case of the breakage of rope the spring, c, presses down the pin, b, and the attached ends of the levers, and so presses the pawls into the ratchets and stops the descent of the platform.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The floor of the platform, the brackets that carry the levers and the ground were added. The sizes, the tooth spacing and how far the platform travels are made up. In the drawing the lever ends simply meet the pin; here they ride on two small cross pins in slots, so they can swing.
