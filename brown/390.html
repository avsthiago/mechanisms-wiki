No. 390: Lever, two belts and ratchets

A lever rocks a half wheel to and fro. Two round belts carry the rocking down to two small pulleys. Through two ratchets, both strokes turn a flywheel the same way, so the rocking becomes steady turning.

How it works

Push one end of the lever down and the half wheel rocks. The belts are tied to its ends, so one side of each belt is pulled in and the other is let out. Both pulleys turn, but in opposite ways, because one belt is crossed.

Each pulley carries a pawl that rests on a toothed ratchet wheel fixed to the shaft. When its pulley turns clockwise, the pawl pushes a tooth and turns the shaft. When it turns back, the pawl just slides over the teeth. On every stroke one of the two pulleys is turning clockwise, so the shaft is pushed both ways the lever goes.

Between pushes the flywheel keeps the shaft going by itself. Try Heavy, then Light, and watch the flywheel speed. A light one slows down between strokes; a heavy one hardly notices.

In Brown's words

Device for converting oscillating into rotary motion. The semicircular piece, A, is attached to a lever which works on a fulcrum, a, and it has attached to it the ends of two bands, C and D, which run around two pulleys, loose on the shaft of the flywheel, B. Band, C, is open, and band, D, crossed. The pulleys have attached to them pawls which engage with two ratchet-wheels fast on the fly-wheel shaft. One pawl acts on its ratchet-wheel when the piece, A, turns one way, and the other when the said piece turns the other way, and thus a continuous rotary motion of the shaft is obtained.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The back plate, the pivot pins, the sizes and the number of ratchet teeth are made up. The second ratchet and its pawl sit behind the back pulley, out of sight from the front. The belts are round cords here and do not slip. The flywheel slows a little by rubbing between pushes.
