No. 478: Expansion steam trap

A trap that lets water out of a steam pipe but keeps the steam in. It works because a metal pipe grows a little longer when it is hot. Steam makes the pipe push its end against a valve and close it.

How it works

Water collects at the end of the long pipe. While the plunger stands a little away from the pipe's end, the water runs out of the gap, into the ball and away down the bottom pipe. Then steam follows it.

Steam is hotter than water, so the pipe warms up and grows longer. It is gripped at the left, so its right end creeps toward the plunger until it shuts against it. The steam is now trapped. As more water gathers in the pipe, the pipe cools, shrinks, and the gap opens again. The weighted lever lets the plunger give way if the pipe grows further.

Turn the stop screw in or out. Screwed in, the plunger stays further away, so the pipe opens after only a little cooling. Screwed out, the pipe must cool much more and hold back more water. Choose Closed to see the trap as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Another steam trap (Ray's patent). Valve, a, closes and opens by longitudinal expansion and contraction of waste-pipe, A, which terminates in the middle of an attached hollow sphere, C. A portion of the pipe is firmly secured to a fixed support, B. Valve consists of a plunger which works in a stuffing-box in the sphere, opposite the end of the pipe, and it is pressed toward the end of the pipe by a loaded elbow lever, D, as far as permitted by a stop-screw, b, and stop, c. When pipe is filled with water, its length is so reduced that valve remains open ; but when filled with steam, it is expanded so that valve closes it. Screw, b, serves to adjust the action of valve.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The front half of the ball and pipe is cut away so the inside shows. A real pipe grows by only about a millimetre, too little to see, so the growth is made many times bigger; the readouts give believable real sizes. The timings and sizes are made up. The dots show the water and the steam.
