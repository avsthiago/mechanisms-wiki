No. 91: Triangular eccentric

A rounded, three-cornered cam turns on a shaft inside a square frame. Its sides push the frame up and down, and the frame rests for a moment at each end. Steam engines in France used this to open and close their valves in jerks.

How it works

The shaft turns, and the cam turns with it. The cam is a rounded triangle, and every way you measure across it gives the same width. Watch the red peg go round.

The frame inside is exactly that wide, so the cam always touches both its top and its bottom. When a flat arc of the cam slides along one side, the frame stays still. When a corner swings round, the frame is pushed up or down. So the rods rest, move, rest, and move back.

Watch the readout while the cam turns. The travel stays at its top value for a part of each turn, then drops to its bottom value and stays there. Press pause at the top and the bottom to see the cam touch.

In Brown's words

Triangular eccentric, giving an intermittent reciprocating rectilinear motion, used in France for the valve motion of steam engines.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The guides for the rods, the bearing behind the shaft, the base and the sizes were made up. The corners of the cam are rounded so the shaft hub sits inside the cam. The frame is made square-cornered and wide enough for the cam to turn all the way round.
