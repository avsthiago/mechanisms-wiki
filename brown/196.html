No. 196: Pinion and swinging wheel arm

A small gear, called a pinion, turns in one place. A lumpy gear rests on it, held up by an arm. As the gear turns, its bumps roll over the small gear and lift the arm up and down in an uneven way.

How it works

The small gear turns steadily. Its teeth drive the lumpy gear the other way. The gear sits on the end of the arm, so it can move up and down. Watch the arm rise and fall.

The lumpy gear is held on the small gear by its own weight. When a big bump rolls over the small gear, the lumpy gear's centre has to be far from the small gear, so the arm is pushed up. When a narrow part comes round, the arm drops. The lumpy gear also speeds up and slows down, because the small gear has to work its way round long bumps and short sides.

Watch the Arm readout through one turn of the lumpy gear. Notice it rises twice, once high and once low. Then slow the speed right down and follow the big bump past the small gear.

In Brown's words

The pinion, B, rotates about a fixed axis and gives an irregular vibratory motion to the arm carrying the wheel, A.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows only the outline of the lumpy gear; its exact shape, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up so the teeth stay in mesh all the way round. The arm is assumed to rest on the small gear by its weight. The shaft stand behind the small gear and the floor were added to hold it up.
