No. 361: Pin clutch for a pulley

A cord turns a pulley that sits loose on a shaft. Slide the pulley along the shaft and a pin on its side meets a pin on the shaft, so the shaft turns with it. Slide it back and the shaft is free again. This is a simple way to start and stop a machine while the driving shaft keeps running.

How it works

Turn the crank. The driving shaft turns, and the cord makes the pulley below turn at the same speed. But the pulley is loose on the driven shaft, so the driven shaft stays still.

Now press Engaged. The lever slides the pulley along the shaft, and the pin on its side moves into the path of the pin on the shaft. After a part of a turn the pins meet. The pulley pushes the shaft round with it. Press Released and the pins slide apart, so the shaft slows down and stops.

Try Engaged, then Released. Notice the pins have to meet before the shaft starts, so there is a short wait. That is why this simple clutch is best for slow machines.

In Brown's words

Another simple form of clutch for pulleys, consisting of a pin on the lower shaft and a pin on side of pulley. The pulley is moved lengthwise of the shaft by means of a lever or other means to bring its pin into or out of contact with the pin on shaft.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The upper drum is made wide so the cord stays on it while the pulley slides. The posts, base, the bracket for the lever and all the sizes were added or made up. Rubbing slows the driven shaft to a stop when it is let go.
