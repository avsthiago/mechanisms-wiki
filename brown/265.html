No. 265: Cone drum and sliding roller

A drum with curved, sloping sides turns at a steady speed. A roller rests on it and is turned by it. Slide the roller towards the big end and it turns faster; towards the small end and it turns slower.

How it works

The drum turns at a steady speed. The roller rests on top and is turned by it, the way one wheel turns another by rubbing. The arm it slides on is hinged on the right, so it can rise and fall with the drum's outline.

Every turn of the drum, the surface under the roller travels once round the drum. Near the big end that is a long way, near the small end a short way. The roller's rim must travel the same distance, so its speed follows the size of the drum where it touches.

Slide the roller from the big end to the small end and watch the Roller reading drop. Try the middle, then the small end, and compare. Notice how the arm tips as the roller goes.

In Brown's words

Variable motion. If the conical drum has a regular circular motion, and the friction-roller is made to traverse lengthwise, a variable rotary motion of the friction-roller will be obtained.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown does not show how the roller is held or moved along; here it slides on a rod hinged at its right end, and the slider moves it. The roller is slightly rounded on its rim so that it touches the curved drum at one point. The bearings, the hinge and the base were made up.
