No. 360: Beam, drum and ratchet

A beam rocks to and fro, but the big wheel behind it turns one way only. A cord from the beam turns a drum back and forth, and a small catch on the drum, called a pawl, pushes a toothed wheel, called a ratchet wheel, round in one direction. This turns a rocking motion into a steady turning one.

How it works

Watch the cord from the right end of the beam. When that end goes up, the cord pulls the drum round one way. When it goes down, the drum turns back. The drum only ever rocks.

The pawl is fixed to the drum and rests against the teeth of the ratchet wheel. Going one way it pushes a tooth and turns the wheel and the big wheel with it. Going back it slides over the teeth with a click and leaves the wheel alone. The heavy big wheel keeps turning in between.

Try a small rock, then a big one. A bigger rock turns the drum further, so the pawl pushes more teeth on each stroke.

In Brown's words

Continuous rotary motion from oscillating. The beam being made to vibrate, the drum to which the cord is attached, working loose on fly-wheel shaft, gives motion to said shaft through the pawl and ratchet-wheel, the pawl being attached to drum and the ratchet-wheel fast on shaft.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The beam is rocked by an unseen hand or crank. The post, base and the two bearings were added so it stands up, and the sizes, the number of teeth and the rubbing on the big wheel were made up. The cord is tied at both ends, so the ball only hangs there and follows the left end of the beam.
