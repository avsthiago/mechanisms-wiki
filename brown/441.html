No. 441: Persian wheel

A wheel with curved arms stands half in a stream. The stream pushes the arms and the wheel turns. Buckets hang from the arm tips, fill in the water and carry the water up to a trough.

How it works

The stream runs past the lower half of the wheel. It pushes on the curved arms and the wheel turns. Each bucket hangs from the tip of its arm, so it stays upright while the wheel turns. At the bottom it dips under the water and fills.

The full bucket is carried up the far side. Near the top it meets a fixed pin. The pin tips the bucket and the water runs out into the trough. Then the empty bucket swings upright and goes down again.

Try Slow, then Fast. Count how many buckets are emptied in the same time.

In Brown's words

Persian wheel, used in Eastern countries for irrigation. It has a hollow shaft and curved floats, at the extremities of which are suspended buckets or tubs. The wheel is partly immersed in a stream acting on the convex surface of its floats, and as it is thus caused to revolve, a quantity of water will be elevated by each float at each revolution, and conducted to the hollow shaft at the same time that one of the buckets carries its fill of water to a higher level, where it is emptied by coming in contact with a stationary pin placed in a convenient position for tilting it.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The back post, the trough, the pin, the stream bed and the water are made up. Brown also says the hollow shaft receives water; that is not shown. The number of arms and buckets, and the wheel speeds, are chosen to look right, not worked out from real flow.
