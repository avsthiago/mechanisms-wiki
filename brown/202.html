No. 202: Worm and wheel for heavy work

A long screw lies under a big wheel that has many fine teeth. Several threads of the screw press on several teeth at once. The load is shared, so the wheel turns very slowly, very smoothly and with great strength.

How it works

Turn the crank and the long screw, called a worm, spins on its shaft. Its thread seems to slide sideways along the shaft. Here the thread is fine and the worm is long, so about ten threads sit in ten teeth of the wheel at the same moment.

One turn of the worm moves the wheel on by just one tooth. Because many teeth carry the push together, each one takes only a small share. That is why this kind of worm is used when the work is heavy, or when the wheel must turn steadily.

Some worms have two threads wound side by side. Then each turn moves the wheel on by two teeth, so it goes twice as fast. Try One thread, then Two threads, and watch the red tooth.

In Brown's words

Worm or endless screw and worm-wheel. Modification of 30, used when steadiness or great power is required.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The crank, the plate and bearings that hold the shafts, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The wheel has plain straight teeth. A real wheel of this kind has its teeth hollowed round the worm so the screw wraps around it. The worm with two threads was added for comparison.
