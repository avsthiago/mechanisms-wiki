No. 270: Anti-friction pulley bearing

A rope pulls a pulley round, and the pulley has to turn on its shaft. Rubbing metal on metal wastes effort and wears out. Here the pulley rides on a ring of small rollers, so the surfaces roll over each other and do not rub.

How it works

On the left you see the pulley from outside: a round plate with bolts, and the end of the shaft in the middle. On the right the same pulley is cut open. Between the shaft and the wheel there are six small rollers.

The rope turns the wheel. The wheel's hole rolls on the rollers, and the rollers roll on the shaft. Nothing slides. A roller travels round the shaft more slowly than the wheel turns, and spins on its own axis faster than the wheel.

Try the three settings for the shaft. Held still, the rollers circle round it. If the shaft turns too, the rollers circle faster or slower, but they never slide.

In Brown's words

Anti-friction bearing for a pulley.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows a pulley from outside and the same pulley cut open, with a rope over each. The back plate and base, the shaft ends, the bolts, the number and size of the rollers and the speeds were made up. The rollers have no cage to hold them apart here. Brown draws the rope ends hanging down; here they carry on out of view.
