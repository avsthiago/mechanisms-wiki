No. 290: Pendulum escapement

Here the pendulum rod carries a ring that hangs right round the escape wheel. Two small ledges inside the ring take turns to catch a tooth. Each swing of the pendulum lets the wheel move on by half a tooth.

How it works

The weight tries to turn the wheel clockwise. On the right, the tip of a tooth comes down onto the flat top of a ledge and stops. The wheel cannot move.

The pendulum swings the ring to the right and the ledge slides out from under the tooth. The tooth runs down the sloping end of the ledge and pushes it further the same way. That push keeps the pendulum swinging. The wheel turns until a tooth on the left comes up under the other ledge, which has just moved in. On the swing back the same thing happens on the left. The flat faces also move the resting tooth a little each swing, so the wheel is pushed back slightly. The Recoil number shows how far.

The pendulum sets the pace. A long pendulum swings more slowly, so the beats come further apart. Try Short, then Long, and count the beats.

In Brown's words

Another kind of pendulum escapement.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown gives no description beyond the name, so how the ledges work is worked out from the picture. The sloping ends of the ledges, the weight at the bottom of the rod, the pin at the top, the back plate and the sizes were made up. The backs of the teeth are hollowed more than drawn so that the ledges have room. The weight that turns the wheel is left out and its pull is taken to be steady. The teeth slide on the ledges without rubbing, and only the pendulum and the wheel are slowed a little.
