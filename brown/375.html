No. 375: Edge runners

Two heavy stone wheels roll round and round in a shallow pan. They crush or grind whatever is in the pan. A level shaft turns an upright shaft through bevel gears, and the upright shaft carries the wheels.

How it works

The level shaft at the top turns the big flat gear. The big gear is on the upright shaft, so it carries the axle round and round. The wheels at the ends of the axle roll in a circle round the pan.

The big gear has twice as many teeth as the small one, so the upright shaft turns once for every two turns of the level shaft. Each wheel rolls without sliding, so a wheel that is farther from the shaft has farther to go and spins faster. Look at the red stripes.

Try sliding the axle. Choose Right wheel in, then Left wheel in, and see which wheel spins faster. The two wheels are different distances from the shaft, so they spin at different speeds.

In Brown's words

Pair of edge runners or chasers for crushing or grinding. The axles are connected with vertical shaft, and the wheels or chasers run in an annular pan or trough.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The wheels are drawn a little different distances from the shaft, and that is kept. The frame, the pan's shape, the bearings and the sizes were made up. The level shaft gets a pointer so you can see it turn. The pan is empty. In a real mill the wheels also rub sideways on the grain, because the outer edge of each wheel has farther to go than the inner edge.
