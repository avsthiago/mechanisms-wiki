No. 245: Bayonet joint

A tube slides into a socket and a short pin on the tube runs along a bent slot. A small twist locks the tube in. A twist the other way lets it be pulled out again. It joins two parts quickly and holds them firmly.

How it works

Watch the red pin. It starts at the end of the sideways part of the slot, which is the locked place. First the tube is twisted back. The pin slides along to the open end of the slot.

Now the pin is under the straight part of the slot. The tube can be lifted straight out. To put it back, push it in until the pin reaches the bottom of the slot, then twist it the other way.

Once twisted, the pin sits under the roof of the sideways slot, so a pull on the tube cannot lift it out. Only a twist back to the open end frees it. Play the cycle and watch the pin each time.

In Brown's words

Bayonet joint. On turning the part, A, it is released from the L-shaped slot in the socket, B, when it can be withdrawn.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown draws one pin and one slot with the socket cut open. The socket is drawn as a thin shell. The size, the length of the slot and the amount of twist were made up. The socket has a floor so it looks closed.
