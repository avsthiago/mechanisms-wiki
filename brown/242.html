No. 242: Strap brake

A strap goes round a wheel, and a lever tightens it. The harder you pull the lever, the harder the strap grips and the slower the wheel turns. Cranes use this to hold a heavy load that is running down.

How it works

The wheel turns and the strap lies round it. The strap has two ends. Both are pinned to the lever, one on each side of the lever's pin. When you pull the end of the lever down, the lever turns about its pin.

Turning the lever moves the two pins so that the strap becomes too short for the loop it has to make. It squeezes the wheel. The rubbing between strap and rim holds the wheel back. A harder pull gives a harder squeeze.

Try Let go, then Light, then Hard. With a light pull the wheel only slows down. With a hard pull it stops. Let go again and it speeds up.

In Brown's words

A brake used in cranes and hoisting machines. By pulling down the end of the lever, the ends of the brake-strap are drawn toward each other, and the strap tightened on the brake-wheel.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows only the wheel, the strap and the lever. The stand, the pin the lever turns on, the shaft and the sizes were made up. Something has to push the wheel round, so the model gives it a steady push, as a load running down would. The way the wheel slows is a simple estimate, not a measurement.
