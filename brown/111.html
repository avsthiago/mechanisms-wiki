No. 111: Differential screw

A hollow screw turns in a fixed nut. A thinner screw with finer threads sits inside it and cannot turn. The block on the lower end of the thin screw creeps down by a tiny step for each turn of the handle. This gives very fine and very strong movement, as in a micrometer.

How it works

Turn the handle. The hollow screw turns in the fixed nut and moves down, one step of its thread for each turn. The thin screw inside cannot turn, so it is carried along with it.

But the thin screw is threaded into the hollow one. As the hollow screw turns, the thin screw is wound backwards through it, by one step of its own finer thread for each turn. The die moves down by the big step, then back up by the small step. What is left is the difference, which is tiny.

A tiny movement for a full turn of the handle means the handle can push very hard. Try Almost the same, then Far apart. Watch the die and its reading.

In Brown's words

Micrometer screw. Great power can be obtained by this device. The threads are made of different pitch and run in different directions, consequently a die or nut fitted to the inner and smaller screw would traverse only the length of the difference between the pitches for every revolution of the outside hollow screw in a nut.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The nut and its post, the base, the guide for the die, the handle, the sizes and the thread spacings were made up. Both threads run the same way, because that is what makes the movement the difference of the two steps. The handle turns one way and back so the travel stays on the page. The thread inside the hollow screw is left out, and the part of the thin screw inside the hollow one is hidden. The four inner threads were added for comparison.
