No. 52: Stud clutch

Two discs sit face to face on two shafts. One has two pins sticking out, the other has two holes. Push them together and the pins drop into the holes, so both shafts turn as one.

How it works

The stud disc turns all the time. The sliding disc is a little way off, so the driven shaft stands still. Nothing touches.

Push the sliding disc in and it meets the tips of the studs. It must wait until its holes come round to the studs. Then the studs slip in and the two discs are locked. The driven shaft jumps straight to the same speed.

Try In and count how long the disc waits. With only two studs it can wait up to half a turn. Then try Out and watch the shaft run down.

In Brown's words

Another kind of clutch-box. The disk-wheel to the right has two holes, corresponding to the studs fixed in the other disk; and, being pressed against it, the studs enter the holes, when the two disks rotate together.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The sizes, the stands and the lever handle were made up. Brown does not show where the two shafts meet; here the end of the driven shaft turns in a hole in the stud disc. The coupling on the right end of the driven shaft is copied from the drawing; it would join the shaft to the next one.
