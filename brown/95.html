No. 95: Tilted disc and rod

A disc is fixed on an upright shaft, but set at a slant. As the shaft turns, the disc wobbles, and an upright rod resting on it is pushed up and lets itself down again. A turning motion becomes a straight up-and-down one.

How it works

The rod stands over the edge of the disc, and its rounded end rests on the top face. When the high side of the disc is under the rod, the rod is at its highest. Half a turn later the low side is under it and the rod has sunk.

The rod always sits exactly on the disc, so its height is just the height of the disc surface beneath it. The disc is flat and tilted, so the height rises and falls smoothly, once for each turn. The rod moves straight up and down, never sideways.

The steeper the slant, the farther the rod moves. Try Small, then Steep, and watch the red ring and the travel reading.

In Brown's words

On rotating the upright shaft, reciprocating rectilinear motion is imparted by the oblique disk to the upright rod resting upon its surface.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The sizes, the round end on the rod, the two guides and the post that holds them, the hub, the collar and the end of the shaft were made up. Brown shows the rod resting on the disc with no guide, so a guide was added to keep it upright. The rod is held down only by its weight. The Small and Steep slants were added for comparison.
