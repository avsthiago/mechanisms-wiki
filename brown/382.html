No. 382: Adjustable mirror stand

A mirror sits on a thin stem inside a pillar. It can be raised or lowered, turned to the left or right, and tipped up or down. Two screws hold each setting.

How it works

The screw in the socket backs off. The stem can now slide up and down, or spin round. Then the screw is tightened and the stem is held fast. The screw on the hinge does the same job for the tilt of the mirror.

The set screw works by pressing its end against the stem. The press makes the rub too strong for the weight of the mirror to overcome, so it stays where it is put. Loosen the screw and the stem moves freely.

Pick Height, Turn or Tilt and watch which screw backs off. Then try All three to see them one after the other.

In Brown's words

Adjustable stand for mirrors, etc., by which a glass or other article can be raised or lowered, turned to the right or left, and varied in its inclination. The stem is fitted into a socket of pillar, and secured by a set screw, and the glass is hinged to the stem, and a set screw is applied to the hinge to tighten it. The same thing is used for photographic camera-stands.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The shape of the pillar, the size of the mirror and the way the hinge is held were made up. Brown says the same stand is used for camera stands. In the model the stem is the only part that spins in the socket, and the movements go back and forth by themselves.
