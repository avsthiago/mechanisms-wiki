No. 165: Waved wheel and rod

A wheel with a wavy lower edge turns on an upright shaft. A small roller rides under that edge and lifts and drops an upright bar. A turning motion becomes a straight up-and-down one, and a jointed rod follows along.

How it works

The wheel turns and the roller stays in one place under its edge. Where the edge dips low, it pushes the roller down, and the bar goes down with it. Where the edge is high, the spring pushes the roller back up against it.

The bar can only slide up and down in its guide, so the roller goes straight up and down. The jointed rod is pinned to the roller. It folds and unfolds a little at the middle joint and swings about the post. It does not move the bar. It just follows it.

Count the dips that pass the roller in one turn: that is how many times the bar goes down and up. Try Few, As drawn and Many, and watch the bar speed reading.

In Brown's words

Circular into rectilinear motion. The waved-wheel or cam on the upright shaft communicates a rectilinear motion to the upright bar through the oscillating rod.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The sizes, the shape of the waves, the spring that holds the roller up, the guide, the bearing at the top of the shaft, the post, the back plate and the base were made up. Brown does not say what presses the roller up, so a spring was added. The bar height is worked out exactly from where the round roller touches the wavy edge. The Few and Many choices were added for comparison.
