No. 8: Speed pulleys

Two stepped pulleys are joined by one belt. Moving the belt to another pair of steps changes how fast the lower shaft turns. A lathe uses this to turn slowly for big, hard jobs and fast for small ones.

How it works

The upper pulley turns at a steady speed and pulls the belt along. The belt turns the lower pulley. Each pulley is a stack of three steps. The stacks face opposite ways, so a big step always sits across from a small one.

The belt moves the same distance on both pulleys. So a small step has to turn more times than a big one. On Fast, the belt runs from a big step above to a small step below, and the lower pulley turns faster. On Slow it is the other way round.

One belt must fit every pair of steps. Big plus small is not quite enough: when the two steps differ a lot, the belt needs a little extra. So the small steps are made a bit smaller. Try Slow, Middle and Fast. The same belt fits every pair without going loose.

In Brown's words

Speed-pulleys used for lathes and other mechanical tools, for varying the speed according to the work operated upon.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing and description. The sizes, the number of steps and the frame are made up. Brown does not say which pulley drives; here the upper one does. The lower steps were worked out from the exact length of an open belt, so the same belt fits all three pairs. In a real shop the belt is moved by hand with the machine stopped. Here it slides across while turning, so you can follow it.
