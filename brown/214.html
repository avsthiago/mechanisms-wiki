No. 214: Watch stop, third kind

A stop that lets a watch spring be wound only so many turns, then blocks the key. Two gears of different sizes each carry a finger. Because they turn at different speeds, the fingers miss each other for a few turns, then meet and stop.

How it works

Turn the key and both gears turn, opposite ways. The small gear has fewer teeth, so it goes round a little faster. Each finger sweeps across the space between the two arbors once per turn.

At first the fingers cross that space at different moments, so they miss. But each turn the small gear gets a little further ahead. After a few turns the two fingers reach the space together and hit. Then neither gear can turn, so the key stops.

Let it run and watch the turns count up until the fingers meet. Then it turns back as the spring unwinds, until the fingers meet again from the other side. Watch how close the fingers come on each turn before they hit.

In Brown's words

Other modifications of the stop, the operations of which will be easily understood by a comparison with 212.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The key, the plate that holds the arbors, the sizes and the shape of the fingers were made up. The numbers of teeth were chosen so the fingers meet once every few turns, so the two gears differ in size a little more than in Brown's drawing. The model stops at the exact place where the two fingers first touch. The spring is left out, so the model just turns back to show unwinding.
