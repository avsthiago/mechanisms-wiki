No. 137: Expansion eccentric

A lumpy cam turns on a shaft between two small rollers. The rollers sit on a forked arm, so the arm is lifted and dropped as the cam goes round. A rod on the arm works the slide valve of a steam engine.

How it works

The shaft turns the cam. The cam is not round, so it pushes on the two rollers as it goes by. Watch the red peg.

One roller sits above the cam and one below. The gap between them is just as wide as the cam, so both rollers touch it all the way round. When the wide part swings up, it lifts the arm. When it swings down, the arm drops. The rods below slide in guides, so the arm goes straight up and down and never sideways.

The lumps make the arm speed up and slow down as it goes. Try Gentle, Flat top and Bumpy, and watch the arm speed.

In Brown's words

Expansion eccentric used in France to work the slide-valve of a steam engine. The eccentric is fixed on the crank-shaft, and communicates motion to the forked vibrating arm to the bottom of which the valve-rod is attached.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The guides, the stand behind the shaft, the second rod under the lower roller, the base and the sizes were made up. Brown's valve rod runs off the edge of the drawing. The cam is shaped so that its width never changes, which keeps both rollers touching. The three cam shapes were added for comparison.
