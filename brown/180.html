No. 180: Single bench clamp

A curved jaw turns on one screw next to a fixed strip. Push a board down between them and the board turns the jaw by touching it. The jaw then grips the board against the strip and holds it still.

How it works

Push the board down. Its lower corner slides along the curved edge of the jaw and pushes it. The jaw can only turn on its screw, so it swings aside to let the board pass.

Once the board is past the narrowest part of the curve, the jaw stays turned and presses on the board, helped by a light spring. A thicker board turns the jaw more, and its lower end swings in until the board cannot go deeper. Pulling the board up turns the jaw back towards the strip, so the grip gets tighter.

Try the thin board, then the thick one. Watch how far each goes in before it is held, and how far the jaw turns.

In Brown's words

This only differs from 174 in being composed of a single pivoted clamp operating in connection with a fixed side-piece.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows the strip, the jaw and the screw, seen from above. The jaw outline is traced from the drawing. The board, the backing plate, the thickness choices and the depth of the board were made up. The jaw has a light spring so that it rests against the strip when empty. The contact is worked out exactly from the jaw outline and the board edges.
