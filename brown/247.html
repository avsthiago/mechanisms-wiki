No. 247: Releasing a sounding weight

A heavy weight rides on a long rod and drags it down to the sea bed. When the rod touches bottom, a catch lets the weight go. The rod can then be pulled up without the heavy load.

How it works

The weight sits on the nose of the catch and hangs on the rod. The rod goes down with it. The flat foot at the bottom reaches the sea bed first.

The foot stops, but the rod keeps sinking a little. So the foot is pushed up into the rod and lifts one end of the catch. The other end, the nose, swings in and leaves the weight with nothing to rest on. The weight drops off and stays on the sea bed.

Now the rod is hauled up and slides out through the hole in the weight. Once the nose is clear, the foot drops back and the catch springs out again. Watch the whole cycle from lowering to the next weight. Choose Closed to see the rod and weight as they look from outside.

In Brown's words

A mode of releasing a sounding-weight. When the piece projecting from the bottom of the rod strikes the bottom of the sea, it is forced upward relatively to the rod, and withdraws the catch from under the weight, which drops off and allows the rod to be lifted without it.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The pivot, the return of the catch, the sea bed and the new weight that appears at the top for each cycle were made up. The rod is cut in half like the drawing. The striker is held up while the rod is hauled so the nose cannot catch on the weight.
