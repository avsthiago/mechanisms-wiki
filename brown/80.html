No. 80: Hooked pawls moving a rack

A lever rocks to and fro, and a toothed bar climbs upward in small steps. Two hooks hang from the lever and take turns to lift the bar. Rocking is turned into a straight, one-way push.

How it works

Each tooth on the bar has a flat underside and a sloping top. A hook hangs from each end of the lever. The two hooks cross over and grip the teeth on the far edge of the bar.

When the right end of the lever rises, its hook lifts the bar by the tooth it holds. At the same time the left end goes down. Its hook slides down the sloping teeth and drops in under a lower one. Then the lever rocks back, and the hooks swap jobs. So the bar rises on every swing and never slips back.

Watch which hook turns red. When the teeth run out, the hooks are lifted off and the bar drops back to start again. Try Tiny. The hooks cannot reach the next tooth, so the bar stops. Then try Wide and compare.

In Brown's words

Rectilinear motion is imparted to the slotted bar, A, by the vibration of the lever, C, through the agency of the two hooked pawls, which drop alternately into the teeth of the slotted rack-bar, A.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The plate, the pin's mounting, the guides at the foot of the bar, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The guides hold the bar lightly, so it stays put between lifts. Lifting the hooks and dropping the bar back at the end was added so the model can run without stopping.
