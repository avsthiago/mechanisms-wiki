No. 391: Weighted racks turning a wheel

A piston rod goes up and down. Two toothed bars, called racks, ride on it, one each side of a gear. One rack turns the wheel on the way up and the other on the way down, so the wheel keeps turning the same way without a crank.

How it works

Look at the wheel first. Its left side moves down and its right side moves up. So a rack on the right can push it round while rising, and a rack on the left can push it round while falling. That is what the two racks do, taking turns.

A rack must stay out of the teeth on its way back, or it would turn the wheel the wrong way. The grooves see to that. The red pin of each rack runs round a loop. On one side of the loop the rack stands upright in the wheel. On the other side it leans clear. Each rack is short, so it runs off the wheel just before the piston turns round.

The weights tip both racks to the left. For the left rack that is away from the wheel, and for the right rack it is towards it. So at the top the right rack needs a push to cross to the far side of its loop. The bell crank and its spring give that push. Change the wheel speed and watch one stroke slowly. Choose Closed to see the cylinder as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Reciprocating into rotary motion. The weighted racks, A, A¹, are pivoted to the end of a piston-rod, and pins at the end of the said racks work in fixed guide-grooves, b, b, in such manner that one rack operates upon the cog-wheel in ascending and the other in descending, and so continuous rotary motion is produced. The elbow lever, C, and spring, d, are for carrying the pin of the right-hand rack over the upper angle in its guide-groove, b.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The tooth numbers, the sizes, the exact shape of the two grooves, the floor and the steam cylinder under it were made up. The drawing is hard to read where the bell crank meets the rack, so its shape, the roller it bears on, its stop and the place of the spring are a guess that works. The racks are shorter than in the drawing so that each one runs clear of the wheel at the end of its stroke. For a moment at each end no rack is in mesh. A real machine would need a heavy wheel to carry it over, and here the wheel is simply kept turning steadily.
