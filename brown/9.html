No. 9: Cone pulleys

Two cones point opposite ways and are joined by one belt. Sliding the belt along changes the speed of the lower cone little by little, with no jumps. Cotton spinning machines use this to speed up or slow down smoothly.

How it works

The upper cone turns at a steady speed and pulls the belt. The belt is crossed, so it turns the lower cone the other way. Wherever the upper cone is thick, the lower cone is thin.

Each turn of a cone moves the belt once round the cone. On Fast, the belt sits where the upper cone is thick and the lower cone is thin. So the lower cone turns faster than the upper one.

One belt has to fit at every place along the cones. A crossed belt stays the same length if the two sizes add up to the same amount. Straight cones do exactly that. Try Slow, then drag slowly to Fast, and watch the lower speed climb while the belt stays tight.

In Brown's words

Cone-pulleys for the same purpose as 8. This motion is used in cotton machinery, and in all machines which are required to run with a gradually increased or diminished speed.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing and description. The sizes and the frame are made up, and the upper cone is taken as the driver. Brown's drawing shows the belt edge on, so you cannot tell if it is crossed. Here it is crossed, because then straight cones keep the belt exactly the same length. With an open belt, straight cones would leave it a little loose in the middle. Real cone drives have a fork to push the belt along; it is left out.
