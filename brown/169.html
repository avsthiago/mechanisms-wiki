No. 169: Crank, link and connecting rod

A turning crank moves a long flat bar, called a connecting rod. The bar pushes a lever to and fro. A short link joins the far end of the bar to a second crank. The second crank is not driven by anything but the link, yet it goes round and round.

How it works

The driving crank pin goes round in a circle. It holds the long bar near its middle, so the bar slides and tilts as the pin moves. The left end rocks the lever. The right end swings the short link, and the link turns the second crank.

The second crank does not turn at an even pace. It speeds up and slows down while the driving crank turns evenly. That is because the link pushes the second crank from a slanting angle that keeps changing.

Try the longer link and watch the second crank. Its speed changes in a different way, and the bar tilts the same.

In Brown's words

A modification of 168, in which a link is used to connect the pitman with the main crank, thereby dispensing with the slot in the said crank.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The back plate, the shafts, the pins, the thickness of every bar and the length of the lever were made up. The lever's pivot is below the picture and its length was chosen so the model turns right round without jamming. The longer link was added for comparison.
