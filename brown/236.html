No. 236: Lever with two pawls, nearly continuous

A long lever rocks about a pin. Two pawls, small catches, hang from it on either side of the pin. While one end of the lever goes down, the other goes up, so one pawl is always ready to push the wheel round. The wheel turns almost without stopping.

How it works

A crank rocks the long lever to and fro about its fixed pin. One pawl hangs from the lever on one side of the pin and the other pawl hangs on the other side. Their tips rest on the teeth of the wheel.

The two sides of the lever move in opposite ways. When one pawl is carried forward it pushes a tooth and turns the wheel. At the same time the other pawl slides back over the teeth and drops into a gap. Then the lever turns round and the two swap jobs. The wheel only pauses for a moment while they change over.

Try the three lever swings. A small swing moves the wheel only a short way each time, and it waits longer between pushes. A big swing carries it on by several teeth with almost no waiting.

In Brown's words

A nearly continuous circular motion is imparted to the ratchet-wheel on vibrating the lever, a, to which are attached the two pawls, b and c.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows the handle end of the lever free, so a crank and rod were added to rock it. The back plate, base, pins, tooth count and sizes were made up. The teeth and pawl tips are shaped a little differently from the drawing so that they work.
