No. 69: One-tooth driver

A small wheel with one tooth turns a big toothed wheel one tooth at a time. Between pushes, the round edge of the small wheel sits between two teeth and holds the big wheel still. It is a simple way to make a wheel turn very slowly, in steps.

How it works

Turn the crank and the small wheel goes round steadily. Once each turn, its tooth catches a tooth of the big wheel. It pushes the big wheel on by one tooth, then lets go.

The rest of the time the round edge of the small wheel rests between two teeth, touching both tips. So the big wheel cannot turn either way. The cut-outs beside the tooth let the tips swing past while the big wheel moves.

The big wheel moves one tooth for each turn of the crank, so it turns very slowly. Count the turns and the teeth moved. Then slow the speed right down to watch the tooth take over from the round edge.

In Brown's words

B, a small wheel with one tooth, is the driver, and the circumference entering between the teeth of the wheel, A, serves as a lock or stop while the tooth of the small wheel is out of operation.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The crank, the plate and the sizes were made up, and the cut-outs were worked out so nothing rubs. Brown draws the tooth sticking well out past the round edge. A tooth that long would push the big wheel more than one tooth, and the round edge would then hit a tip. So here the tooth only just reaches past the edge. It gives the big wheel a small knock as it starts to push.
