No. 158: Treadle and disc

A foot board, called a treadle, is pressed down and let up again. A rod joins it to a pin on a heavy disc, and the pin drags the disc round and round. So a foot that only goes up and down can keep a wheel turning, as on an old sewing machine or a potter's wheel.

How it works

The pin goes round in a circle. The rod pulls the end of the treadle up and pushes it down, so the board rocks on its hinge. One turn of the disc makes one down stroke and one up stroke.

At the top and bottom of each stroke the rod points straight at the middle of the disc. Then pushing along the rod cannot turn the disc, so the disc's own weight carries it past. This is why the disc is heavy.

Try Near, then Medium, then Far. A pin close to the middle makes the treadle swing only a little. A pin far out makes it swing a lot. Watch the swing reading.

In Brown's words

Reciprocating curvilinear motion of the treadle gives a circular motion to the disk. A crank may be substituted for the disk.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. In the model the speed control turns the disc and the treadle follows it, which gives the same motion as a foot driving the treadle. A real foot would need the disc's weight to pass the two dead spots. The post, the shaft, the base, the hinge block, the pins and all the sizes were made up.
