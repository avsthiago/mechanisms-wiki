No. 312: Bloxam's gravity escapement

A weight tries to turn a big wheel and a small wheel on one shaft. Two hanging arms, called pallets, take turns to stop a tooth of the big wheel. The small wheel lifts each pallet a little, and the pallet then pushes the pendulum with its own weight as it falls back. The wheels never touch the pendulum, so every push is the same.

How it works

Start with the pallet whose stop has a tooth of the big wheel resting on it. The wheels cannot turn. A point of the small wheel sits under the nose of this pallet and holds it pushed out a little way.

The pendulum swings out and meets the fork pin of that pallet. It carries the pallet further out, and the stop slides off the tooth. The wheels jump on. As they turn, a point of the small wheel slides along the nose of the other pallet and pushes it out, and the next tooth lands on that pallet's stop. Then the pendulum swings back and the first pallet falls with it. The point that held it has moved away, so it falls further than it was carried. That extra fall is the push that keeps the pendulum swinging.

The push comes from the weight of the pallet, not from the wheels. Try Weak, then Strong. The wheels jump faster, but the pendulum swings just as far as before. A clock like this keeps good time even when the pull on its wheels changes.

In Brown's words

Bloxam's gravity escapement. The pallets are lifted alternately by the small wheel, and the stopping is done by the action of the stops, A and B, on the larger wheel. E and F are the fork-pins which embrace the pendulum.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The small wheel is larger than Brown shows, so you can see it lift the pallets. The two pallets hang from two pins side by side, where Brown draws one. The shape of the noses and stops, the place of the fork pins, the pendulum, the back plate, the rest pins, the base and all the sizes were made up. The weight that turns the wheels is left out. Nothing rubs in this model. Only the air slows the pendulum and the wheels.
