No. 414: Scroll gear and sliding pinion

A small gear turns a plate whose teeth run round it in a spiral. The gear slides along its shaft to keep up with the spiral. Turned one way the plate speeds up; turned back it slows down.

How it works

The shaft turns at one steady speed, and so does the small gear on it, called a pinion. Its teeth push the teeth of the spiral sideways, so the plate turns. As it turns, the spiral under the shaft moves in or out, and the pinion slides along the shaft to stay on it.

Each tooth the pinion pushes moves the plate by the same distance along the spiral. Near the middle that distance is a bigger part of a turn, so the plate turns faster. Far out it is a smaller part, so the plate turns slower.

The shaft turns one way until the pinion reaches an end of the spiral, then turns back. Watch the plate readout and the red stripe: they speed up while the pinion slides inward and slow down while it slides out.

In Brown's words

Scroll gear and sliding pinion, to produce an increasing velocity of scroll-plate, A, in one direction, and a decreasing velocity when the motion is reversed. Pinion, B, moves on a feather on the shaft.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The three arms and hub behind the scroll, the back plate and the brackets that hold the shafts, the sizes, the number of teeth and the spacing of the spiral were made up. Brown does not say what turns the shaft; here it simply turns back at each end of the spiral.
