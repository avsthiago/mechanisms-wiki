No. 12: Single fixed pulley

A rope runs over a wheel hung from a beam. Pulling one end down lifts a weight on the other end. It does not make the weight any lighter, but it lets you pull down instead of lifting up.

How it works

The rope goes up from the weight, over the wheel and down to the winch. When the winch pulls in some rope, the weight rises by exactly the same amount. The red mark and the weight move together.

Only one rope holds the weight up. So the winch must pull as hard as the weight pulls down. A 50 kg weight needs a 50 kg pull. The pulley only turns the pull round, from up to down.

That still helps. Pulling down is easier than lifting up, because you can lean back and use your own weight. Watch the two readouts: the rope pulled and the weight raised always match.

In Brown's words

Simple pulley used for lifting weights. In this the power must be equal to the weight to obtain equilibrium.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows a hand pulling the rope; a small winch was added in its place. The sizes are made up. A real pulley rubs a little on its pin, so a real lift needs a bit more pull than shown here.
