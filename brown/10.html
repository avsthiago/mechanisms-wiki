No. 10: Curved cone pulleys

Two curved cones point opposite ways and are joined by one belt. The upper one is hollowed in, the lower one bulges out. Sliding the belt along changes the lower speed smoothly, and the belt stays just as tight everywhere.

How it works

The upper cone turns at a steady speed and pulls the belt. The belt turns the lower cone the same way. Wherever the upper cone is thick, the lower cone is thin.

Each turn of a cone moves the belt once round the cone. On Fast, the belt sits where the upper cone is thick and the lower cone is thin, so the lower cone turns faster. On Slow it turns slower.

A belt that is not crossed needs a little extra length between a big and a small pulley. So the two sizes cannot simply add up to the same amount everywhere. In the middle, where the cones are closest in size, they must be a little fatter. That is why the sides curve. Try Slow, Middle and Fast. The belt stays just as tight at every place.

In Brown's words

Is a modification of 9, the pulleys being of different shape.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing and description. The sizes and the frame are made up, and the upper cone is taken as the driver. Brown does not give the curves. Here the upper curve was chosen to look like his drawing. The lower curve was then worked out from the exact length of an open belt, so the belt is the same length at every place. Its bulge came out of that sum, not from tracing the drawing. Real cone drives have a fork to push the belt along; it is left out.
