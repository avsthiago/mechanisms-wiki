No. 272: Bevelled disc cam

A thick disc turns on a shaft. One face of the disc is wavy, and its rim slopes. A rod lies along the slope with its end against the wavy face. As the disc turns, the rod is pushed back and then slides down again. This turns a steady spin into a push and pull.

How it works

Look at the left face of the disc. It is not flat. It bulges out in two places and dips in two places. The rim between the two faces slopes, like the side of a cone.

The rod lies along the top of the sloping rim, and its end rests against the wavy face. When a bulge comes round to the top, it pushes the rod back up its guides. When a dip comes round, the rod slides down again under its own weight. One turn of the disc gives two pushes.

Try shallow waves, then deep ones. The deeper the waves, the farther the rod is pushed. Watch the red band at the end of the rod.

In Brown's words

Rotary motion of the beveled disk cam gives a reciprocating rectilinear motion to the rod bearing on its circumference.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows the wavy face from the side only, so the shape of the waves, two to a turn, was made up. The base, the two uprights, the posts for the guides and the sizes were also made up. The rod is kept on the disc by its own weight, as it slopes down towards it.
