No. 338: Parallel motion with the radius bar above

The end of a rocking beam moves on a curve, but a piston rod must go straight up and down. Here a short rod is pinned to the beam end, and a radius bar above the beam holds its top. The bottom of the short rod then goes up and down almost straight.

How it works

The end of the beam swings on a curve. At the top and bottom of its swing it moves to the left, toward the pivot. The short rod goes with it, but the radius bar holds its top on a curve of its own.

The radius bar is shorter than the beam, so its curve is tighter. That tilts the short rod. At the top and bottom of the swing the bottom of the rod swings out to the right. This just makes up for the beam end moving left. So the bottom of the rod goes up and down almost straight, and that is where the piston rod hangs.

The line is only nearly straight. Look at the Off line reading. Try Small, then Large, and watch the dark path open into a thin figure of eight. Choose Closed to see the cylinder as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Another modification, in which the radius bar is placed above the beam.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The cylinder, the piston, the posts and bearings, the floor and all sizes were made up. The beam is rocked to and fro as if by a crank on its far end, which is left out. The radius bar is set behind the beam so that its fixed pin is clear of the beam. The bottom of the short rod was placed where the line comes out straightest for these lengths.
