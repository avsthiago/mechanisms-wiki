No. 225: Ratchet wheel and swinging pawl

An arm on a tall post swings up and down. A small finger on its end, called a pawl, pushes a toothed wheel round a little on every downward swing. So a to-and-fro swing becomes turning that only goes one way, in steps.

How it works

The arm swings down, then up, again and again. A small finger called a pawl is hinged to its end. It rests in the gap between two teeth of the wheel.

Going down, the pawl pushes against the steep side of a tooth and carries the wheel round with it. Going up, it meets the sloping side of the next tooth. It lifts, slides over the top and drops into the next gap. The wheel stays where it is. So the wheel only ever turns one way.

Try the Tiny swing. The pawl rises and falls in the same gap and the wheel does not move. Then try Wide. The pawl now passes two teeth on every swing and the wheel moves on two teeth each time.

In Brown's words

Intermittent circular motion of the ratchet-wheel from vibratory motion of the arm carrying a pawl.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. In the drawing the pawl is the shaped end of the arm itself. Here it is a separate finger hinged to the arm, so that it can lift over the teeth. Its own weight keeps it down. The base, the plate behind the wheel, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. A little rubbing in the wheel's shaft stops it slipping back.
