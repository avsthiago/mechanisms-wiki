No. 269: Part-toothed racks turning a gear

A frame slides steadily one way, then back. It has short toothed bars along its top and bottom edges, with smooth stretches between them. A gear inside the frame turns one way when a top bar pushes it and the other way when a bottom bar does.

How it works

The frame slides along at a steady pace. A toothed bar on the top edge meets the gear and pushes its top to one side, so the gear turns one way. When that bar runs out, the gear stops.

Next, a toothed bar on the bottom edge reaches the gear. It pushes the bottom of the gear in the same direction as the frame moves, so the gear now turns the other way. The frame never changes its pace. Only the bar that touches the gear changes.

Watch the red stripe. It turns, stops, then turns back, over and over. The bars sit far enough apart that the top and bottom never touch the gear together. That would lock it.

In Brown's words

Continued rectilinear movement of the frame with mutilated racks gives an alternate rotary motion to the spur-gear.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The gear, the toothed bars and the frame are as drawn. The bars are spaced wider apart than in the drawing, so that the top and bottom never touch the gear together. The plate behind, the shelf under the frame, the handle length, the tooth sizes and the length of the stroke were made up.
