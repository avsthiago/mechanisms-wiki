No. 415: Dickson's two-pawl lever

A lever rocks to and fro inside a wheel and turns it a step at a time. Two small hooks, called pawls, on the lever press on the inside of the rim, one for each direction. A small crank lifts one and lets the other down, so the wheel can be sent either way.

How it works

The crank rocks the lever over and back. The pawl that is down presses its end on the inside of the rim. On one stroke the wheel goes round with the lever. On the way back the wheel stays still and the pawl slides over the rim.

The pawl stands almost straight out from the shaft, leaning a little. When the lever swings the way that would stand it up straighter, it would have to get longer to fit, so it jams hard against the rim and drags the wheel along. Swing it the other way and it tilts back and slides. Each pawl leans the opposite way, so each one drives the wheel in its own direction.

Try Anticlockwise, then Clockwise. Watch the small crank on the lever swing over: its cords lift one pawl and let the other down, and the steps of the wheel change direction.

In Brown's words

P. Dickson's patent device for converting an oscillating motion into intermittent circular, in either direction. Oscillating motion communicated to lever, A, which is provided with two pawls, B and C, hinged to its upper side, near shaft of wheel, D. Small crank, E, on upper side or lever, A, is attached by cord to each of pawls, so that when pawl, C, is let into contact with interior of rim of wheel, D, it moves in one direction, and pawl, B, is out of gear. Motion of wheel, D, may be reversed by lifting pawl, C, which was in gear, and letting opposite one into gear by crank, E.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The spokes, the crank and rod that rock the lever, the back plate, the sizes and the lean of the pawls were made up. Brown's cord runs from one pawl over the small crank to the other; here it is shown as two cords from the crank pin. The rim is smooth, as Brown says, so the pawls grip it by rubbing; the wheel is assumed to stay put while the lever swings back.
