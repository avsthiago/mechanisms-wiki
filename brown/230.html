No. 230: Two cranks at right angles

Two shafts turn together because long links join their cranks. There are two pairs of links, set a quarter turn apart. When one pair has no pull, the other pair has its full pull, so the shafts never stick.

How it works

Watch the two long links. The cranks are the same length and the links are as long as the gap between the shafts. So each link keeps upright as it goes round, and the lower shaft turns exactly with the upper one.

A link cannot turn a crank when the crank points straight along it. That is a dead point. The far crank and the near disc are set a quarter turn apart. When one link is at a dead point, the other is pulling with its full strength. The readouts show how hard each link is able to pull.

Try the angle slider. At right angles the two readouts take turns, and one is always strong. Set the angle to the same and both fall to nothing together. That is why Brown needs no heavy wheel to carry the shafts past the dead point.

In Brown's words

Circular motion into ditto. The connecting-rods are so arranged that when one pair of connected links is over the dead point, or at the extremity of its stroke, the other is at right angles; continuous motion is thus insured without a fly-wheel.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown draws the shafts in the air, so no bearings are drawn. The two long links are shown as flat bars in front of the cranks and the discs. All sizes are made up. The pull readouts show how much of each crank is sideways to its link, as a share of its best. Brown shows right angles only, and the slider is added.
