No. 150: Expansion cams for a valve

Four cams of different size sit side by side on one turning shaft. Slide the shaft's cam stack along, and a different cam sits under the lever. A bigger cam lifts the lever higher, so the valve rod moves further.

How it works

The shaft turns. Each cam turns with it, and each has one bulge. When a bulge comes round to the top, it pushes the left end of the lever up. When the bulge has passed, the lever drops back.

The valve rod hangs from the middle of the lever, so it moves up and down with the lever, but only about half as far. Only the cam that is lined up with the lever matters. The others turn past beside it without touching.

Try Smallest, then Biggest. Watch the cam stack slide, then watch the lever and the rod. A bigger bulge gives a longer stroke.

In Brown's words

A valve motion for working steam expansively. The series of cams of varying throw are movable lengthwise of the shaft so that either may be made to act upon the lever to which the valve-rod is connected. A greater or less movement of the valve is produced, according as a cam of greater or less throw is opposite the lever.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The number of cams, their sizes and the shape of each bulge were made up. The lever's pin post, the guide for the rod, the bearing wall behind the shaft and the base were added to hold it up. Brown draws the lever from the side. Here the lever's end is a small pointed foot, and the lever height is worked out exactly from the edge of the cam under it. When a big bulge rises beside the foot, it lifts the lever by its bar. The long key along the shaft is drawn to show how the cams slide yet turn with the shaft.
