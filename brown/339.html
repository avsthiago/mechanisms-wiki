No. 339: Parallel motion with a sliding end

In an engine with no beam, the piston rod pushes a crank above it. Its top must still go straight up and down. Here one end of a bar slides in a straight slot, and a radius bar holds the bar's middle. The other end of the bar then moves in a perfectly straight line.

How it works

The sliding block keeps one end of the yellow bar on a straight line across. The radius bar is pinned to the middle of the yellow bar. It is exactly half as long as the yellow bar, and its fixed pin sits on the line of the slot.

So the fixed pin, the sliding end and the piston end are all the same distance from the middle pin. They lie on a circle round it. The slot passes through the fixed pin, so the piston end must stay on the upright line through that pin. It is not nearly straight, but truly straight.

Look at the Off line reading. As drawn, it stays at zero. Try Shorter or Longer. Now the red pin runs to one side of the fixed pin, on a line that is no longer quite straight. The piston rod has to lean to follow it. Choose Closed to see the cylinder as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Parallel motion for direct action engines. In this, the end of the bar, B, C, is connected with the piston-rod, and the end, B, slides in a fixed slot, D. The radius bar, F, A, is connected at F with a fixed pivot, and at A, midway between the ends of B, C.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The frame is drawn flat and see-through, and the crank, cylinder and all sizes were made up to fit the drawing. The fixed pin sits a little above the top of the stroke, so the parts never stack on it. The other radius bar lengths were added for comparison.
