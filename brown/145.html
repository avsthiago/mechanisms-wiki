No. 145: Beam, crank and flywheel

A beam rocks up and down, and through a chain of rods it makes a crank and heavy wheel spin round and round. A short lever on a small post keeps the joint in the chain on a steady path. This is how a rocking motion becomes a smooth turning one.

How it works

The beam rocks. Its left end moves a thin rod up and down. The rod moves a joint, and a long bar from that joint pulls the crank pin round the wheel's shaft.

The joint is also held by a short lever on a small post. The lever makes the joint swing on a small arc, so the long bar always pushes the crank in a sensible direction. The crank goes right round, and the heavy wheel carries it past the stiff spots.

Try a short crank arm, then the long one. A longer arm makes the joint swing higher and the beam rock further. Watch the tilt readings.

In Brown's words

Reciprocating curvilinear motion of the beam gives a continuous rotary motion to the crank and fly-wheel. The small standard at the left, to which is attached one end of the lever with which the beam is connected by the connecting-rod, has a horizontal reciprocating rectilinear movement.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown draws the lever flat. Here it tilts up a little at the start so the rods can turn the crank all the way round. The post for the lever, the post for the beam pivot, the base, the bearing behind the wheel, the shaft, the pins and all the sizes were made up. The shorter crank arms were added for comparison.
