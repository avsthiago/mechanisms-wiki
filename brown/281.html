No. 281: Disc groove and lever

A flat disc has a closed groove cut into its face. A pin on a long swinging arm rides in the groove. As the disc turns, the groove pushes the pin in and out, and the arm swings to and fro.

How it works

The pin on the arm sits in the groove and cannot leave it. As the disc turns, the groove carries the pin along with it.

Where the groove is far from the middle of the disc, the pin is pushed outwards and the arm swings one way. Where the groove comes close to the middle, the pin is drawn inwards and the arm swings back. One turn of the disc gives one swing out and back. The groove has two walls, so it pushes the pin both ways and no spring is needed.

Slow the disc down with the speed slider. Follow the red pin round the groove and see where the arm is at its furthest left and furthest right.

In Brown's words

The revolution of the disk causes the lever at the right to vibrate by the pin moving in the groove in the face of the disk.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The shape of the groove was worked out from the arm's swing, so the pin always touches both walls. The stand, the base, the post that holds the arm's pivot and all the sizes were made up.
