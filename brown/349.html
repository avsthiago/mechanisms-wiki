No. 349: Jointed-arm parallel ruler

Two rulers are joined by jointed arms that bend in the middle, and a bar rides between them. When the rulers open or close, their edges and their ends all stay parallel. A pencil along the edge draws lines that never meet.

How it works

Each pair of arms is pinned to both rulers and to the middle bar. The two arms are the same length, so the bar always sits halfway between the rulers. The rulers cannot twist, because both pairs of arms bend the same amount.

When the rulers close, the arms bend more and the bar slides sideways. When they open, the arms straighten and the bar slides back. The rulers themselves only move up and down, so every pencil line is parallel to the last.

Try Close spacing, then Wide. Watch the bar slide sideways while the rulers stay in line.

In Brown's words

Another form of parallel ruler. The arms are jointed in the middle and connected with an intermediate bar, by which means the ends of the ruler, as well as the sides, are kept parallel.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The sheet of paper and the pencil were added to show the lines. The lengths, thicknesses and pin positions are made up. In real use you hold one ruler and push the other; here the lower one stays still.
