No. 31: Worm and worm wheel

A screw on a level shaft turns a toothed wheel just below it. Each turn of the screw moves the wheel on by only one tooth. It turns a fast shaft into a very slow and strong one, at right angles to it.

How it works

Turn the crank and the screw, called a worm, spins on its shaft. Its thread seems to slide sideways along the shaft. The thread sits in a gap between two teeth of the wheel, so it pushes a tooth along.

In one turn of the worm, the thread moves along by the gap from one thread to the next. That gap is the same as the gap from one tooth to the next. So one turn moves the wheel on by one tooth. The wheel cannot turn the worm back, because pushing a tooth only jams it against the thread.

Some worms have two threads wound side by side. Then each turn moves the wheel on by two teeth, so it goes twice as fast. Try One thread, then Two threads, and watch the red tooth.

In Brown's words

Worm or endless screw and a worm-wheel. This effects the same result as 29; and as it is more easily constructed, it is oftener used.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The crank, the plate and bearings that hold the shafts, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The wheel has plain straight teeth, as drawn. A real worm wheel has slanted, hollowed teeth so the thread presses along the whole tooth. The worm with two threads was added for comparison.
