No. 351: Stamp with a part-toothed pinion

A small gear, called a pinion, with teeth on only part of its rim lifts an upright rod that has a row of teeth. When the last tooth lets go, the rod drops by its own weight and hits the base. Then the gear comes round and lifts it again.

How it works

The gear turns steadily. While its teeth pass the rod, they hook into the rod's teeth and push the rod up. The rod rises at the same speed as the rim of the gear.

Then the last tooth lets go. The smooth part of the rim cannot hold the rod, so nothing holds it up. It drops by its own weight and the head hits the base. It rests there until the first tooth comes round again and drops into the same gap.

The more teeth the gear has, the higher the rod goes and the harder the blow. Try 3 teeth, then 7, and watch how far the rod falls.

In Brown's words

Stamp. Vertical percussive falls derived from horizontal rotating shaft. The mutilated toothed pinion acts upon the rack to raise the rod until its teeth leave the rack and allow the rod to fall.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The two guides, the back plate, the base and the sizes were made up. The head is shown six-sided as in the drawing. The rod falls only under its own weight, as in the book, with no spring. The choice of 3 or 5 teeth was added for comparison.
