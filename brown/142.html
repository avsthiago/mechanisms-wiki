No. 142: Changing the stroke of a guide bar

A big disc carries a gear round a small fixed gear, called a pinion. The gear is made to spin as it travels, so a crank pin on it moves closer to the centre, then farther away. A rod joins the pin to a bar that slides up and down, and the bar's stroke grows and shrinks. Silk machines use this to lay thread evenly on a bobbin.

How it works

Turn the disc and the big gear is carried round the small pinion. The pinion cannot turn, so the teeth of the big gear roll round it. That makes the big gear spin on its own stud as well.

The crank pin is on the big gear. When the spin brings the pin near the centre of the disc, the bar only moves a short way up and down. When the spin carries the pin far from the centre, the bar moves a long way. The stroke changes a little on every turn of the disc.

Try the small gear, then the big one. Watch the stroke reading. A small gear spins faster as it goes round, so the stroke changes faster.

In Brown's words

Movement used for varying the length of the traversing guide-bar which, in silk machinery, guides the silk on to spools or bobbins. The spur-gear, turning freely on its center, is carried round by the larger circular disk, which turns on a fixed central stud, which has a pinion fast on its end. Upon the spur-gear is bolted a small crank, to which is jointed a connecting-rod attached to traversing guide-bar. On turning the disk, the spur-gear is made to rotate partly upon its center by means of the fixed pinion, and consequently brings crank nearer to center of disk. If the rotation of disk was continued, the spur-gear would make an entire revolution. During half a revolution the traverse would have been shortened a certain amount at every revolution of disk, according to the size of spur-gear; and during the other half it would have gradually lengthened in the same ratio.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows no frame. The back plate, the base, the shaft, the handle, the guide block, the rod and bar lengths, the stud and bolt heads, the sizes and the tooth counts were made up. The drawing's dotted ring on the disc was left out. The choice of gear size was added to show what it changes.
