No. 487: Paddle wheel

A big wheel on the side of a boat, with flat boards round its edge. As it turns, the lowest boards push the water backwards. The water pushes back on the boards, and that drives the boat forwards.

How it works

The wheel turns clockwise in this picture. Only the boards at the bottom are in the water, and they move to the left, towards the back of the boat. Watch the dots: the boards shove the water backwards.

Pushing water one way pushes the wheel the other way, like pushing off from a wall. So the boat moves forwards, to the right. The water in front of the wheel seems to stream past, because the boat is moving through it.

Try tying the boat up. Now it cannot move, so the boards hit still water at full speed and throw it back hard. Set it free again. The boat speeds up until the push of the boards matches the drag of the water on the hull.

In Brown's words

Common paddle-wheel for propelling vessels ; the revolution of the wheel causes the buckets to press backward against the water and so produce the forward movement of the vessel.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The water, the side of the boat and the depth of the wheel were made up. The boat speed comes from a simple rule: the push grows with how much faster the boards move than the boat.
