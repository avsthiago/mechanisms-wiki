No. 462: Chain pump

An endless chain carries a row of discs up through a pipe. Each disc fits the pipe, so it lifts the water resting on it like a small bucket. At the top the water spills out. Turning the upper wheel keeps the chain going round.

How it works

Look at the chain. It is one endless loop that runs over a wheel at the top and a wheel at the bottom. Round discs are fixed on it, all the same distance apart. The left side goes up through the green pipe.

Each disc fits the pipe closely, so water cannot run back past it. The water between two discs is trapped and rides up with them, shown as blue dots. When a disc comes out of the top of the pipe, its water spills into the spout and runs to the trough.

The discs come down the right side empty, dip under the water and go round again. The crank only has to keep the upper wheel turning. Follow the red disc all the way round. Then move the speed slider and see more water come out. Choose Closed to see the pipe as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Chain pump ; lifting water by continuous circular motion. Wood or metal disks, carried by endless chain, are adapted to water-tight cylinder, and form with it a succession of buckets filled with water. Power is applied at upper wheel.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The pipe is cut open at the front, and the water is drawn as small blue balls so you can see it move. Brown says only that power is applied at the upper wheel, so the crank was made up. The forked arms of the wheels, the shape of the spout, the tank, the trough, the stand and all the sizes were also made up. Inside the pipe the chain is drawn straight. Outside it sways a little as each pin leaves a wheel.
