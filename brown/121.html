No. 121: Pawl and ratchet wheel

A rod moves up and down, and a ratchet wheel turns in small steps. A finger on the rocking disc, called a pawl, pushes the teeth round. Throw the pawl over and the wheel turns the other way.

How it works

The crank at the top moves the rod up and down. The rod rocks the big disc a short way to the left, then back to the right. The pawl is fixed to the disc, so it rocks with it.

On one swing the pawl catches the side of a tooth and pushes the ratchet wheel round by one tooth. On the other swing it slides up the other side of the tooth and drops into the next gap. The wheel stands still while that happens, so it moves in steps.

To turn the wheel the other way, throw the pawl over. It then catches the other side of the teeth. Try Forwards, then Backwards, and watch the red tooth. Planing machines use this to feed the work a little at a time.

In Brown's words

Alternating rectilinear motion of the rod attached to the disk-wheel produces an intermittent rotary motion of the cog-wheel by means of the click attached to the disk-wheel. This motion, which is reversible by throwing over the click, is used for the feed of planing machines and other tools.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows the rod cut off at the top, so a small crank was added to move it. The rod end is drawn as a simple eye. The plate, the base, the pins, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The pawl is a straight finger instead of Brown's curved one. While it is thrown over, the machine waits.
