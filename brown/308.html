No. 308: Detached pendulum escapement

Here the pendulum swings almost free. A lever holds the escape wheel still. Once in every swing to the left, a small finger on the pendulum, called a pawl, trips the lever, and one tooth gives the pendulum a single push.

How it works

The wheel tries to turn clockwise, but the stop on the lever sits in front of the top tooth and holds it. The pendulum swings to and fro below, and for most of the time it touches nothing.

As the pendulum swings to the left, the pawl meets the tail of the lever and pushes it aside. That lifts the stop and the wheel is free. A tooth at the bottom of the wheel runs into the right end of the pallet and pushes the pendulum to the left, the way it is already going. Then the pawl slips out from under the tail, the lever drops back, and the stop catches the next tooth.

On the swing back to the right the pawl only tips over and passes under the tail. Nothing is let go, so the pendulum gets one push for every two beats. Its length sets the pace. Try Short, then Long, and count the beats.

In Brown's words

A detached pendulum escapement, leaving the pendulum, P, free or detached from the escape-wheel, except at the time of receiving the impulse and unlocking the wheel. There is but one pallet, I, which receives impulse only during the vibrations of the pendulum to the left. The lever, Q, locks the escape-wheel until just before the time for giving the impulse, when it is unlocked by the click, C, attached to the pendulum. As the pendulum returns to the right, the click, which oscillates on a pivot, will be pushed aside by the lever.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing and description. The top of the pendulum, the bob, the back plate, the pin the lever rests on and the sizes were made up. The exact shape of the lever's short arm and stop is a guess, because the drawing is hard to read there. Of the two small pins beside the pawl in the drawing, only the one it rests on is shown. The pawl is taken to be so light that carrying it costs the pendulum nothing. The weight that turns the wheel is left out and its pull is taken to be steady. The parts slide on each other without rubbing, and only the pendulum and the wheel are slowed a little.
