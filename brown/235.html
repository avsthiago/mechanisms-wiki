No. 235: Tappet arm and ratchet wheel

A long arm swings up and down. A hinged hook on its end, called a tappet, catches a tooth of a star-shaped wheel and pushes it round as the arm rises. On the way down the hook folds out of the way. So the wheel only ever turns one way.

How it works

The long arm swings up, then down. A hook called the tappet is hinged to its left end. The hook points up at the star wheel, and a small spring holds it out.

Going up, the tip of the hook pushes against the steep side of a tooth and carries the wheel round. Coming down, it meets the sloping side of the next tooth. The slope pushes the hook back and the spring squeezes. The hook slides over the top, then springs out again. A curved finger on a fixed pin stops the wheel from turning back.

Try the Small swing. The hook never reaches the steep side of a tooth, so the wheel does not move at all. Then try Medium. The wheel moves on exactly one tooth for each swing, however far the arm goes.

In Brown's words

The oscillation of the tappet-arm produces an intermittent rotary motion of the ratchet-wheel. The small spring at the bottom of the tappet-arm keeps the tappet in the position shown in the drawing as the arm rises, yet allows it to pass the teeth on the return motion.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The drawing shows the tappet and the holding pawl as curved shapes. Here they are straight pieces. The plate behind the wheel, the pins, the sizes, and the shape of the star were made up. In the drawing the spring is a thin wire. Here it is a coil.
