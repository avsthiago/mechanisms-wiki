No. 288: Recoil escapement

A weight tries to spin the toothed wheel of a clock. A rocking piece shaped like an anchor lets only one tooth pass for each swing of the pendulum. Each time it catches a tooth it also pushes the wheel back a little, which is why it is called a recoil escapement.

How it works

The weight tries to turn the wheel anticlockwise. A tooth runs up against the slanted face of one pallet and stops. The wheel cannot go on until the pendulum swings that pallet out of the way.

The tooth presses on the slanted face, and that press pushes the pallet outward. This small push at every swing is what keeps the pendulum going. When the pallet lifts clear, the tooth slips past and the other pallet drops in to catch a tooth on the far side. But the pendulum does not stop at once. It carries the pallet a little deeper, and the slanted face pushes the tooth backward. That backward step is the recoil. Watch the red tooth and the Recoil number.

The pendulum sets the pace. A long pendulum swings more slowly, so the beats come further apart. Try Short, then Long, and count the beats.

In Brown's words

The former is what is termed a recoil, and the latter a repose or dead-beat escapement for clocks. The same letters of reference indicate like parts in both. The anchor, H, L, K, is caused, by the oscillation of the pendulum, to vibrate upon the axis, a. Between the two extremities, or pallets, H, K, is placed the escape-wheel, A, the teeth of which come alternately against the outer surface of the pallet, K, and inner surface of pallet, H. In 289 these surfaces are cut to a curve concentric to the axis, a ; consequently, during the time one of the teeth is against the pallet the wheel remains perfectly at rest. Hence the name repose or dead-beat. In 288 the surfaces are of a differeent form, not necessary to explain, as it can be understood that any form not concentric with the axis, a, must produce a slight recoil of the wheel during the escape of the tooth, and hence the term recoil escapement. On the pallets leaving teeth, at each oscillation of the pendulum, the extremities of teeth slide along the surfaces, c, e, and d, b, and give sufficient impulse to pendulum.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing, which shows the top of the wheel and the anchor. The rest of the wheel, the pendulum, the back plate and the sizes were made up. Brown says the shape of the pallet faces is not necessary to explain, so they are plain straight slopes here. The pendulum hangs in front so that it can be seen. The weight that turns the wheel is left out and its pull is taken to be steady. The teeth slide on the pallets without rubbing, and only the pendulum and the wheel are slowed a little.
