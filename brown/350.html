No. 350: Lever traverse motion

A long lever with a slot at each end swings about a pin that rides on a bar. One slot holds a pin that stays still. The other holds a pin that moves from side to side. The lever then slides the bar to and fro in its guides.

How it works

Watch the lever. Its upper slot slides over the fixed pin, so the lever can only lean about that pin. The moving pin in the lower slot pushes the bottom of the lever left and right, and the lever leans with it.

The middle of the lever is pinned to a post on the bar. When the lever leans, that pin is carried sideways, and it takes the bar with it. The bar is closer to the fixed pin than the moving pin is, so it moves much less than the moving pin does.

Try the three settings of the slider. A longer reach makes the lever lean more and the bar travel farther, but the bar always moves less than the moving pin.

In Brown's words

Traverse or to-and-fro motion. The pin in the upper slot being stationary, and the one in the lower slot made to move in the direction of the horizontal dotted line, the lever will by its connection with the bar give to the latter a traversing motion in its guides, a, a.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows no drive for the lower pin, so a slide on a rail that moves back and forth by itself was added. The wall, the rail, the guides, the post on the bar and all the sizes were made up. The post is cut shorter than in the drawing so that it clears the fixed pin.
