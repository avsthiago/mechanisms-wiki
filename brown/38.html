No. 38: Part steady, part varied rotation

Two equal gears turn each other at the same speed for most of a turn. Then their teeth stop, and two pairs of toothed arms take over. One pair makes the second gear hurry and the other makes it dawdle, while the first gear keeps turning steadily.

How it works

The left gear turns steadily. For most of the turn its teeth push the teeth of the right gear. The gears are the same size, so the right one turns at the same speed, the other way. A minus sign in the readout means the other way.

Each gear has a slice cut out, so for a while the big teeth do not meet. Toothed arms fill the cut. First a long arm on the left pushes a short arm on the right. The tip of a long arm travels far, so the short arm is whirled round and the right gear hurries. Later a short arm on the left pushes a long arm on the right, and the right gear dawdles.

The hurry and the dawdle cancel out, so both gears still finish each turn together. Try As drawn, then Closer in size, and watch how much the Driven speed changes.

In Brown's words

A means of converting rotary motion, by which the speed is made uniform during a part, and varied during another part, of the revolution.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows only the two cut wheels and the toothed arms between their centres. The plate, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. A tooth or two is left out at the end of some rows, so that teeth do not hit each other when the speed changes. In a real machine these changes of speed would come as sharp knocks. The choice of arm sizes was added to show what it changes.
