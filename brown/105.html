No. 105: Screw press

A big screw turns in a fixed nut and slides down as it turns. It carries a heavy block, called the ram, down onto a blank of metal and squeezes it. Presses like this stamp coins and medals.

How it works

Push the bar round. The screw turns with it. Its thread runs in a nut fixed in the frame, so the screw cannot just spin. It has to travel down the nut, one step of the thread for each turn.

The ram is on the foot of the screw, so it goes straight down without turning. It stops on the blank and squeezes it. Then the bar is swung back and the screw lifts the ram up again. The bar is long, so a small push on the balls gives a very large push on the blank.

The ram always goes down the same distance. Try Fine, then Coarse. Count the turns the screw needs each time, and look at how the thread is spaced.

In Brown's words

Screw stamping-press. Rectilinear motion from circular motion.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The frame, the base, the bed and the blank, the sizes and the thread spacing were made up. Brown draws the bar swinging back and forth; here it turns round and back so the turns can be counted. The coarse thread has two threads side by side. A guide in the frame was added so the ram stays upright.
