No. 251: Pile-driver releasing hook

A heavy weight is pulled up a tall frame by two hooks. At the top, the hooks let go by themselves and the weight drops onto a post. Each blow drives the post a little deeper into the ground.

How it works

The winch pulls the hook block up, and the weight hangs from its two hooks. Near the top, the upper ends of the hooks run into the slot in the frame. The slot gets narrower as they rise, so it squeezes them together.

Each hook turns on a pin, like a see-saw. When its upper end is pushed in, its lower end swings out. Once the lower ends clear the head of the weight, nothing holds it, and it falls onto the post.

Then the winch lets the block down. The lower ends of the hooks slide over the head and snap back under it, ready for the next lift. Watch the readouts: the post sinks less at each blow, because the ground holds it more firmly the deeper it goes.

In Brown's words

Releasing-hook, used in pile-driving machines. When the weight, W, is sufficiently raised, the upper ends of the hooks, A, by which it is suspended, are pressed inward by the sides of the slot, B, in top of the frame; the weight is thus suddenly released, and falls with accumulating force on to the pile-head.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The rope wheel, the winch, the ground and the post were added to show where the rope goes and what the weight strikes. The shapes of the hooks and the head, the sizes, and how far the post sinks at each blow are made up. The hooks are taken to fall shut by their own weight.
