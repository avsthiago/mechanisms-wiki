No. 76: Revolution counter tappet

A stud on a big wheel gives a lever a knock once every turn. The lever lifts a jointed finger, which moves a toothed wheel on by one tooth. So the toothed wheel counts the turns of the big wheel.

How it works

The big wheel turns slowly. Once a turn its stud comes down onto the right end of the lever and pushes it down. The lever turns on its pin, so its left end goes up.

The finger hinged to the left end hooks under a tooth and lifts it, so the counting wheel turns a little. Then the stud slides off the end of the lever. The heavier left end falls back by its own weight. On the way down the finger folds back over the next tooth and drops under it. The holding pawl holds the counting wheel still meanwhile.

Count the turns of the big wheel and the teeth of the counting wheel. They stay the same. Try 2 studs. Now the wheel moves on two teeth for each turn.

In Brown's words

Is another contrivance for registering or counting revolutions. A tappet, B, supported on the fixed pivot, C, is struck at every revolution of the large wheel (partly represented) by a stud, D, attached to the said wheel. This causes the end of the tappet next the ratchet-wheel, A, to be lifted, and to turn the wheel the distance of one tooth. The tappet returns by its own weight to its original position after the stud, D, has passed, the end, being jointed to permit it to pass the teeth of the ratchet-wheel.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The lever is drawn as one straight bar instead of Brown's shaped one, and the big wheel is shown whole but smaller. The plates, the post, the stop pin under the lever, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The lever falls freely with no bounce.
