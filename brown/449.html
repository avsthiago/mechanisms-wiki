No. 449: Lifting pump with a flap valve

A lift pump with a closed top. The piston rod slides out through a tight collar, and the water leaves by a side pipe with a hinged flap in it. Because nothing can leak back, the pipe can carry the water up as high as you like.

How it works

Watch the piston go up. Its lid is pressed shut, so it pushes all the water above it out of the side pipe. The flap in that pipe swings open to let it pass. Below the piston, the flap in the floor opens and water from the well follows the piston up.

On the way down, both flaps fall shut. The flap in the pipe holds up all the water above it, so none runs back. The lid on the piston lifts, and the piston slides down through the water ready for the next stroke.

The tube is closed at the top and the flap holds the water in the pipe, so the pipe can climb as high as needed. Try pausing in the middle of each stroke and see which valves are open. Choose Closed to see the pump as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Modern lifting pump. This pump operates in same manner as one in previous figure, except that piston-rod passes through stuffing-box, and outlet is closed by a flap-valve opening upward. Water can be lifted to any height above this pump.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing, which is a cut-away. The front half of the pump is left off so you can see in. The handle is not in Brown's drawing, so a plain grip on the rod stands in for it. The sizes, the shape of the valves and the made-up size used for the litres are not from the book. The pipes are cut short, and the model starts with them full of water.
