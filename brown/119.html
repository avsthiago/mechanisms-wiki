No. 119: Endless rack

A small gear turns steadily one way, yet the long bar beside it slides to the right and then back to the left. The gear runs along the top of the bar, round its end and back along the bottom. This is a way to turn a steady turning into a back and forth push.

How it works

Turn the crank and the small gear, called a pinion, turns with it. It meshes with the teeth along the top of the rack. Along the top it pushes the rack one way.

At the end of the rack the pinion rolls round the curved end. Its shaft slides down the slot to the underside. Along the bottom the same turning pushes the rack the other way. The pinion never changes direction, but the rack goes out and comes back. A rod out of each end keeps it moving in a straight line.

Try Anticlockwise, then Clockwise. See which way the bar moves while the pinion is on the top run.

In Brown's words

Reciprocating rectilinear motion of the bar carrying the oblong endless rack, produced by the uniform rotary motion of the pinion working alternately above and below the rack. The shaft of the pinion moves up and down in, and is guided by, the slotted bar.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The crank, the two posts, the top beam, the base and all the sizes and numbers of teeth were made up. The two holes Brown draws in the rack were left out. The pinion's shaft is held only by the slot, as Brown describes. The teeth round the curved ends are spaced evenly along the loop, so they mesh well but are not exact gear teeth.
