No. 224: Expanding pulley

A belt pulley that can be made bigger or smaller without taking it off its shaft. Its rim is cut into six pieces on sliding arms. Turning a small gear pushes all six pieces out together, or pulls them in.

How it works

Turn the small gear and it turns the big slotted gear the other way. The arms cannot turn with the gear, because each one is held in a straight guide. They can only slide in and out.

Each slot curves outwards as it goes round. When the gear turns, the slot slides past the stud and pushes it further out, or lets it come in. All six slots are the same, so all six arms move together and the rim stays round.

A bigger pulley moves the belt faster at the same speed of turning. Try Smallest, then Largest, and watch the gaps in the rim open up.

In Brown's words

This represents an expanding pulley. On turning pinion, d, to the right or left, a similar motion is imparted to wheel, c, which, by means of curved slots cut therein, thrusts the studs fastened to arms of pulley outward or inward, thus augmenting or diminishing the size of the pulley.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The shaft, its bearing, the spokes behind the arms, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. Brown does not say how the small gear is turned; here it has a square end for a key. The pulley is shown turning so you can see that the whole thing goes round together.
