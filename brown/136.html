No. 136: Toothed cam wheel

A wheel has a ring of teeth cut into its flat face. A rod, pressed against the teeth by a spring, is pushed out by each tooth and drawn back between them. The wheel turns round, and the rod moves in and out in a straight line.

How it works

The wheel turns on its shaft. The rod's flat end rests on the ring of teeth. As a tooth comes round, its slope pushes the rod to the right. Then the tooth moves on, and the spring pushes the rod back to the left.

The rod goes out and back once for every tooth that passes it. The wheel has many teeth, so the rod makes many short strokes in each turn. Its position is simply the height of the tooth under its end.

The shape of the teeth decides how the rod moves. Try Pointed, then Rounded, then Saw. With Saw the rod is pushed out slowly, then drops back quickly.

In Brown's words

A cam-wheel — of which a side view is shown — has its rim formed into teeth, or made of any profile form desired. The rod to the right is made to press constantly against the teeth or edge of the rim. On turning the wheel, alternate rectilinear motion is communicated to the rod. The character of this motion may be varied by altering the shape of the teeth or profile of the edge of the rim of the wheel.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown draws the wheel from the side, so the teeth show as a zigzag edge. Here they are cut in the flat face of the wheel. The number and height of the teeth, the bearing and base, the wall with its hole, the collar on the rod and the marker ring were made up. Brown does not say what presses the rod on the teeth, so he draws a spring, as here. The other tooth shapes were added for comparison.
