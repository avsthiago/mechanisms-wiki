No. 34: Internal gear and pinion

A small gear turns inside a big ring that has its teeth on the inside. Both turn the same way. More teeth touch at once, so the drive can push harder without breaking a tooth.

How it works

The small gear, called a pinion, turns. Its teeth push the teeth on the inside of the ring, so the ring turns too. Both go the same way. A minus sign in the readout would mean the other way.

The ring curves round the pinion, so the two edges stay close for longer. That puts more teeth in contact at once. Each tooth carries less of the load, so teeth of the same size can pass on more force.

Try Inside ring, then Outside gear. The pinion and the big gear stay the same size and speed, but the outside gear turns the other way. Watch the red stripe.

In Brown's words

An internally toothed spur-gear and pinion. With ordinary spur-gears (such as represented in 24) the direction of rotation is opposite; but with the internally toothed gear, the two rotate in the same direction; and with the same strength of tooth the gears are capable of transmitting greater force, because more teeth are engaged.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows only the ring and the pinion. The plate, the three rollers that hold the ring, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The outside gear was added to compare the turning direction.
