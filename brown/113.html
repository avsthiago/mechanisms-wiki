No. 113: Rack and pinion

A small gear, called a pinion, turns against a straight bar with teeth, called a rack. When the gear turns, the bar slides along. When the bar is pushed, the gear turns. It changes turning into straight motion, or the other way round.

How it works

The gear turns one way and its teeth push the teeth of the rack. The rack and the table above it slide sideways. At the end of the swing the gear turns back, and the table slides back.

The gear and the rack move together, tooth for tooth. For every tooth that passes the meeting point, the table moves one tooth along. So a gear that turns once makes the table slide as far as the gear's rim is long. It works both ways. Push the table and the gear turns.

Try Small, then Large. The gear swings the same amount each time, but watch how far the red block on the table travels.

In Brown's words

Circular into rectilinear motion, or vice versa, by means of rack and pinion.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows the rack on a table that rests on two rollers, with the gear below. The upright plate and base, the way the gear swings back and forth, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The choice of gear size was added to show what it changes.
