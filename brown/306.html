No. 306: Three-legged escapement

A weight tries to spin a wheel with only three teeth. The wheel sits in an opening cut in a plate on the pendulum. Two steps in the edge of the opening take turns to stop a tooth, so the wheel moves one sixth of a turn for each swing.

How it works

The wheel tries to turn clockwise. A leg comes up on the left and its tip meets the curved edge of the upper pallet from below. It rests there and the wheel stands still. That edge is part of a circle round the pin of the pendulum, so the plate can slide past the tip without moving the wheel.

As the pendulum swings to the right, the corner of the pallet slides off the tip. The leg is free and runs into the upright side of the step. Now it pushes the plate to the right, the way the pendulum is already going. That push keeps the pendulum swinging. When the tip slips under the corner, the next leg lands on the lower pallet. On the swing back the same thing happens at the bottom, with the push to the left.

The pendulum sets the pace. A long pendulum swings more slowly, so the beats come further apart. Try Short, then Long, and count the beats.

In Brown's words

Three-legged pendulum escapement. The pallets are formed in an opening in a plate attached to the pendulum, and the three teeth of the escape-wheel operate on the upper and lower pallets alternately. One tooth is shown in operation on the upper pallet.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The top of the pendulum, the bob, the back plate and the sizes were made up. The pendulum here is much shorter than a real one would be, so the resting edges of the opening are plainly curved and the swing is wide. In the drawing they look straight. The weight that turns the wheel is left out and its pull is taken to be steady. The legs slide on the pallets without rubbing, and only the pendulum and the wheel are slowed a little.
