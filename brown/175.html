No. 175: One turn for each stroke

The piston of an engine pushes a rod to and fro. Each push, in or out, is one stroke. This linkage turns a shaft exactly once for every stroke. A plain crank would turn it only once for every two strokes.

How it works

The block goes up and down. The rod turns the big crank. Two gears behind the frame, one big and one small, join the big crank to the small red one. Look at them from the 3d view.

The big gear has twice as many teeth as the small one, so the small crank goes round twice for each turn of the big one. The big crank turns once for each two strokes. So the small red crank turns once for each stroke.

Watch the readouts. Each time the block reaches the top or the bottom, the stroke count goes up by one. The crank count goes up by one at the same time.

In Brown's words

A means of giving one complete revolution to the crank of an engine to each stroke of the piston.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows one crank, a dotted circle, a rod and a slot. He does not show how one turn per stroke is got. The two gears behind the frame, which double the turns, are made up. So are the small red crank, the base, the window panel and all the sizes. The slot is cut in the tower as drawn. The rod is longer and the slot closer than drawn, so that the rod can reach the pin all the way round.
