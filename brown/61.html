No. 61: Two speeds, one by differential gearing

One belt from a drum can turn a shaft at two speeds. On one pulley the shaft turns as fast as the drum. On the next, gears hidden inside the pulley make it turn twice as fast.

How it works

The belt runs from the drum down to one of three pulleys. The fork slides it across while the drum keeps turning. On the middle pulley the shaft turns at the same speed as the drum, because that pulley is fixed to it.

The right-hand pulley carries a small bevel gear on a pin. It sits between two big bevel gears. One is fixed to the shaft. The other is held still by a brake. As the pulley turns, the small gear is carried round and rolls on the held gear, so it spins. Being carried round turns the shaft gear once. The spin adds a second turn. So the shaft turns twice for each turn of the pulley.

The brake only grips by rubbing. When the shaft must suddenly speed up, the held gear is pushed hard and slips forward a little, then stops again. This saves the gears from a jerk. Try Middle, then Right, and watch the shaft's speed double while the held gear gives a small jump. Choose Closed to see the pulley as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

For transmitting two speeds, one a differential motion. The band is shown on a loose pulley on lower shaft. The middle pulley is fast on said shaft, and has a small bevel-gear secured to its hub. Pulley on the right, which, like that on the left, is loose on shaft, carries, transversely, another bevel-gear. A third bevel-gear, loose upon the shaft, is held by a friction-band which is weighted at the end. On moving band on middle pulley a simple motion is the result, but when it is moved to right-hand pulley a double speed is given to shaft. The friction-band or curb on the third bevel-gear is to allow it to slip a little on a sudden change of speed.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing and description. His dotted lines show the gears hidden inside the pulleys. Here part of the right-hand pulley's rim is cut away, below the belt, so you can see them. The sizes, the tooth counts, the frame and the fork were made up. Brown draws the belt on the loose pulley. How hard the brake grips and how heavy the shaft is were also made up.
