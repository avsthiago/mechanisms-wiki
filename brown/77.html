No. 77: Lever with two pawls

A lever rocks back and forth beside a wheel with a ring of pins. Two rods hang from the lever and push the pins round. One pushes on each swing, so the wheel keeps turning the same way nearly all the time.

How it works

The lever rocks to and fro on its pin. The upper rod is joined above the pin and the lower rod below it. So when one rod moves left, the other moves right.

Each rod ends in a hooked tip that rests between the pins. A light spring holds it down. When a rod moves left, the flat front of its tip pushes a pin and turns the wheel. When it moves right, the sloped back of its tip rides up over the pins behind it and drops into the next gap. While one rod slides back, the other pushes. So the wheel turns on both swings, with only a short pause each time the lever changes direction.

The rod that is pushing turns red. Try Tiny. The tips no longer slide back past a pin, so the wheel hardly moves. Then try Wide and watch it run almost without stopping.

In Brown's words

The vibration of the lever, C, on the center or fulcrum, A, produces a rotary movement of the wheel, B, by means of the two pawls, which act alternately. This is almost a continuous movement.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown does not show how the rods catch the wheel. Here the circles in his drawing are short pins, and each rod ends in a hooked tip that drops between them. The raised ring the tips rest on, the springs, the plate and base, the number of pins and all the sizes were made up.
