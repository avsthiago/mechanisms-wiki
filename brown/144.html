No. 144: Lazy tongs

Strips of metal cross like scissors and are joined by pins. When the right end is pushed in and out a little, the left end moves in and out much further. Toy grabbers and old pumps used this to reach a long way from a small push.

How it works

Every bar is the same length. Where two bars cross, a pin joins them in the middle. The block holds the crossing nearest the right end still. The two rods can move only away from it or towards it.

When the right rod moves out a little, every diamond in the zigzag gets a little wider. All the widenings add up along the whole chain. The left rod has more diamonds between it and the block, so it moves further.

Try 3 crossings, then 5, then 7. Compare the two readings. The left rod moves three, five or seven times as far as the right rod.

In Brown's words

A system of crossed levers, termed " Lazy Tongs." A short alternating rectilinear motion of rod at the right will give a similar but much greater motion to rod at the left. It is frequently used in children's toys. It has been applied in France to a machine for raising sunken vessels; also applied to ships' pumps, three-quarters of a century ago.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The block, its base, the two layers of bars, the rod lengths and all the sizes were made up. Brown shows four diamonds. The different numbers of crossings were added for comparison.
