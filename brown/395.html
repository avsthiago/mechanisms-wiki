No. 395: Four-way cock

A round plug turns inside a casing with four pipes. Two curved passages cut through the plug join the pipes in pairs. One quarter turn changes the pairs, so steam goes to the other end of the cylinder.

How it works

Steam comes in at the top pipe. The left and right pipes go to the two ends of a steam cylinder. The bottom pipe lets used steam escape. The plug has two curved passages. Each one joins two pipes that are next to each other.

In the first position, one passage joins the top pipe to the left pipe. Fresh steam pushes the piston from the left. The other passage joins the right pipe to the bottom pipe, so the steam on the right side is pushed out.

Turn the plug a quarter turn and the passages move round to the next pair of pipes. Now fresh steam goes to the right end and the left end exhausts. Choose each position and watch the colours. Choose Closed to see the cock as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Four-way cock, used many years ago on steam engines to admit and exhaust steam from the cylinder. The two positions represented are produced by a quarter turn of the plug. Supposing the steam to enter at the top, in the upper figure the exhaust is from the right end of the cylinder, and in the lower figure the exhaust is from the left—the steam entering, of course, in the opposite port.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing, which shows the two positions side by side. Here one plug turns between them. The casing, its back plate and the length of the pipes are made up. The plug and casing are cut open so you can see the passages. The cylinder the pipes lead to is not drawn. The plug is turned by a hand lever in real life, which is left out.
