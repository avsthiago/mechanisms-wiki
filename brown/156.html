No. 156: Crank pin in a bell crank slot

A round disc turns at a steady speed. A pin on the disc slides in a long slot in a lever bent at a corner, called a bell crank. The lever rocks to and fro and moves a rod up and down. It turns a spinning motion into a straight push and pull that is not even.

How it works

The disc turns round and round. Its red pin slides up and down the slot. The pin pushes the slotted arm to one side and then the other, so the lever rocks about its pivot. The short arm moves the rod up and down through the link.

The pin is close to the pivot at one end of the slot and far from it at the other. When it is far, a small turn of the disc swings the arm only a little, so the rod is slow. When it is near, the same turn swings the arm a lot, so the rod is quick. The disc never changes speed. The rod does.

Move the pin closer to the centre and the lever rocks less. Try Near, then Far, and watch the speed readout.

In Brown's words

Circular motion into variable alternating rectilinear motion, by the wrist or crank-pin on the rotating disk working in the slot of the bell-crank or elbow-lever.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The pivot, the backing plate, the base, the link and the rails for the rod were made up. Brown draws the rod joined straight to the end of the short arm. Here a short link is added, because the end of the arm moves on a curve and the rod can only go straight. The pin distances were added for comparison.
