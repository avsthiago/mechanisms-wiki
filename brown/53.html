No. 53: Reversing gear with double clutch

One shaft always turns the same way. A sliding clutch lets it drive a second shaft forwards or backwards, or not at all.

How it works

The driving gear touches both side gears at once, one on its left and one on its right. That makes them spin in opposite directions. But they are loose on their shaft, so the shaft itself does not turn yet.

The clutch is the only part locked to the shaft. Slide it left and its teeth grip the left gear, so the shaft turns with that gear. Slide it right and it grips the other gear, so the shaft turns the other way. In the middle it grips neither, and the shaft slows down and stops.

Watch closely when you change sides. The clutch waits until the gaps line up with its teeth, then it drops in.

In Brown's words

The vertical shaft is made to drive the horizontal one in either direction, as may be desired, by means of the double-clutch and bevel-gears. The gears on the horizontal shaft are loose, and are driven in opposite directions by the third gear; the double-clutch slides upon a key or feather fixed on the horizontal shaft, which is made to rotate either to the right or left, according to the side on which it is engaged.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown drew slanted clutch teeth; square ones are used here so both sides grip the same way. The number of gear teeth, the sizes and the stand for the lever were made up.
