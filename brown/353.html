No. 353: Tilt hammer as a first-order lever

A wheel with pointed wipers turns beside the short end of a long lever. Each wiper pushes the short end down, so the heavy hammer head at the other end swings up. When the wiper slips off, the head drops onto the anvil.

How it works

The wheel turns. A wiper meets the short end of the arm and pushes it down. Because the pivot is between the ends, the long end with the heavy head goes up.

The wiper reaches only so far. When its tip slides past the end of the arm, nothing holds the arm up. The head drops by its own weight and strikes the anvil. A moment later the next wiper arrives.

The long end moves much farther than the short end. A small push on the short end gives a big swing of the head. Try 2 wipers, then 6, and count the blows for each turn of the wheel.

In Brown's words

A modification of the tilt or trip hammer, illustrated by 74. In this the hammer helve is a lever of the first order. In 74 it is a lever of the third order.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The posts that hold the pivot and the wheel, the base, the anvil's shape and all the sizes were made up. The wipers are drawn as plain spikes. The head falls only under its own weight and bounces a little on the anvil. The choice of 2 or 6 wipers was added for comparison.
