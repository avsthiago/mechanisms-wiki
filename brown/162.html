No. 162: Water-wheel governor

Two balls spin on an upright spindle. When the wheel goes too fast they swing out, and a gear train lifts a gate to let in less water. When it goes too slow the gate is lowered to let in more.

How it works

The engine turns the driving shaft. Two bevel gears pass the turning down to the upright spindle. The spindle carries two balls on hinged arms, and the balls swing outwards as it spins.

When the engine speeds up, the balls swing out and pull the sleeve up. The red pin on the sleeve then catches a stud on the upper loose gear. That gear turns with the spindle and drives the lower shaft, which lifts the gate. When the engine slows, the pin drops and catches the lower gear instead. It turns the shaft the other way, and the gate goes down. In between, the pin touches neither stud and the gate stays still.

Move the speed slider from slow to fast and watch the pin and the gate. Then try a heavy sleeve. The balls need a higher speed before they rise.

In Brown's words

Water-wheel governor acting on the same principle as 161, but by different means. The governor is driven by the top horizontal shaft and bevel-gears, and the lower gears control the rise and fall of the shuttle or gate over or through which the water flows to the wheel. The action is as follows:—The two bevel-gears on the lower part of the center spindle, which are furnished with studs, are fitted loosely to the said spindle and remain at rest so long as the governor has a proper velocity; but immediately that the velocity increases, the balls, flying further out, draw up the pin which is attached to a loose sleeve which slides up and down the spindle, and this pin, coming in contact with the stud on the upper bevel gear, causes that gear to rotate with the spindle and to give motion to the lower horizontal shaft in such a direction as to make it raise the shuttle or gate, and so reduce the quantity of water passing to the wheel. On the contrary, if the speed of the governor decreases below that required, the pin falls and gives motion to the lower bevel-gear, which drives the horizontal shaft in the opposite direction and produces a contrary effect.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The stand, the wall, the gate with its guides and rope, and the screw and wheel that slow the gate down were made up. The pin is joined to the sleeve by a rod hidden inside the spindle. The balls rise and fall by a simple spring and weight rule, not by a real engine. The engine speed is set by the slider, so the gate does not slow the engine as it would in life. At the end of its travel the rope slips on the drum.
