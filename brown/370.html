No. 370: Mirror polishing motion

A machine that rubs a mirror over a polishing bed in an ever-changing pattern. A crank swings a long bar, and the mirror rides on the bar. A pawl, a small hook that pushes one tooth, turns the mirror a little at every turn, so no two strokes rub the same way.

How it works

Turn the handle and the top of the bar goes round in a circle. The lower end can only slide between the two pins. So the bar slides back and forth and swings from side to side at the same time, and the mirror scrubs over the bed in a long loop.

If that were all, the mirror would follow the same loop for ever. But the eccentric pulls the pawl lever back and forth once each turn. On each push the pawl moves the ratchet wheel on by one tooth. The mirror turns with it, so the next loop rubs at a new angle.

Watch the line on the bed fill in round the middle. Try 12 teeth, then 20. Fewer teeth means a bigger turn each stroke, so the pattern comes round sooner.

In Brown's words

Motion for polishing mirrors, the rubbing of which should be varied as much as practicable. The handle turns the crank to which the long bar and attached ratchet-wheel are connected. The mirror is secured rigidly to the ratchet-wheel. The long bar, which is guided by pins in the lower rail, has both a longitudinal and an oscillating movement, and the ratchet-wheel is caused to rotate intermittently by a click operated by an eccentric on the crank-shaft, and hence the mirror has a compound movement.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The posts under the rails, the polishing bed, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The eccentric is drawn big, with its centre close to the crank pin, so the click swings just over one tooth. The pins leave the bar a little play, and the bar is shown centred between them. Nothing holds the wheel between pushes except the rub of the mirror on the bed.
