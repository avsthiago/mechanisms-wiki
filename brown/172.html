No. 172: Egg-shaped motion

A long bar is pushed round by a crank, while one spot of the bar is held in a swivel. A pencil on the bar then draws a rounded shape like an egg. It shows how a round motion can be bent into another shape.

How it works

The crank pin goes round in a small circle and carries one end of the bar. Further along, the bar is held by the swivel pin. So the bar must turn and slide as the crank goes round.

The pencil sits on the bar, away from the crank. It moves with the crank pin, and it also swings up and down as the bar tilts. The two together make a shape that is fuller at one end, like an egg.

Try Near crank, then Near swivel, and compare. The nearer the pencil is to the swivel, the flatter the egg gets.

In Brown's words

A mode of obtaining an egg-shaped elliptical movement.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows no slot and no board. The two rails that make the slot, the crank arm, the swivel post, the board, the belt and all the sizes were made up. The dashed egg is drawn in advance so you can see where the pencil is going. The other pencil positions were added for comparison.
