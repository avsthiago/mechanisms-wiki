No. 99: Spiral guide

A flat disc has a spiral groove on its face. A small roller sits in the groove and is held by a sliding rod. As the disc turns, the groove pushes the roller steadily along. It feeds a drill forward at a slow, even pace.

How it works

Watch the roller. It sits in the gap between two turns of the spiral, so it cannot wander off. The rod it is fixed to can only slide up and down, in a straight line toward the middle of the disc.

As the disc turns, each turn of the rib sweeps past the roller and pushes it along. Every full turn of the disc moves the roller out by exactly one gap between turns. That is why the rod moves at an even, slow pace, however fast the disc turns.

Try Forwards. The rod moves down. At the end of the groove the roller cannot go further, so the disc stops. Now try Backwards, and then Back and forth. Turning the disc the other way pulls the rod back.

In Brown's words

Spiral guide attached to the face of a disk; used for the feed-motion of a drilling machine.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows a groove; here it is drawn as a raised rib, with the roller between two turns. The rod, the guides, the stand, the number of turns and all the sizes were made up. Brown gives no drill, so none is shown. In the model the disc stops when the roller reaches either end of the spiral.
