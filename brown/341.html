No. 341: Grasshopper beam engine

A long beam rocks over a steam cylinder, but it has no fixed pivot. One end rides on a swinging post, and a bar from the wall steers it. Together they make the other end of the beam go straight up and down, right above the piston.

How it works

Watch the beam. Its left end goes straight up and down over the cylinder. Its right end sits on the rocking post and only shuffles a little to the side. The crank near the floor goes round once for every stroke.

The steering bar from the wall is exactly half as long as the beam, and it holds the middle of the beam. When the beam tilts, the bar swings and pulls its middle along an arc. Its right end can only shuffle sideways on the post. Those two moves together keep the left end on a straight upright line, so the piston rod needs no slide to guide it.

Pick the piston rod and watch it slide straight through the cover. Then compare the two readings. The piston moves up and down a long way, while the beam only tilts a little. Choose Closed to see the cylinder as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

"Grasshopper" beam engine. The beam is attached at one end to a rocking-pillar, A, and the shaft arranged as near to the cylinder as the crank will work. B is the radius-bar of the parallel motion.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown draws the steering bar short, joined near the connecting rod. That shape cannot keep the piston rod straight, so here the bar is half as long as the beam and holds its middle, which makes the path straight to within a hair. The wall bracket, the bearings, the cylinder cover, the floor and all the sizes were made up. The cylinder is drawn see-through so the piston shows.
