No. 371: Mangle wheel toothed on both faces

A small gear turns steadily one way, yet the big wheel turns almost a whole turn one way and then back again. The wheel has teeth on its front face and on its back face, and a gap in its rim lets the small gear cross from one face to the other.

How it works

Turn the crank and the small gear, called a pinion, turns with it. Its shaft points at the middle of the wheel, so its teeth meet the teeth standing on the face of the rim. In the picture it is on the front face, pushing the wheel round.

When the gap in the rim comes round, the pinion rolls round the toothed end of the rim and through the gap. Now it is behind the rim, on the back face. The same turning now pushes the wheel the other way. A pin on the pinion's end runs in a groove on the wheel. The groove holds the pinion at the right depth on each face and leads it round the end of the rim.

Watch the wheel readout as the pinion goes through the gap. The wheel slows, stops and turns back. A minus sign means the other way.

In Brown's words

Modification of mangle-wheel motion. The large wheel is toothed on both faces, and an alternating circular motion is produced by the uniform revolution of the pinion, which passes from one side of the wheel to the other through an opening on the left of the figure.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The crank, the sliding bearing, the post, the base, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. Brown does not say what moves the pinion across. Here a groove on a drum inside the rim guides a pin on the pinion's end, as on other mangle wheels. The teeth round the two ends of the rim lean a little, and are a little thinner and shorter, so the pinion meets them cleanly as it goes round.
