No. 173: Tappet wheel and screw

A turning disc carries a screw with a small toothed wheel on its end. Once in each turn the wheel knocks a fixed pin and turns by one tooth. The screw turns with it and moves a nut a little farther out, so the stroke of a bar grows turn after turn. Silk machines use this to lay thread evenly on a bobbin.

How it works

The disc turns round and carries the screw with it. The peg on the nut goes round in a circle. The slotted bar only moves from side to side, so it copies the sideways part of that circle. The wider the circle, the longer the stroke.

Once in each turn the tappet wheel reaches the fixed pin. A tooth hits the pin and the wheel is pushed on by one tooth. The screw turns with the wheel, and the nut cannot turn, so it slides outward along the screw. The circle gets a little wider on every turn. At the far end the screw is wound back and it starts again.

Try 3 teeth, then 6. Watch the stroke reading. With fewer teeth, each knock turns the screw further, so the stroke grows faster.

In Brown's words

A movement used in silk machinery for the same purpose as that described in 142. On the back of a disk or bevel-gear is secured a screw with a tappet-wheel at one extremity. On each revolution of the disk the tappet-wheel comes in contact with a pin or tappet, and thus receives an intermittent rotary movement. A wrist secured to a nut on the screw enters and works in slotted bar at the end of the rod which guides the silk on the bobbins. Each revolution of the disk varies the length of stroke of the guide-rod, as the tappet-wheel on the end of the screw turns the screw with it, and the position of the nut on the screw is therefore changed.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows the disc as a bevel gear and no frame. The plain disc, the hub and handle, the back plate, the rails and end plates, the stand and pin, the guide for the arm, the sizes and the tooth counts were made up. The nut is solid, so its inside thread is not drawn. The screw is shown in line with the disc centre. When the nut reaches the far end, the model winds the screw back during one turn of the disc so you can keep watching. The choice of teeth was added to show what it changes.
