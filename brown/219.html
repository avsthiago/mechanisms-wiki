No. 219: Eccentric crown wheel and pinion

A long gear, called a pinion, turns a crown wheel, a wheel with its teeth standing up round the rim. The wheel is fixed to its shaft off centre. So the pinion meets its rim sometimes near the shaft and sometimes far out, and the wheel speeds up and slows down in every turn.

How it works

The pinion turns at a steady speed. Each of its teeth pushes one tooth of the ring past it. The ring is held on its shaft well off centre, so the whole ring swings round the shaft.

Watch where the rim crosses the pinion. Sometimes it is close to the shaft and sometimes far away. Close in, the pinion's push acts on a short arm, so the wheel spins fast. Far out, the same push has to carry the rim round a much bigger circle, so the wheel turns slowly. The pinion is long so its teeth reach the rim wherever it is.

Watch the From shaft and Crown wheel readouts together. Then pick On the shaft. Now the rim always crosses the pinion at the same place, and the speed stays the same all the way round.

In Brown's words

Variable circular motion by crown-wheel and pinion. The crown-wheel is placed eccentrically to the shaft, therefore the relative radius changes.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown's pinion is long and fluted, so here it is a pinion with teeth along its whole length, and the rim slides along it as the wheel swings. Its teeth meet the ring's teeth at a slight slant when the rim is far from the middle of the pinion; a real wheel would need its teeth shaped for that. The bearings, posts, base, the foot bearing under the shaft, the shape of the arms, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The other two wheel positions were added to compare.
