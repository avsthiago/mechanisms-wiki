No. 216: Inner and outer part-toothed wheels

A big ring with teeth on the inside and an inner gear with teeth on the outside are fixed together and turn steadily. They take turns driving one small gear. The small gear goes slowly one way, then quickly back.

How it works

Turn the crank and the ring and the inner gear turn together. The pinion sits between them. Only one of them has teeth at the pinion at any moment. When the inner gear's teeth reach it, the pinion turns slowly the opposite way to the inner gear.

When the ring's teeth come round, the pinion turns the same way as the ring, and much faster. Its teeth are far from the middle, so they move fast and push the pinion round quickly. The pinion goes slow one way, then quick the other way.

With Half and half, as Brown drew it, the ring passes more teeth than the inner gear. So the pinion goes back further than it went forward, and it creeps round one way. Try Same number and watch the Moved readout come back to zero each time.

In Brown's words

The external and internal mutilated cog-wheels work alternately into the pinion, and give slow forward and quick reverse motion.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The crank, the disc behind the ring, the plate, the post that holds the pinion, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The teeth at each end of both toothed parts are cut down to stubs. Without that, the pinion would strike them as it changes direction. The choice of Same number was added: it gives the ring fewer teeth and the inner gear more, so the pinion swings to and fro instead of creeping round.
