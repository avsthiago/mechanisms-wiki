No. 160: Treadle, spring and belt

A foot treadle pulls a belt, and the belt is wound once round a wheel. Each push turns the wheel one way. A bent spring lifts the treadle back up, and the wheel turns back the other way.

How it works

Press the treadle down. It pulls the belt down with it, and the belt cannot slip on the wheel, so it drags the wheel round. The belt also bends the spring down.

Let go and the spring springs back. It pulls the belt up and lifts the treadle ready for the next push. Now the wheel turns the other way. The red bead on the belt shows the belt moving the same distance as the treadle.

A wheel that is turned back and forth like this can drive a lathe or a grindstone. Try the wide wheel, then the narrow one, and compare how far the wheel turns on each push.

In Brown's words

Alternating curvilinear motion into alternating circular. When the treadle has been depressed, the spring at the top elevates it for the next stroke; the connecting band passes once round the pulley, to which it gives motion.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The base, the pin supports, the post behind the wheel and the wall that holds the spring were made up. The belt runs straight up and is tied to the spring at a fixed place, and the treadle moves by itself instead of by a foot. The spring bends like a springy strip held at one end. All sizes are made up. The narrow wheel is added to show the effect of size.
