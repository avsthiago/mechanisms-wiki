No. 4: Crossed belt to an upright shaft

A belt carries turning from a level shaft to an upright one below it. Two guide pulleys bring the belt down and round to a drum on the upright shaft. Crossing the belt makes it grip the pulleys better.

How it works

The top pulley pulls the belt down to the guide pulleys. From each one the belt runs level to the drum. It wraps once round the drum and leaves on the other side, a little higher up.

On the level runs the belt makes a quarter turn. That lets it lie flat on the drum, whose shaft stands upright. The top shaft and the drum shaft both lie in one flat plane, at a right angle.

Try crossed, then open. With the open belt it touches less than half of the top pulley. Crossed, it wraps most of the way round, so it grips much better. Watch the drum turn the other way too.

In Brown's words

A method of transmitting motion from a shaft at right angles to another whose axis is in the same plane. This is shown with a crossed belt. An open belt may be used, but the crossed one is preferable, as it gives more surface of contact.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing and description. The sizes, the distance between the shafts and the frame are made up. Brown does not say which pulley drives; here the top one does. In his drawing the belt seems to wind round the drum; here it makes exactly one turn. So the right guide pulley sits a little higher than the left one.
