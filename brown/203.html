No. 203: Curved slotted arm and straight arm

A hook-shaped arm swings to and fro at a steady speed. A pin on a straight arm rides in its curved slot. The straight arm swings too, but slowly at one end of the slot and faster at the other.

How it works

The curved arm swings back and forth at a steady speed. The pin must stay in the slot, and the straight arm can only swing round its own pivot. So the pin is pushed up or down, and the straight arm swings with it.

Where the slot runs straight across the top, it mostly slides past the pin. The pin is hardly pushed, so the straight arm creeps. Where the slot curves down, it pushes the pin much harder, and the straight arm moves faster. The same steady swing gives a changing speed.

Try Straight end, then Curved part, and compare the straight arm's speed in the readout. With Most of the slot you see it creep and then hurry in every swing.

In Brown's words

A regular vibrating movement of the curved slotted arm gives a variable vibration to the straight arm.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown does not show what swings the curved arm, so the model swings it at a steady speed and turns it round at each end. The backing plate, the pivot pins, the sizes and how far the arm swings were made up. The choice of slot part was added to show where the speed changes.
