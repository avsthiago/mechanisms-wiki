No. 192: Eccentric mangle wheel

A small gear turns steadily one way, yet the big wheel turns most of a turn one way and then back again. The wheel's teeth are not centred on its shaft, so its speed keeps changing all the way round.

How it works

Turn the crank and the small gear, called a pinion, turns with it. In the picture it runs along the outer edge of the toothed band and pushes the wheel round. At the far end it rolls round the end of the band and comes back along the inner edge, so the wheel turns back.

The band is not centred on the wheel's shaft. Where its edge is far from the shaft, the pinion moves the wheel slowly. Where it is near, the wheel goes faster. So the speed keeps changing. The pinion's shaft rides up and down in the guide, and its joints let it tilt to follow.

Watch the wheel speed readout through a whole trip. A minus sign means the wheel turns the other way. See where it is fastest and where it stops.

In Brown's words

A variety of what is known as the "mangle-wheel." One variety of this was illustrated by 36. In this one the speed varies in every part of a revolution, the groove, b, d, in which the pinion-shaft is guided, as well as the series of teeth, being eccentric to the axis of the wheel.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The crank, the guide, the jointed shaft, the post, the base, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. Brown draws the groove close to the teeth. Here it runs one pinion's radius from them, so the pinion stays in mesh. Brown says the shaft has a universal joint; here it has two, so the pinion stays square to the wheel and turns at the same speed as the crank.
