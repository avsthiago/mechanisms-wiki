No. 268: Crank and swinging rod

A long rod is joined to a turning crank at one end. Near its other end it slides through a guide that can swing. As the crank goes round, the rod slides back and forth through the guide and its far end swings up and down.

How it works

The pin on the crank goes round in a circle. The rod is joined to the pin, so its near end goes round too. But the rod must pass through the guide, so it cannot just follow the pin. It tilts, and it slides through the guide at the same time.

When the pin is high, the far end of the rod is low. When the pin is low, the far end is high. The guide works like the middle of a see-saw. The rod also slides a little in and out of the guide, because the pin is sometimes nearer to it and sometimes farther away.

Try the three crank arms. A longer arm makes a bigger circle for the pin, so the far end of the rod swings through a bigger arc.

In Brown's words

Circular into reciprocating motion by means of a crank and oscillating rod.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown also draws the rod in a second position with dotted lines; the model shows that by moving. The base, the posts, the pin that holds the guide, the sizes and the three crank arms were made up. In the drawing the wheel is only a stand beside the guide, so here it stays still.
