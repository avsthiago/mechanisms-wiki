No. 439: Self-emptying bucket

A bucket and a heavy weight hang on the two ends of a cord over a pulley. Water falling into the bucket makes it heavier, so it sinks. At the bottom a valve opens and the water runs out, and the weight pulls the bucket up again. One steady stream of water gives an up-and-down motion.

How it works

Water pours from the trough into the bucket. As the bucket gets heavier it starts to sink, and the weight on the other side of the pulley is pulled up.

When the rod under the bucket hits the ground, it is pushed up and opens a hole in the bottom. The water runs out. Now the bucket is lighter than the weight, so the weight sinks and the bucket is lifted back up to the trough. The valve closes as it leaves the ground.

Try a trickle: the bucket takes a long time to fill, so it waits at the top. Try a flood: it fills so fast on the way up that it turns round before it reaches the top.

In Brown's words

A method of obtaining a reciprocating motion from a continuous fall of water, by means of a valve in the bottom of the bucket which opens by striking the ground and thereby emptying the bucket, which is caused to rise again by the action of a counter-weight on the other side of the pulley over which it is suspended.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The post behind the pulley, the ground and the trough are made up so the machine stands up. The weights, the size of the hole and the speeds are chosen so the cycle works. A foot ring under the bucket lets the water run out below it. The motion is worked out from the weights, with some drag from the air and the pulley.
