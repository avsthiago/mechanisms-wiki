No. 383: Cloth dressing rollers

Cloth is wound from one roller to another. On the way it passes over a turning cylinder covered with brushes. The brushes dress the cloth, which means they smooth and finish it.

How it works

The cloth runs from the upper roller, round the left side of the cylinder, and down to the lower roller. The brushes brush it as it goes past. Both rollers turn the same way as the cloth moves.

The cloth moves at one steady speed. The roller it is leaving gets thinner, so it must turn faster and faster to keep that speed. The roller it is winding onto gets thicker, so it turns slower and slower. When one roller is nearly bare, the cloth runs back the other way.

The brush cylinder can turn with the cloth, faster than the cloth or against it. Try each one. The faster or opposite choices make the brushes rub across the cloth, and that does the dressing.

In Brown's words

Represents the principal elements of machinery for dressing cloth and warps, consisting of two rollers, from one to the other of which the yarn or cloth is wound, and an interposed cylinder having its periphery either smooth-surfaced or armed with brushes, teasels, or other contrivances, according to the nature of the work to be done. These elements are used in machines for sizing warps, gig-mills for dressing woolen goods, and in most machines for finishing woven fabrics.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown draws a cut through the frame. The arch, the cloth thickness and the sizes were made up. The cloth is drawn much thicker than real cloth so the rollers visibly change size. When a roller is nearly empty, the model simply runs the cloth the other way. The cylinder is drawn with brush blocks round its rim.
