No. 321: Harrison's going-barrel

A clock weight turns the great wheel through a spring. While you wind the weight up, the spring keeps pushing the wheel round. So the clock keeps going during winding, instead of stopping or running backwards.

How it works

The weight hangs on a cord from the barrel. Its pull turns the barrel and the small ratchet. The small ratchet wheel pushes the short pawl, which is fixed to the big ratchet, so the big ratchet turns too. The big ratchet pulls the great wheel along through the bent spring.

To wind, a key turns the barrel backwards. Now the weight pulls nothing round, and the spring tries to drag the big ratchet back. The long holding pawl from the frame stops it. So the spring, held at one end, keeps pushing the great wheel forward on its own. It only needs to last the minute or so that winding takes.

Press Wind and watch the great wheel's readout: it does not change. The spring readout drops instead, as the spring gives up its push. Let it run again, and the weight soon winds the spring back up.

In Brown's words

Harrison's "going-barrel." Larger ratchet-wheel, to which the click, R, is attached, is connected with the great wheel, G, by a spring, S, S'. While the clock is going, the weight acts upon the great wheel, G, through the spring ; but as soon as the weight is taken off by winding, the click, T, whose pivot is set in the frame, prevents the larger ratchet from falling back, and so the spring, S, S', still drives the great wheel during the time the clock takes to wind, as it need only just keep the escapement going, the pendulum taking care of itself for that short time. Good watches have a substantially similar apparatus.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The rest of the clock is left out; the great wheel simply turns at a steady speed while the spring still pushes it. The key, the clock plate and the stud for the long holding pawl were added. The great wheel is drawn as a ring on spokes in front, so the spring and ratchets behind it show. The springs that press the catches onto the teeth are left out. Sizes, tooth counts and how far the weight falls were made up.
