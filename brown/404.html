No. 404: Cyclograph with a bent bar

A springy bar is bent by a screw until its edge is part of a true circle. A pencil run along the edge then draws an arc through three chosen points. You never need the centre of the circle, which may be far off the paper.

How it works

Three points of the arc are marked on the paper. The screw is turned and pushes out the middle of the springy bar. The rollers hold its ends back, so the bar bends. When its edge touches all three points, the pencil is run along it.

A bar of even thickness would bend most in the middle and hardly at all near the ends. This bar is thick where the push bends it hardest and thin where the push is weak. So every part bends by the same amount, and the edge is a true circle at any setting.

Try a flat arc, then a curved one. Count the screw turns needed, and see how the radius gets smaller as the bar bends more.

In Brown's words

Another cyclograph. The elastic arched bar is made half the depth at the ends that it is at the middle, and is formed so that its outer edge coincides with a true circular arc when bent to its greatest extent. Three points in the required arc being given, the bar is bent to them by means of the screw, each end being confined to the straight bar by means of a small roller.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The paper, the three points and the pencil were added. The ends of the springy bar past the rollers are drawn straight, because nothing bends them there. The sizes, the thread of the screw and the shape of the handle are made up. The tool hangs over the edge of the paper so the handle can turn.
