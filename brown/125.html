No. 125: Two cranked gears, second form

Three gears in a row turn together. Each has a pin on its face, and rods and bars join the pins to one sliding rod at the top. The rod rises and falls in a pattern that does not repeat each turn, because the three gears turn at different speeds.

How it works

Turn the small driving gear. Its teeth push the middle gear the other way, and the middle gear pushes the big one. Each gear has a pin, and each pin pulls a thin rod. The lower rods rock the lower bar. A short rod carries that rocking up to the upper bar.

The sliding rod is held by the upper bar, so it feels the pull from all three gears at once. The big gear turns slowly and the small one quickly, so they fall in and out of step. The rod does not repeat its path every turn.

Watch the red rod and count how often it reaches its highest point in one turn of the small gear. Then slow the speed right down and follow how the lower bar tilts, and how the tilt changes the height of the rod.

In Brown's words

A modification of the motion shown in 122, but of a more complex character.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows no frame, no handle, no guide for the rod and no spring. The back plate, the guide for the rod, the spring and the sizes and tooth counts were made up. As drawn, the rods fix the rod's height three ways at once, so the linkage would jam. The spring was added to make it work: it pulls the rod up, and the rod rises as high as the rods let it. The upper bar was left free to tilt for the same reason.
