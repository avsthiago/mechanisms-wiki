No. 98: Crank pin in a closed groove

A pin on a turning disc runs round inside a closed groove cut in an arm. The pin pushes the arm up and down, so the arm rocks about its own shaft. The disc turns steadily, but the arm rocks faster in some places than in others.

How it works

The disc turns at a steady speed. The red pin is fixed in the disc, away from its centre, so it goes round in a circle. It stands inside a groove that goes right round the middle of the arm.

The groove is a closed loop, so the pin cannot slip out. It runs along both long sides and round both ends, once for every turn of the disc. It pushes on the groove walls, and the arm swings up and down about its shaft on the right. The pin has to slide along the groove as it goes round. That makes the arm speed up and slow down, even though the disc is steady. Brown calls this irregular vibrating motion.

Watch the red pin and the arm together. Notice where the pin is when the arm swings fastest, and where it is when the arm stops for a moment before turning back.

In Brown's words

Irregular vibrating motion is produced by the rotation of the circular disk, in which is fixed a crank-pin working in an endless groove cut in the vibrating arm.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The shape of the groove was worked out so that the pin can run round all of it while the arm rocks smoothly. It is a little different from the drawn one. The drawing also shows no way to hold the middle piece, so a small strap was added over the groove. The pin passes under it. The thickness of the arm, the shaft supports, the base and all the sizes were made up.
