No. 343: Parallel motion for an upright engine

In this upright engine the crank sits on top of the frame, right above the cylinder. The top of the piston rod is held by a small rocking piece with two radius rods, one to each side of the frame. They keep the piston rod going up and down almost straight.

How it works

The left radius rod holds the top of the rocking piece. The right one holds its bottom. Each rod swings on a curve, so its end moves toward its own column at the top and bottom of the stroke.

One end of the rocking piece is pulled left and the other end is pulled right, by the same amount. The middle stays where it is sideways. That is where the piston rod hangs, so it goes up and down almost straight. The piece only tilts.

The line is only nearly straight, and less so the further the rods swing. Look at the Sideways reading. Try Short, then Long, and watch the dark path. Choose Closed to see the cylinder as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Parallel motion for upright engine. A, A, are radius-rods connected at one end with the framing and at the other with a vibrating piece on top of piston-rod.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The crank is drawn shorter than in Brown's figure, because the radius rods could not follow a stroke that long. The two radius rods are made the same length, the brackets, the floor, the cylinder and all sizes were made up, and the flywheel is set behind the frame. The other strokes were added for comparison.
