No. 243: One belt to two upright shafts

One belt takes the turning of a sideways shaft and passes it to two upright shafts at the same time. The belt twists a quarter turn as it goes. This is how a machine can drive shafts that point in different directions from one source.

How it works

Turn the middle pulley and the belt moves with it. It goes round the two small pulleys, out to the tall pulley on the left, straight across to the tall pulley on the right, and back. Both tall shafts turn the same way.

The belt moves at the same speed everywhere. A big pulley turns slowly while a small pulley turns fast to keep up. The tall pulleys are bigger than the middle one, so they turn slower. Where the belt goes from a flat pulley to an upright one, it twists a quarter turn.

Try the small middle pulley, then the big one. The belt travels more slowly round the small one, so the tall shafts turn more slowly too.

In Brown's words

Represents a mode of transmitting power from a horizontal shaft to two vertical ones by means of pulleys and a band.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The back plate, the bearings, the shafts, the sizes and the red mark were made up. Brown draws the two runs of belt across the top almost on top of each other. Here one run is in front and one behind, so they do not touch.
