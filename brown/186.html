No. 186: Unhooking the eccentric rod

A rod pushed to and fro by the engine ends in an open hook, called a gab, that rests on a pin. The pin is on an arm that rocks a shaft. A spring handle lifts the hook off the pin, so the rod no longer moves the shaft.

How it works

The rod slides to and fro. Its hooked end sits round the red pin, so the arm swings with it and rocks the shaft above.

Pull the handle up and the hooked end swings up on its hinge. The hook leaves the pin. The rod keeps sliding, but the arm and the shaft stay still.

Press Lifted off and watch the hook rise. Then press Hooked on. The hook waits until it is over the pin, then drops onto it.

In Brown's words

Apparatus for disengaging the eccentric-rod from the valve-gear. By pulling up the spring handle below until it catches in the notch, a, the pin is disengaged from the gab in the eccentric-rod.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The stand, the bearing, the hinge in the rod and the sizes were made up. The notch that holds the handle up is left out. The engine's eccentric is off the picture, so the rod simply slides to and fro.
