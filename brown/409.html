No. 409: Proportional compasses

Two long legs cross like a pair of scissors and have a sharp point at each of their four ends. The two points at the top are always a set fraction as far apart as the two points at the bottom. Measure something with one end and copy it, bigger or smaller, with the other end.

How it works

The slide holds the two legs together where they cross. The legs open and close about it. Watch the two red lines. When one gets longer, so does the other.

Each pair of points is the same angle apart as seen from the slide. So the gap at each end depends on how far the points are from the slide. If the long ends are twice as far from the slide as the short ends, their gap is always twice as big.

Loosen the screw and move the slide along the slots. Try the same size, then double, then three times, and compare the two gaps.

In Brown's words

Proportional compasses used in copying drawings on a given larger or smaller scale. The pivot of compasses is secured in a slide which is adjustable in the longitudinal slots of legs, and capable of being secured by a set screw, the dimensions are taken between one pair of points and transferred with the other pair, and thus enlarged or diminished in proportion to the relative distances of the points from the pivot. A scale is provided on one or both legs to indicate the proportion.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The two legs are drawn plain, with the slots, slide and a scale along one leg. The red lines and the opening and closing were added to show the ratio. The sizes and the scale marks are made up.
