No. 135: Triangular tappet

A rounded, three-cornered tappet turns on a shaft inside a frame. It pushes the frame up and down, and the frame rests for an instant at the end of each stroke. A steam engine at the Paris Mint used this to slide its valve quickly from one end to the other.

How it works

The shaft turns, and the tappet turns with it. The tappet is a rounded triangle, and every way you measure across it gives the same width. Watch the red peg go round.

The frame around it is exactly that wide, so the tappet always touches both its top and its bottom. When a flat arc of the tappet slides along one side, the frame stays still. When a corner swings round, the frame is pushed quickly up or down. So the rods rest, move, rest, and move back.

Watch the readout while the tappet turns. The travel stays at its top value for a moment, then drops to its bottom value and stays there. Press pause at each end to see the tappet touch the frame.

In Brown's words

Modification of the triangular eccentric 91, used on the steam engine in the Paris Mint. The circular disk behind carries the triangular tappet, which communicates an alternate rectilinear motion to the valve-rod. The valve is at rest at the completion of each stroke for an instant, and is pushed quickly across the steam-ports to the end of the next.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. In the drawing the big round disc sits behind the frame. Here it is a fixed backing plate, and it holds the shaft. The guides for the rods, the arms that hold them, the narrow plate behind the disc, the base and all the sizes were made up. The corners of the tappet are rounded so the shaft hub sits inside it. The frame is made wide enough for the tappet to turn all the way round.
