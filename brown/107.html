No. 107: Grooved cam, second form

A groove winds back and forth round the outside of a turning drum, like a snake. A pin on a sliding rod sits in the groove. The rod slides to and fro along the drum several times for each turn, always at one steady speed.

How it works

Watch the pin. The drum turns, and the groove slides past underneath it. Each slanting stretch of groove pushes the pin to one side, and the pin pushes the rod. At each bend of the groove the rod stops for a moment and turns round.

The groove has several bends to each side in one turn, so the rod goes right and back left several times while the drum turns once. Every stretch is cut at the same slant. The rod therefore moves at one steady speed, and the speed reading changes only its sign.

Nothing pulls the rod back, because the pin cannot leave the groove. Try Short, Medium and Long. A longer slide needs a steeper groove, so the rod goes faster at the same turning speed.

In Brown's words

Uniform reciprocating rectilinear motion, produced by rotary motion of grooved cams.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The pin, the arm, the rod, the two guides, the ceiling, the shaft supports and the base were made up. So were the depth and width of the groove and the number of bends. Brown draws the bends as smooth curves, and so are they here. Between the bends the groove is cut so the pin moves at a steady speed. The choice of stroke was added to show how the speed changes.
