No. 296: Lever escapement

A watch keeps time with a small wheel that swings to and fro on a coiled spring. Each swing knocks a rocking lever over, and the lever lets the escape wheel move on by half a tooth. Almost every wind-up watch works this way.

How it works

The escape wheel is always trying to turn, but the anchor will not let it. One pallet sits in the way of a tooth and holds it. For most of each swing the balance touches nothing at all.

In the middle of the swing the red pin runs into the notch of the fork and knocks the lever over. That lifts the pallet clear of its tooth. The end of the pallet is sloped, so as the tooth slides off it pushes the anchor further the same way. Now the fork gives the pin a small kick, which keeps the balance swinging. The other pallet has dropped in by then and catches the next tooth.

The balance and its spring set the pace, like a weight bouncing on a spring. A stiff spring pulls the balance back harder, so it swings faster and the watch ticks faster. Try Soft, then Stiff, and count the beats.

In Brown's words

Lever escapement. The anchor or piece, B, which carries the pallets, is attached to lever, E, C, at one end of which is a notch, E. On a disk secured on the arbor of balance is fixed a small pin which enters the notch at the middle of each vibration, causing the pallet to enter in and retire from between the teeth of escape-wheel The wheel gives an impulse to each of the pallets alternately as it leaves a tooth, and the lever gives impulse to the balance-wheel in opposite directions alternately.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The balance wheel and its coiled spring, the two pins that stop the lever, the back plate and the sizes were made up. The teeth are straight here and curved in the drawing. The mainspring is left out and its push on the wheel is taken to be steady. The parts knock and slide on each other without rubbing, and only the balance and the wheel are slowed a little.
