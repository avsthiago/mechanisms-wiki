No. 128: Three wipers and a frame

A shaft with three arms turns round and round. The arms push on two stops inside a frame, one after another. So the frame slides to and fro while the shaft keeps turning one way.

How it works

The shaft turns clockwise. Its three arms end in balls. Watch the red ball go round.

A ball coming up on the right meets the stop that hangs from the top. It pushes the stop, and the whole frame, to the right. Then it slips past the end of the stop. At that moment a ball at the bottom meets the stop that rises from the bottom. It pulls the frame back to the left.

The balls take turns, so one is always touching a stop. The frame goes right and left three times in every turn of the shaft. Press pause and see which ball is touching which stop.

In Brown's words

A continuous rotary motion of the shaft carrying the three wipers produces a reciprocating rectilinear motion of the rectangular frame. The shaft must revolve in the direction of the arrow for the parts to be in the position represented.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The guides for the end rods, the post for the shaft, the base, the thickness of the frame and all the sizes were made up. The stops are drawn with straight faces. The frame is held by the balls alone: one ball is always touching a stop, so it cannot wander.
