No. 346: Table engine

Steam pushes a piston up and down inside an upright cylinder that stands on a table. A sliding block at the top of the rod is held straight by guides. Two long rods run down past the cylinder to cranks under the table, and turn the push into a spin.

How it works

Watch the block at the top. It slides straight up and down in its guides, and the piston below it goes up and down with it. The two long rods lean first one way, then the other.

The rods are joined to the block at the top and to the crank pins at the bottom. The pins go round in circles, and the block can only move up and down. So the rods have to tilt. The guides take the sideways push from the rods, so the piston rod stays straight.

Try a short crank arm, then a long one. The long arm makes the piston travel further and makes the rods tilt more. Choose Closed to see the cylinder as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Table engine. The cylinder is fixed on a table-like base. The piston-rod has a cross-head working in straight slotted guides fixed on top of cylinder, and is connected by two side connecting-rods with two parallel cranks on shaft under the table.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The front half of the cylinder is cut away so the piston shows. The table, its legs, the two bearings under it, the slots in the table top for the rods, the shape of the guides and all the sizes were made up. Steam pipes and valves are left out.
