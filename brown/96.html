No. 96: Heart cam

A heart-shaped plate turns at a steady speed. A roller rests on its edge and pushes a bar out, then lets it come back. The bar moves at one steady speed in each direction.

How it works

Watch the roller. It starts in the dip on the right, where the edge is closest to the centre. As the cam turns, the edge moves further out in a straight, steady way, so the roller is pushed out. Half a turn later the point of the heart reaches the roller, and the bar is as far out as it goes.

Then the edge comes back in just as steadily, and the spring pulls the bar home. Out and back each take half a turn, so the bar moves at the same speed all the way. It only turns round at each end. The speed reading stays the same, and only its sign changes.

The pale lines show how Brown draws the curve. The push is cut into equal steps, with a circle for each. Half a turn is cut into the same number of equal steps, with a line for each. The curve passes through the crossings. Try Small, Medium and Large. A bigger heart gives a longer push, so the bar goes faster at the same turning speed.

In Brown's words

A heart-cam. Uniform traversing motion is imparted to the horizontal bar by the rotation of the heart-shaped cam. The dotted lines show the mode of striking out the curve of the cam. The length of traverse is divided into any number of parts; and from the center a series of concentric circles are described through these points. The outside circle is then divided into double the number of these divisions, and lines drawn to the center. The curve is then drawn through the intersections of the concentric circles and the radiating lines.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The roller, the bar, the guides, the back plate and the shaft were made up. Brown does not say what keeps the roller on the cam, so a spring was added. The edge of the cam is shaped so the centre of the roller moves exactly in a straight line at a steady speed. The pale lines turn with the cam. The choice of heart size was added to show how the push changes.
