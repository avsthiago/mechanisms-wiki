No. 179: Reversing gear for a single engine

An engine shaft pushes its valve to and fro through a rod with a notched end. Lift the rod off, slide the valve with a hand lever, and drop the rod again. The engine now runs the other way.

How it works

The shaft turns the disc, and the ring round the disc pushes the long rod to and fro. The notch at the end of the rod sits on the red pin, so the valve rod moves with it. The valve lets steam into the engine.

To reverse the engine, lift the rod. The notch leaves the pin and the valve rod is free. The hand lever now slides it to the other side. The shaft turns the other way, and the loose disc slips half a turn on it.

Press Up, and watch the lever swing and the shaft change direction. Then press Down. The notch waits until it passes over the pin, then drops onto it. Try it again to go back.

In Brown's words

Reversing-gear for a single engine. On raising the eccentric-rod the valve-spindle is released. The engine can then be reversed by working the upright lever, after which the eccentric-rod is let down again. The eccentric in this case is loose upon the shaft and driven by a projection on the shaft acting upon a nearly semi-circular projection on the side of the eccentric, which permits the eccentric to turn half-way round on the shaft on reversing the valves.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The shaft, base, bearing, guide and the hinge in the rod were made up. So was the stop that pushes the loose disc. The sizes are guesses, and the disc is shown with a small throw. In the drawing the lever stays put while the engine runs, but here it sways a little with the valve rod.
