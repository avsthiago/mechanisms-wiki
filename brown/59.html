No. 59: Two speeds by gearing

One belt from a drum can turn a shaft slowly, fast, or not at all. You only slide the belt sideways onto a different pulley. The drum above keeps turning at the same speed the whole time.

How it works

The belt runs from the drum down to one of three pulleys. Only the pulley under the belt gets pulled round. The fork slides the belt from one pulley to the next while the drum keeps turning.

The middle pulley is fixed to the main shaft, which has a small gear at its left end. The right pulley is fixed to a tube around the main shaft, with a big gear at its right end. A small gear turns a big lower gear slowly. A big gear turns a small lower gear fast.

Both pairs of gears are always in mesh. So when one pair drives the lower shaft, the lower shaft turns the other upper gear too. Try Slow, then Fast, and watch the lower shaft. A minus sign means it turns the other way to the drum.

In Brown's words

For transmitting two speeds by gearing. The band is shown on the loose pulley—the left-hand one of the lower three. The middle pulley is fixed on the same shaft as the small pinion, and the pulley to the right on a hollow shaft, on the end of which is fixed the large spur-gear. When the band is on the middle pulley a slow motion is transmitted to the shaft below; but when it is on the right-hand pulley a quick speed is given, proportionate to the diameter of the gears

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing and description. The sizes, the tooth counts, the bearings and the frame are made up. Brown draws the belt on the loose pulley; here it starts on the slow pulley so the model opens moving. Brown does not show how the belt is moved, so the sliding fork was added. He does not say what the loose pulley turns on; here it turns freely on the main shaft.
