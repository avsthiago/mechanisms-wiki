No. 86: Pump wheel with a catch

A shaft that turns all the time works a pump that must go up and down. Once each turn, a catch hooks the wheel to the shaft and lifts the pump bucket. Then it lets go, and the bucket falls back by its own weight.

How it works

The shaft and the arm on it, called the cam, turn all the time. The big wheel is loose on the shaft, so it does not have to follow. When the cam comes round, it meets the hook of the red catch and drags the wheel along. The wheel winds up the rope and lifts the bucket.

As the wheel turns, the tail of the catch swings up to the stop under the beam. The stop pushes the tail back, so the hook tips outward and slides off the end of the cam. Now the wheel is free. The heavy bucket falls and turns the wheel back until the bucket rests at the bottom. The cam keeps going and takes hold again on its next turn.

Try moving the stop further left, then further right. Watch how high the bucket rises before the catch lets go. Choose Closed to see the pump as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

A method of working a reciprocating pump by rotary motion. A rope, carrying the pump-rod, is attached to the wheel, A, which runs loosely upon the shaft. The shaft carries a cam, C, and has a continuous rotary motion. At every revolution the cam seizes the hooked catch, B, attached to the wheel, and drags it round, together with the wheel, and raises the rope until, on the extremity of the catch striking the stationary stop above, the catch is released, and the wheel is returned by the weight of the pump-bucket.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The pump barrel, the second stand behind the wheel and the shape of the cam were made up. Brown draws ropes on both sides of the wheel; here one rope hangs from the left of the rim. A light spring, not shown, keeps the catch back against a small pin on the wheel. The engine that turns the shaft is left out. The choice of stop place was added to show what it changes.
