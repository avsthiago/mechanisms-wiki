No. 221: Elliptical gear and swinging pinion

An oval gear turns steadily and drives a gear through a small gear, called a pinion, on a swinging arm. The arm rises and falls to keep the pinion in mesh with the oval. The driven gear turns round, but its speed keeps changing.

How it works

The oval gear turns steadily on a shaft near one end. Its teeth come nearer and go further from the shaft as it turns. The pinion meshes with it, so the pinion has to move in and out. It rides on an arm that swings about the driven gear's shaft.

The pinion turns the driven gear through the gear fixed behind it on the same axle. When the far end of the oval meets the pinion, its teeth move fast and the driven gear hurries. Near the shaft end the teeth move slowly and it lags. The arm's swing adds a little push or drag of its own.

A pin behind the pinion runs in the groove of the plate. The groove follows the oval at exactly the right distance, so the teeth stay at the right depth. Watch the Driven readout and the red mark as the oval goes round.

In Brown's words

Irregular circular motion imparted to wheel, A. C is an elliptical spur-gear rotating round center, D, and is the driver. B is a small pinion with teeth of the same pitch, gearing with C. The center of this pinion is not fixed, but is carried by an arm or frame which vibrates on a center, A, so that as C revolves the frame rises and falls to enable pinion to remain in gear with it, notwithstanding the variation in its radius of contact. To keep the teeth of C and B in gear to a proper depth, and prevent them from riding over each other, wheel, C, has attached to it a plate which extends beyond it and is furnished with a groove, g, h, of similar elliptical form, for the reception of a pin or small roller attached to the vibrating arm concentric with pinion, B.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown's drawing is unclear about the pinion. One gear touching both the oval and the driven gear would let the oval hit it. Here the pinion meshes with the oval, and a bigger gear on its axle, a little further back, meshes with the driven gear. The oval is a true ellipse. Its shaft sits at the special point near one end that is called a focus. The bracket holding the driven gear, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up.
