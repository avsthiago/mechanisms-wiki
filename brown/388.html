No. 388: Planing machine feed rollers

A board is pulled through a planing machine between two rollers. The smooth roller below holds it up. The toothed roller above bites into it and pushes it along.

How it works

The board slides to the right between the two rollers. The smooth roller turns clockwise under it. The toothed roller turns the other way above it. Both rollers touch the board.

The smooth roller is driven, so it pushes the board along by rubbing against it. The teeth of the upper roller dig into the wood, so they cannot slip. The board turns the toothed roller. The toothed roller is smaller, so it spins faster than the smooth one.

Try a thin board and then a thick one. The upper roller rides up on the thick board, so it always presses on the wood. The speeds of the rollers do not change.

In Brown's words

Feed-motion of Woodworth's planing machine, a smooth supporting roller, and a toothed top roller.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing, which shows only the rollers and the board. The side plates that hold the shafts were added, and the spring or weight that presses the toothed roller down is left out. The teeth only touch the board here. In a real machine they dig in a little. The board is drawn long and the marks on it show it moving.
