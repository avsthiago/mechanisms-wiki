No. 120: Toothed jaws

Two curved jaws swing on one pin, like tongs. Each jaw is moved by a toothed arm, and one small shaft turns both arms. Turning the shaft a little closes the jaws on whatever is between them, and they grip very hard.

How it works

Turn the handle and the shaft turns both pinions. The upper pinion pushes the arm with outside teeth one way. The lower pinion pushes the rim with inside teeth the other way. So the two jaws swing towards each other.

The pinions are small and the arms are big, so the shaft turns about four times for each turn of a jaw. Each jaw moves slowly but pushes with about four times the force of the handle. That is why the jaws grip so hard.

Try Nothing, then Thin rod, then Thick bar. The jaws stop when they touch it, and the handle stops with them. Watch the gap and the shaft turns.

In Brown's words

Each jaw is attached to one of the two segments, one of which has teeth outside and the other teeth inside. On turning the shaft carrying the two pinions, one of which gears with one and the other with the other segment, the jaws are brought together with great force.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows the two jaws, the two toothed arms and the shaft. The pin, the plate, the handle, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The two arms sit one behind the other so they do not touch. The shaft swings back and forth, because the arms only have a short run of teeth. The rod and the bar were added to show the jaws gripping.
