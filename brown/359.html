No. 359: Pump drill

A heavy disc spins on a stick, and the stick carries a drill. Press a bar down and the cords unwind, which spins the stick. The disc keeps spinning and winds the cords back up the other way, lifting the bar.

How it works

Look at the cords. They are wound round the top of the spindle and tied to the ends of the bar. When the bar goes down, the cords pull out and unwind. Pulling a cord off makes the spindle spin.

The bar reaches the bottom, but the heavy disc is still spinning. It winds the cords on again, this time the other way round. That lifts the bar back up. Then you press down again, and the spindle turns one way, then the other, without stopping.

Try the light disc, then the heavy one. A heavy disc keeps going longer and gives a steadier spin. Turn the push up or down and watch how fast the spindle goes.

In Brown's words

Primitive drilling apparatus. Being once set in motion, it is kept going by hand, by alternately pressing down and relieving the transverse bar to which the bands are attached, causing the bands to wind upon the spindle alternately in opposite directions, while the heavy disk or fly-wheel gives a steady momentum to the drill-spindle in its rotary motion.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The hand is not drawn: the model presses the bar down for you while it is moving down. The lengths, the weight of the disc and the amount of rubbing are made up. A stop lets the bar rise only so far.
