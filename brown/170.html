No. 170: Crossed-arm governor

Two heavy balls swing out as the spindle spins faster. Their arms cross at the top and pull a rod down. The rod closes the steam valve, so the engine does not run away.

How it works

The engine turns the spindle through two bevel gears. The two balls turn with it. Spinning balls want to fly outward, and they swing out from the spindle.

The arms cross at the hinge. So when the balls swing out, the other ends of the arms swing in. Two short links join those ends to the rod. The rod is pulled down, and the steam valve closes a little. When the engine slows, the balls drop, the rod rises and the valve opens.

Move the speed slider up and watch the balls and the rod. Below a certain speed the balls just hang. Then try a stronger spring on the rod. The balls need more speed to move it.

In Brown's words

Another form of steam engine governor. Instead of the arms being connected with a slide working on a spindle, they cross each other and are elongated upward beyond the top thereof and connected with the valve-rod by two short links.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The frame, the bearings, the gear sizes, the ball weight, the rod spring and the rod's top end were made up. Stops keep the balls from touching and from going too high. The curved bar in the drawing is made as two halves that slide past each other. The valve is not shown.
