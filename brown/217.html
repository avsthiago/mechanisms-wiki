No. 217: Wool-comb roller cam

A disc with a heart-shaped groove in its face drives the roller of a wool-combing machine. Each turn moves the roller back a little, then forward twice as far, then holds it still. So the combed wool is fed out in steps.

How it works

The disc turns at a steady speed. A stud on the end of a lever sits in its groove. Where the groove comes closer to the centre, the stud is pulled in. Where it goes further out, the stud is pushed out. So the lever swings to and fro once each turn.

Follow the stud round the heart. From the left side to the dip at the bottom, the groove moves in, so the lever swings back a little. From the dip round to the point at the top, it moves out, twice as far. A catch on the lever drives a notched wheel and the roller with it. At the point, a raised piece on the disc lifts the catch out of its notch. From the point back to the left side, the lever swings back with the catch sliding over the rim, so the roller rests.

Watch the Now reading and the stud in the groove together. Back, Forward and Resting each belong to one stretch of the heart. Then pick the cam and watch the raised piece pass under the catch's pin.

In Brown's words

These are parts of the same movement, which has been used for giving the roller motion in wool-combing machines. The roller to which wheel, F (218), is secured is required to make one third a revolution backward, then two thirds of a revolution forward, when it must stop until another length of combed fiber is ready for delivery. This is accomplished by the grooved heart-cam, C, D, B, e (217), the stud, A, working in the said groove; from C to D it moves the roller backward, and from D to e it moves it forward, the motion being transmitted through the catch, G, to the notch-wheel, F, on the roller-shaft, H. When the stud, A, arrives at the point, e, in the cam, a projection at the back of the wheel which carries the cam strikes the projecting piece on the catch, G, and raises it out of the notch in the wheel, F, so that, while the stud is traveling in the cam from e to C, the catch is passing over the plain surface between the two notches in the wheel, F, without imparting any motion; but when stud, A, arrives at the part, C, the catch has dropped in another notch, and is again ready to move wheel, F, and roller as required.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing and description. Brown shows the cam and the lever on two separate drawings, so how they sit together was made up. The cam is placed behind the lever, with its centre below and to the right of the roller. Brown puts the lifting projection on the back of the cam's wheel; here it is a raised piece on the front face, shaped so the catch lifts and falls at the right moments. The pin it strikes, the spring on the catch, the roller, the shafts and the stands were made up. Brown says the roller goes back a third of a turn and forward two thirds. With as many notches as his drawing shows, the lever cannot swing that far, so here it goes back one notch and forward two, which keeps his one to two share.
