No. 18: Two fixed pulleys and one movable pulley

Two pulleys hang one above the other from the beam. A third pulley below them carries the weight. Three ropes now hold the weight up, so you only pull a third as hard.

How it works

Follow the rope from its tied end on top of the movable block. It goes up over the small fixed pulley, down under the movable pulley, up over the big fixed pulley and down to the winch.

Now count the ropes coming up out of the movable block. There are three, and they share the weight. If it weighs 90 kg, each rope holds 30 kg. The winch only holds one of them, so it pulls 30 kg.

To lift the weight 1 cm, all three ropes must get 1 cm shorter. So the winch must pull in 3 cm of rope. Watch the readouts: the rope pulled is always three times the weight raised.

In Brown's words

Is a combination of two fixed pulleys and one movable pulley.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown leaves the end of the rope hanging; a small winch was added to pull it. The sizes, the ring where the rope is tied and the ground are made up. The pulleys and rope are taken to weigh nothing, and real pulleys rub a little, so a real lift needs a bit more pull than shown here.
