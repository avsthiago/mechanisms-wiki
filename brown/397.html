No. 397: Shuttle drive for a sewing machine

A crank turns steadily, but the shuttle stops for half of every turn. Then it shoots across and back. Sewing machines used this to wait for the needle and then pass the shuttle through the loop of thread.

How it works

The crank turns at a steady speed. Its red pin sits in a curved slot in the lever. The lever can only swing on the pivot at its foot.

Half of the slot is bent in a circle round the crank shaft. While the pin travels round that half, it just slides along the slot and the lever stays still. On the other half of the turn the pin is no longer on that circle. It pulls the lever over to the right and brings it back. The link passes this on to the shuttle driver. So the shuttle waits for half a turn, then goes out and back.

Turn the speed down and watch the readouts. The crank angle keeps rising, but the shuttle reading stays at 0 mm for half of every turn.

In Brown's words

Continuous circular into intermittent rectilinear reciprocating. A motion used on several sewing machines for driving the shuttle. Same motion applied to three-revolution cylinder printing-presses.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The bearing for the crank, the bracket at the foot, the posts holding the bar and all the sizes were made up. The crank is drawn shorter than in Brown's figure. With his longer crank the lever would have to swing so far that the link could not reach the bar. The shuttle itself is left out.
