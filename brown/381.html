No. 381: Joiner's wedge clamp

Two wedges are driven in beside a board. They squeeze it tight against the bed so it cannot slide while the carpenter planes it. No screw or lever is needed.

How it works

The board slides in between the two cheeks. Then a wedge is pushed in on each side. Each wedge slides along the slanted face of a cheek and moves a little closer to the board.

A wedge turns a long push into a small, strong squeeze. The wedge travels a long way along the slope, but it only moves a short way towards the board. The sideways squeeze is much bigger than the push. The grip also holds, because the wedge is wedged hard against the slope.

Watch the wedges stop when they touch the board. Try Narrow, Medium and Wide boards and see how far the wedges have to go each time.

In Brown's words

Bowery's joiner's clamp, plan and transverse section. Oblong bed has, at one end, two wedge-formed cheeks, adjacent sides of which lie at an angle to each other, and are dovetailed inward from upper edge to receive two wedges for clamping the piece or pieces of wood to be planed.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows the plan. The height of the cheeks and the thickness of the board were made up. The dovetail that holds the cheeks to the bed, and the small section drawn above the plan, are left out. The wedges are shown by hand without a hammer.
