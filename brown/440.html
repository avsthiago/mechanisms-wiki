No. 440: Rocking trough

A trough split into two halves rocks on a pin like a seesaw. A steady stream of water fills one half until its weight tips the trough over. The water pours out and the other half moves under the stream. The trough keeps rocking by itself, and it can be used to count water.

How it works

Water falls from the chute onto the high half of the trough. The half has a closed end next to the pin, so the water collects there. As it fills, its weight pulls that side down.

When the weight wins, the trough tips over and stops against a block. The full half is now low and its water runs out of the open end. At the same moment the ridge swings across, so the stream falls into the other half, which is now high.

That half fills and tips in turn, and so on. Each tip delivers about the same amount of water, so counting the tips measures the water. Try a trickle, then a lot, and compare how often it tips.

In Brown's words

Represents a trough divided transversely into equal parts and supported on an axis by a frame beneath. The fall of water filling one side of the division, the trough is vibrated on its axis, and at the same time that it delivers the water the opposite side is brought under the stream and filled, which in like manner produces the vibration of the trough back again. This has been used as a water meter.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The base, the posts, the blocks that stop the trough and the chute are made up so it stands up. The front wall of the trough is made see-through so you can watch the water. The trough's weight and the amount of water needed to tip it are chosen so the rocking works. The water level is worked out from how the water settles in the tilted halves.
