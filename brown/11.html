No. 11: Belt to a shaft at right angles, without guide pulleys

A belt carries turning from one level shaft to another that crosses it at a right angle. The belt simply twists a quarter turn on each side, so no guide pulleys are needed. The catch is that it only works one way round.

How it works

The drum pulls the belt down its front. The belt runs to the right side of the lower pulley and goes round under it. Then it climbs straight up and runs back to the back of the drum.

A belt stays put on a pulley only if it arrives straight, in line with the middle of the rim. Here each run leaves one pulley exactly in line with the middle of the other. That is why the lower pulley sits to one side and out in front. Each run twists a quarter turn on the way.

Try forwards, then backwards. Backwards, each run arrives at a slant, so the belt creeps sideways across the rims and falls off. Switch back before it falls and watch it creep back to the middle. Pick forwards to put a fallen belt back on.

In Brown's words

Another method of effecting the same result as 3, without guide-pulleys.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The sizes, the distance between the shafts and the frame are made up. Brown does not say which pulley drives; here the drum does. How fast the belt creeps sideways is worked out from where each run lands, as if the belt never slid sideways on a rim. A real belt slides a little, so it may creep off more slowly.
