No. 324: Crossed-arm parallel ruler

Two rulers are joined by two arms that cross like a pair of scissors. When the rulers open or close, their edges stay parallel and their ends stay in line. A pencil along the edge draws lines that never meet.

How it works

The two arms are the same length and cross at their middles. When the rulers move apart, the arms open evenly on both sides of the crossing. So the upper ruler goes straight up and never tilts.

The end of each arm that is not pinned slides in a slot. As the arms open they stand more upright, so they reach less far sideways. The pins slide along the slots to allow this.

Try Close spacing, then Wide. Watch the pins slide in the slots while the pencil lines stay parallel.

In Brown's words

Compound parallel ruler, composed of two simple rulers, A, A, connected by two crossed arms pivoted together at the middle of their length, each pivoted at one end to one of the rulers, and connected with the other one by a slot and sliding-pin, as shown at B. In this the ends as well as the edges are kept parallel. The principle of construction of the several rulers represented is taken advantage of in the formation of some parts of machinery.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The sheet of paper and the pencil were added to show the lines. The slot lengths, the arm lengths and the thicknesses are made up. In real use you hold one ruler and push the other; here the lower one stays still.
