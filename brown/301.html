No. 301: Two-wheel escapement, side view

Two toothed wheels stand side by side on one shaft, seen here edge on. A weight tries to turn them. A half disc rocks to and fro between them and lets one tooth past at a time, first from one wheel and then from the other.

How it works

You are looking along the balance shaft. The lowest teeth of both wheels come straight towards you, just above the shaft. The half disc stands across their path. Watch its straight top edge rock like a seesaw. When its right end is up, a tooth of the right wheel runs into the flat face of the disc and stops.

The balance swings back and the right end dips. When the edge drops below the tooth, the tooth moves on over it. The edge is cut on a slope, so the tooth pushes it further down as it slides across. That push keeps the balance swinging. Once the tooth is clear, both wheels jump on until a tooth of the left wheel hits the flat face, where the left end is now up. On the next swing the same thing happens on the left wheel.

The balance wheel and its spring set the pace. Heavy weights make the balance slow to turn, so the beats come further apart. Try Light, then Heavy, and count the beats.

In Brown's words

A clock or watch escapement ; 300 being a front elevation, and 301 a side elevation. The pallet is acted upon by the teeth of one and the other of two escape-wheels alternately.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's two drawings and his description. Brown shows the wheels, the shaft and the half disc. The balance wheel, the balance spring and its post, the posts, the base, the slope on the half disc and all the sizes were made up. The balance wheel, the balance spring and the stand are drawn faint so that the wheels and the half disc show clearly. The weight that turns the wheels is left out. Rubbing between a resting tooth and the disc is left out, and the rubbing that keeps the swing steady is a simple guess.
