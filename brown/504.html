No. 504: Ferguson's paradox

Three thin gears sit on one pin and are driven by the same thick gear. As the arm carries them round, one creeps forwards, one creeps backwards and one does not turn at all. It looks impossible, which is why it is called a paradox.

How it works

The arm swings round the pedestal. The thick gear rolls on the fixed gear, so it spins on its pin. It passes that spin to the three thin gears, turning them backwards on their pin while the arm carries them forwards.

The fixed gear has 20 teeth. The middle gear also has 20, so its backward spin exactly cancels the arm's forward turn. Its red tooth keeps pointing the same way. The upper gear has 21 teeth, so it spins back a little too slowly and ends up creeping forwards. The lower gear has 19, spins back a little too fast, and creeps backwards.

The three red teeth start almost in line. Let the arm go round a few times and watch them drift apart. The upper gear needs 21 turns of the arm to go round once, and the lower gear needs 19 to go round once the other way.

In Brown's words

"Ferguson's mechanical paradox," designed to show a curious property of the epicyclic train. The wheel, A, is fixed upon a stationary stud about which the arm, C, D, revolves. In this arm are two pins, M, N, upon one of which is fitted loosely a thick wheel, B, gearing with A, and upon the other are three loose wheels, E, F, G, all gearing with B. When the arm, C, D, is turned round on the stud, motion is given to the three wheels, E, F, G, on their common axis, viz., the pin, N ; the three forming with the intermediate wheel, B, and the wheel, A, three distinct epicyclic trains. Suppose A to have twenty teeth, F twenty, E twenty-one, and G nineteen ; as the arm, E, C, D, is turned round, F will appear not to turn on its axis, as any point in its circumference will always point in one direction, while E will appear to turn slowly in one and G in the other direction, which—an apparent paradox—gave rise to the name of the apparatus.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing, with the tooth numbers he gives. The three thin gears are all the same size, so their teeth are very slightly different in size. The real toy was made the same way. The number of teeth on the thick gear, the red teeth and stripes, and the sizes were made up.
