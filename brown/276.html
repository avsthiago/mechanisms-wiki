No. 276: Constant-width cam

A three-lobed cam turns on a shaft. Two pins on a bar touch it, one on each side. The cam is the same size across its centre in every direction, so both pins stay touching and the bar slides to and fro.

How it works

The cam turns on a fixed shaft. The two pins on the bar are always the same distance apart. Measured straight across the centre of the cam, in any direction, the cam is that same size. So both pins keep touching it.

When a lobe points at one pin, the other pin touches the waist on the far side. The bar is pushed toward the lobe. The bar slides to and fro three times for every turn of the cam.

Try the three lobe depths. Deeper lobes make the bar slide further. The pins still touch the cam every time.

In Brown's words

Continuous rotary motion of the cam gives a reciprocating rectilinear motion to the bar. The cam is of equal diameter in every direction measured across its center.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The shape is a three-lobed outline whose size across the centre is the same in every direction, so the pins can touch it exactly. The pins are small so they fit the waists. The slot for the shaft, the back plate, the guides and the length of the bar were made up. The lobe choices change how deep the lobes are.
