No. 327: Roller guide for a piston rod

Two small wheels on the cross-head roll along straight bars instead of sliding. They keep the piston rod running straight and rub much less than a sliding block would. It was used on small engines in France.

How it works

The cross-head has a wheel at each end. The two wheels run between two straight bars. As the connecting rod pushes the cross-head up and down, the wheels roll along the bars.

A rolling wheel does not rub. The wheel on the left rolls on its left side and the wheel on the right rolls on its right side, so they turn in opposite ways. The rod leans a little, and the bars take its sideways push.

The wheels turn once each time the cross-head travels as far as the distance round the rim. Try a short throw, then a long one. Watch the stripes and the number of turns. Choose Closed to see the cylinder as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Differs from 326 in having rollers substituted for the slides on the cross-head, said rollers working against straight guide-bars, A, A, attached to the frame. This is used for small engines in France.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows the rod, the cross-head with its rollers and the bars. The flywheel is drawn behind. The frame, the cross-beam, the bearing, the crank arm, the cylinder with its cover and all the sizes were made up. The cylinder is see-through so the piston can be seen. The rollers turn at exactly the rate needed to roll without slipping.
