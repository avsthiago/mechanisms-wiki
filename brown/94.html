No. 94: Variable crank

Two plates turn together on one shaft, and a bolt runs through both. One plate has slots that point out from the middle. The other has a spiral groove. Turn one plate a little against the other and the bolt slides in or out, so the crank gets longer or shorter while it keeps turning.

How it works

The bolt goes through a slot in the front plate and through the groove in the back plate. The slot is a straight line out from the middle. The groove is a spiral that winds outward. The bolt can only sit where the two cross.

Here the back plate turns slowly against the front plate. Watch the dark tab slide past the blue tab. The crossing moves along the slot, and the bolt goes with it. The bolt is the crank pin, so its distance from the middle is the length of the crank. The slider moves twice that distance each turn.

When the two plates are held together, the crank has one fixed length. Try Held, short crank, then Held, long crank, and compare the stroke of the slider.

In Brown's words

Variable crank, two circular plates revolving on the same center. In one a spiral groove is cut; in the other a series of slots radiating from the center. On turning one of these plates around its center, the bolt shown near the bottom of the figure, and which passes through the spiral groove and radial slots, is caused to move toward or from the center of the plates.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows the plates and the bolt only. The shaft, the stand, the rod and the slider were made up to show what the crank does. The spiral makes one turn here, where the drawing shows more, so the bolt always meets the groove in one place. The slow turning of the spiral plate is added to show the adjustment. How the two plates are held against each other is not shown.
