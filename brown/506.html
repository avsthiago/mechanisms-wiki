No. 506: Epicyclic train with both ends moving

One crank drives two sets of gears in opposite directions at nearly the same speed. An arm between them, carrying two small gears, picks up only what is left over. So a fast crank gives a very slow, strong turn of the arm.

How it works

Turn the crank. One of its gears drives the big gear at the top, the other the big gear below. They are driven from opposite edges, so they turn opposite ways. Each carries a smaller gear that faces the arm in the middle.

The pair of gears on the arm is caught between those two. If the upper and lower gears pushed it equally, the pair would only spin and the arm would stand still. The two pushes are not quite equal, so the arm creeps round at the small difference. That is the sum of two motions, one of them backwards.

Brown gives another way to connect it. Fix the big upper gear to the upright shaft, so it turns the arm directly, and leave the small upper gear loose. Now the motions add instead of cancelling, and the small upper gear spins fastest of all. Try both and compare the readouts.

In Brown's words

Another epicyclic train in which neither the first nor last wheel is fixed. m, n, is a shaft to which is firmly secured the train-bearing arm, k, l, which carries the two wheels, d, e, secured together, but rotating upon the arm itself. The wheels, b and c, are united and turn together, freely upon the shaft, m, n; the wheels, f and g, are also secured together, but turn together freely on the shaft, m, n. The wheels, c, d, e and f, constitute an epicyclic train of which c is the first and f the last wheel. A shaft, A, is employed as a driver, and has firmly secured to it two wheels, a and h, the first of which gears with the wheel, b, and thus communicates motion to the first wheel, c, of the epicyclic train, and the wheel, h, drives the wheel, g, which thus gives motion to the last wheel, f. Motion communicated in this way to the two ends of the train produces an aggregate motion of the arm, k, l, and shaft, m, n. This train may be modified ; for instance, suppose the wheels, g and f, to be disunited, g to be fixed to the shaft, m, n, and f only running loose upon it. The driving-shaft, A, will as before communicate motion to the first wheel, c, of the epicyclic train by means of the wheels, a and b, and will also by h cause the wheel, g, the shaft, m, n, and the train-bearing arm, k, l, to revolve, and the aggregate rotation will be given to the loose wheel, f.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The drawing shows the arm set a little above the driving shaft; here they are level, and the gears in the middle are a little smaller so the arm can swing past the driving shaft's gears. The shape of the frame, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The model does not show the pin or screw that would join the big upper gear to the small one or to the shaft.
