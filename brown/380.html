No. 380: Cramp drill with a hollow feed screw

A C-shaped frame holds a hand drill over a plate. The drill shaft runs right through the middle of a hollow screw. Turning the screw pushes the drill down into the work.

How it works

Turn the crank and the drill shaft spins. At the same time the wing handle turns slowly. The handle turns the screw, and the screw moves down through the upper arm. The drill sinks with it until the bit cuts into the plate.

The shaft goes through the middle of the screw, so the drill and the screw share one line. The pushing force goes straight down the shaft and does not bend the frame. The screw turns in the frame, and the shaft spins inside the screw, so the two turn at different speeds. When the hole is through, the screw winds back and a fresh plate is put in.

Try Light, Medium and Heavy. A heavy bite makes the screw turn faster for each turn of the crank, so the hole gets deeper sooner.

In Brown's words

Portable cramp drills. In 379 the feed-screw is opposite the drill, and in 380 the drill spindle passes through the center of the feed-screw.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The thread, the collar and the drill bit are drawn simply, and all the sizes were made up. In real use a person turns both the crank and the handle. Here the handle turns by itself at the right speed to match the crank. The plate lies still on the lower arm and is replaced when the hole is through.
