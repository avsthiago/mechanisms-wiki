No. 2: Pulleys and a crossed belt

Two pulleys are joined by a belt that crosses over itself between them. The crossing makes the upper pulley turn the opposite way to the lower one. It is a simple way to make a shaft run backwards.

How it works

The belt leaves the lower pulley on one side and reaches the upper pulley on the other side. So it pulls the upper pulley round the opposite way. The two halves of the belt rub where they cross.

The belt does not slip, so both rims move just as fast as the belt. A pulley twice as wide turns half as fast.

Try the upper pulley at half size, then twice the size. Watch where the belt crosses. It moves closer to the smaller pulley.

In Brown's words

Differs from 1 in the substitution of a crossed belt for the open one. In this case the direction of rotation of the pulleys is reversed. By arranging three pulleys, side by side, upon the shaft to be driven, the middle one fast and the other two loose upon it, and using both an open and a crossed belt, the direction of the said shaft is enabled to be reversed without stopping or reversing the driver. One belt will always run on the fast pulley, and the other on one of the loose pulleys. The shaft will be driven in one direction or the other, according as the open or crossed belt is on the fast pulley.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The sizes, the distance between the shafts and the frame behind are made up. Brown draws both pulleys the same size; the other two sizes were added to show the speed change. His text also describes three pulleys and two belts for running a shaft either way; that is not shown here.
