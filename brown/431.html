No. 431: Undershot water-wheel

A stream runs under the wheel and pushes the flat boards at the bottom. The wheel turns with the stream. No hill is needed, only a river that keeps flowing.

How it works

On the left, a deep pool is held back by a gate. Water squeezes out under the gate and runs fast down the stream. The floats at the bottom of the wheel dip into it.

The stream pushes each float along as it passes. That pushes the whole wheel round. The wheel turns more slowly than the water, because it takes some of the push for itself.

Try Low flow, then High flow. Watch the gate rise and the wheel speed up.

In Brown's words

Undershot water-wheel.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The stone bed, the back wall that holds the shaft, the gate frame and the water are made up. The wheel speed is chosen to look right: it moves at about half the speed of the stream, and the gate and handles move together.
