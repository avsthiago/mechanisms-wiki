No. 75: Pawl on a swinging bar

A rod moves up and down, and a toothed wheel turns round in steps. A bar on the wheel's shaft swings to and fro, and a finger on its end pushes the teeth. The wheel only ever turns one way.

How it works

The crank at the bottom moves the rod up and down. The rod rocks the bar on the wheel's shaft. The long arm of the bar swings to and fro over the top of the wheel.

When the arm swings forward, the pawl on its end pushes a tooth and the wheel turns with it. When the arm swings back, the pawl slides up the slanted teeth and drops into a new gap. The holding pawl on the fixed pin stops the wheel from being dragged back.

The wheel only moves on the forward swing, so it turns in steps. Try Short. The pawl never gets past the next tooth, so the wheel stops after one push. Then try Medium and Long, and count the teeth for each stroke.

In Brown's words

Reciprocating rectilinear motion of the rod, C, transmits an intermittent circular motion to the wheel, A, by means of the pawl, B, at the end of the vibrating-bar, D.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows the rod cut off below the wheel, so a small crank was added to move it. The plate, the base, the pins, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The pawl and holding pawl are drawn as straight fingers instead of Brown's curved ones.
