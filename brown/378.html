No. 378: Pendulum saw

A heavy weight on a long rod swings to and fro, like a swing. A cord ties the rod to a saw, so each swing pulls the saw along. The saw cuts a lying log a little deeper with every stroke.

How it works

The rod swings from the top of the stand. As it swings right it lets the cord go. As it swings left it pulls the cord, and the saw is pulled left with it. The weights on the ropes pull the saw back again.

A pendulum swings at a pace set only by how long it is. A longer pendulum swings more slowly. A small push at each swing keeps it going. Here the push is built in, so no person is needed. Each stroke lets the saw bite a little deeper.

Slide the block up the rod, then down, and count the strokes a minute. A block that is higher up makes a shorter pendulum. It swings faster, so the saw makes more strokes and the cut deepens sooner.

In Brown's words

Saw for cutting trees by motion of pendulum, is represented as cutting a lying tree.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The swing is worked out from the real motion of a pendulum, with a small push at each swing built in in place of a person. The stand, the frame, the weights, the sizes and the speed of the cut were made up. The frame is simpler than the drawing, and the saw hangs from ropes with no guide frame round it.
