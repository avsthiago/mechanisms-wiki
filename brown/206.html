No. 206: Lever with two pawls

A lever rocks to and fro above a toothed wheel. It carries two pawls, small catches that fit between the teeth. One pawl pushes the wheel on the way up and the other on the way down, so the wheel turns on every swing.

How it works

A crank at the top rocks the lever to and fro. The lever swings about the pin above the wheel, and the curved plate on it swings too. A pawl hangs from each end of the plate, with its tip resting on the teeth.

As the lever swings one way, one pawl pushes against the steep face of a tooth and drives the wheel round. The other pawl slides up the long slope of the teeth and drops into the next gap. When the lever swings back, the two swap jobs. So the wheel is driven on the way up and on the way down.

Try the three lever swings. With a small swing each pawl only just reaches the next tooth, so the wheel creeps. With a big swing it jumps several teeth on every swing. Watch which pawl is pushing each time.

In Brown's words

A continuous circular movement of the ratchet-wheel, produced by the vibration of the lever carrying two pawls, one of which engages the ratchet-teeth in rising and the other in falling.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown draws the pawls as teeth on the ends of the curved plate and shows only a piece of the lever. Here each pawl is a short arm hinged to the plate, so it can lift over the teeth. The crank, rod, back plate, base, pins, tooth count and sizes were made up.
