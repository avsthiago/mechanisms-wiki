No. 285: Lathe tailstock

This is the sliding head of a lathe. Turn the hand wheel and a screw turns inside it. The screw pushes the spindle, with its pointed centre, in or out. The centre holds the end of the work being turned.

How it works

The hand wheel is fixed to a screw. The screw can turn but it cannot slide. The spindle is a hollow tube with a nut in its inner end, and the nut grips the thread of the screw.

The spindle is held so that it cannot turn. So when the screw turns, the nut has to slide along it, and the whole spindle slides too. One turn of the wheel moves the spindle only a small, fixed way. That is why you can set the centre point very exactly.

Choose wind out and watch the red centre point move out. Then choose wind in. The spindle stops at each end of its travel, so the wheel stops too. Choose Closed to see the tailstock as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

The movable head of a turning lathe. By turning the wheel to the right, motion is communicated to the screw, producing rectilinear motion of the spindle in the end of which the center is fixed.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The front half of the casting is cut away so you can see inside, as in the picture. The clamp on top and the clamp bolt below are drawn but do not move. The bed, the way the spindle is kept from turning, the hand wheel's spokes and all the sizes were made up.
