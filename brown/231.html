No. 231: Drag link

Two cranks on separate shafts are joined by a link. When one shaft turns, the link drags the other crank all the way round with it. The second shaft turns faster and slower than the first.

How it works

Turn the first shaft and watch the link. It is tied to the end of each crank, so when one pin goes round, the other pin has to follow. The second crank is dragged round with it, a whole turn each time.

The two shafts are side by side, not in a line. So the link pulls the second crank at a changing angle. When the pull is nearly along the crank, it turns slowly. When the pull is sideways, it turns quickly. The Driven reading goes up and down while the Driving reading stays the same.

Try the gap slider. With no gap, the shafts are in a line and both turn at the same speed. The larger the gap, the more the second shaft speeds up and slows down.

In Brown's words

Drag-link motion. Circular motion is transmitted from one crank to the other.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown draws the shafts in the air, so no bearings are drawn. The cranks and link lie in separate flat layers so they pass each other, and the pins are made long to join them. All lengths are made up, chosen so that both cranks can go right round. Brown shows one fixed gap, and the slider is added.
