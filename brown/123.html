No. 123: Double rack and toothed halves

A frame with a toothed bar on each side slides up and down. Two half-wheels take turns to be pushed by the bars, and they turn one centre gear. The centre gear always goes round the same way, so straight pushing and pulling becomes steady turning.

How it works

The frame slides up and down. Its two toothed bars face two half-wheels, one on each side. A half-wheel has teeth along only part of its curved edge, so it grips a bar for part of each turn and then lets go.

When the frame rises, the left half-wheel is pushed round. Its teeth run out and the frame stops for a moment. Then the right half-wheel's first tooth meets the other bar, and the frame starts to come down. Now the right half-wheel is pushed round the same way. A small gear fixed behind each half-wheel turns the centre gear, so the centre gear never stops and never turns back.

Slow the speed right down. Follow the red block as the frame goes up and down, and watch the red stripe on the centre gear. The frame changes direction every time, but the stripe never does.

In Brown's words

Intended as a substitute for the crank. Reciprocating rectilinear motion of the double rack gives a continuous rotary motion to the center gear. The teeth on the rack act upon those of the two semi-circular toothed sectors, and the spur-gears attached to the sectors operate upon the center gear. The two stops on the rack shown by dotted lines are caught by the curved piece on the center gear, and lead the toothed sectors alternately into gear with the double rack.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The two stops on the rack and the curved piece on the centre gear are left out. Here the centre gear keeps the two half-wheels in step, and the teeth are lined up by calculation, so each half-wheel meets its bar after the other has let go. The stand, the guide blocks, the end bars, the sizes and the numbers of teeth were made up. The frame stops and turns round in an instant at each end, which a real one would only manage with a heavy wheel to carry it through. The teeth are left out of the ends of each half-wheel so that they never clash with the bars.
