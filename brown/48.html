No. 48: Clutch box with loose gear

A gear turns all the time, but the shaft it sits on stays still. Push a sliding clutch against the gear and the shaft starts turning with it. The engine never has to stop.

How it works

A small gear, called a pinion, turns the big gear all the time. The gear is loose on the shaft, so the shaft stays still. Half of a clutch is fixed to the side of the gear and turns with it.

The other half slides along the shaft on a long key. It cannot turn on the shaft, only slide. Push it in and its jaws fit between the jaws of the turning half. Now the gear, the clutch and the shaft all turn together. The big gear turns the other way to the pinion, so its speed shows a minus sign.

Try In and watch the jaws. They wait tip to tip until the gaps come round, then drop in, and the shaft jumps to full speed at once. Try Out and the shaft slowly runs down.

In Brown's words

Clutch-box. The pinion at the top gives a continuous rotary motion to the gear below, to which is attached half the clutch, and both turn loosely on the shaft. When it is desired to give motion to the shaft, the other part of the clutch, which slides upon a key or feather fixed in the shaft, is thrust into gear by the lever.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown drew rounded teeth on the clutch; square jaws are used here. In the drawing the pinion seems to sit inside the edge of the big gear, so here it meshes on the upper front side of the gear, which looks the same from the front. The sizes, the tooth counts, the stand and the handle were made up.
