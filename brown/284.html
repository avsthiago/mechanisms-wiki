No. 284: Saw-bed feed

This moves the bed of a sawing machine forward a little at a time, so the log on it is fed to the saw. A turning crank rocks a lever. A pawl on the lever pulls a toothed wheel round a tooth or two at each rock.

How it works

The crank goes round and its rod pulls the level arm of the bell crank down, then pushes it up. So the upright arm swings left and right. The pawl hangs from that arm. When the arm swings left, the pawl is pulled up and its tip drags a tooth of the wheel with it.

When the arm swings back, the tip slides down over the sloping backs of the teeth and drops in behind a new one. The holding pawl on the left keeps the wheel from going back with it. The small gear on the wheel's shaft rolls the rack, so the bed moves on a little after every turn of the crank.

The screw sets how far below the hinge the pawl hangs. Lower down, the arm swings farther, so the pawl drags more teeth each time. Try the short setting and watch the pawl slide back and forth without moving the wheel. Then try the long one.

In Brown's words

Represents a feeding apparatus for the bed of a sawing machine. By the revolution of the crank at the lower part of the figure, alternate motion is communicated to the horizontal arm of the bell crank lever whose fulcrum is at a, near the top left-hand corner of the figure. By this means motion is communicated to the catch attached to the vertical arm of the lever, and the said catch communicates motion to the ratchet-wheel, upon the shaft of which is a toothed pinion, working in the rack attached to the side of the carriage. The feed is varied by a screw in the bell-crank lever.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The frame, the ways the bed slides on, the studs and the number of teeth were made up. Brown's screw is read as one that raises and lowers the pawl pin in a slot in the upright arm. The thing that turns the crank is left out. When the bed reaches the end of its run, the model puts it back at the start.
