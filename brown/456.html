No. 456: Cary's rotary pump

A drum turns inside a round casing and carries two sliding bars, called pistons. A fixed heart-shaped track slides each piston out to sweep water round, and pulls it back in to pass a block at the bottom. The water is pushed up a side passage and out of a spout.

How it works

Look at the two green pistons. They turn with the orange drum. Each has a pin on its inner end, and the pin runs in the groove of the yellow cam. The cam stands still, so the groove steers each pin as it goes round.

Over the top the groove is far from the shaft. There the piston is pushed out until it touches the casing, and it sweeps the water along in front of it, shown as blue dots. Behind it the space grows and new water is drawn up the pipe on the left.

At the bottom a block fills the space between drum and casing, so the water must leave by the passage on the right. There the groove dips in towards the shaft and pulls the piston back into the drum. Follow the red dot. The piston slides in to pass the block, then out again on the other side. Choose Closed to see the pump as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Cary's rotary pump. Within the fixed cylinder there is placed a revolving drum, B, attached to an axle, A. Heart-shaped cam, a, surrounding axle, is also fixed. Revolution of drum causes sliding-pistons, c, c, to move in and out in obedience to form of cam. Water enters and is removed from the chamber through ports, L and M ; the directions are indicated by arrows. Cam is so placed that each piston is, in succession, forced back to its seat when opposite E, and at same time other piston is forced fully against inner side of chamber, thus driving before it water already there into exit-pipe, H, and drawing after it through suction-pipe, F, the stream of supply.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The front cover is left off, and the water is drawn as small blue balls so you can see it move. Brown shows a heart cam with the pistons bearing on it. Here it is drawn as a groove, so that it can pull the pistons in as well as push them out. The groove dips more gently than in the drawing, so a piston starts to slide in sooner than Brown shows. The exact shapes of the cam, the block and the passages, and all the sizes, were made up.
