No. 459: Wind-driven well lift

A wind wheel turns a screw that hangs between two toothed wheels. The screw drives one wheel at a time, so one bucket is wound up while the other goes down. Each bucket that reaches the top knocks a lever, which swings the screw over to the other wheel and sends the buckets back the other way.

How it works

Look at the screw between the two orange wheels. It leans towards one of them, and its thread pushes that wheel's pegs down. The wheel turns its pulley and winds the rope. One bucket comes up full and the other goes down empty.

Near the top the bucket catches on a hook, tips over and pours into the trough. It also lifts one arm of the yellow lever, called the tappet. When the tappet's heavy upper arm passes upright it falls over to the other side. Its red pin pushes the purple step across, and the screw swings over to the other wheel.

The wind wheel never changes direction. But the screw now pushes the other wheel's pegs down, on the opposite side of the rope, so the rope runs back the other way. Watch the tappet each time a bucket arrives. Then make the wind stronger with the speed slider and see the buckets change over sooner.

In Brown's words

Reciprocating lift for wells. Top part represents horizontal wind-wheel on a shaft which carries spiral thread. Coupling of latter allows small vibration, that it may act on one worm-wheel at a time. Behind worm-wheels are pulleys over which passes rope which carries bucket at each extremity. In center is vibrating tappet, against which bucket strikes in its ascent, and which, by means of arm in step wherein spiral and shaft are supported, traverses spiral from one wheel to other so that the bucket which has delivered water is lowered and other one raised.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown does not show the well, how the bucket tips, or how the tappet finishes its swing once the screw has let go. Here a hook on each trough tips the bucket, the upper arm of the tappet is heavy and falls over by its own weight, and its pin has some free play in the step. The screw also nudges a wheel a little as it slides in between two pegs. The well, the troughs, the back board, the number of pegs and all the sizes were made up.
