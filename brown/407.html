No. 407: Drawing pointed arches

A springy wooden strip is bent into a curve by a cord. A pencil run along the bent strip draws the curve. This gives a smooth pointed arch with no compass, however wide the opening is.

How it works

The strip stands upright at its foot, held by a small block. The cord pulls straight down on its other end. Watch the strip bow over to the right as the slide moves out along the slot.

A springy strip bends most where the pull is strongest, and that is near its foot. So it bends smoothly all the way along and never kinks. The pencil on its end draws that smooth curve on the paper.

The slide can stop anywhere along the slot. Try Narrow, then Wide, and see how the arch gets lower and flatter as it gets wider.

In Brown's words

Instrument for describing pointed arches. Horizontal bar is slotted and fitted with a slide having pin for loop of cord. Arch bar of elastic wood is fixed in horizontal at right angles. Horizontal bar is placed with upper on and back of arch bar with I edge springing line, ranging jamb of opening, and the latter bar is bent till the upper side meets apex of arch, fulcrum-piece at its base insuring its retaining tangential relation to jamb ; the pencil is secured to arched bar at its connection with cord.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The paper behind, the wall and the pencil were added to show the line. Brown fixes the pencil to the strip, so here it is simply run along the strip once it is bent. The strip bends as a real springy strip does, found by working out its shape for each slide position. Brown does not say how the cord is kept the right length, so here it simply gets shorter as the strip bends. The sizes are made up.
