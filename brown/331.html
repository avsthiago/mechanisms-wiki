No. 331: Slotted cross-head engine

A crank pin works in a slot cut across the cross-head. The pin slides along the slot while it pushes the cross-head up and down. The cross-head runs between two upright pillars, so it can only move straight.

How it works

The crank pin goes round in a circle. The slot is just wide enough for the pin. The pin can slide to and fro along the slot, but it pushes the cross-head up and down.

There is no connecting rod, so nothing leans and nothing pushes the cross-head sideways. The cross-head goes fastest when the pin is level with the shaft. It stops for a moment when the pin is at its highest and lowest points.

Try a short throw, then a long one. Watch how far the cross-head goes, and how far the pin slides along the slot. Choose Closed to see the cylinder as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

An engine with crank motion like that represented in 93 and 279 of this table, the crank-wrist journal working in a slotted cross-head, A. This cross-head works between the pillar guides, D, D, of the engine framing.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The cylinder, its cover, the base, the beam that carries the shaft, the crank arm and all the sizes were made up. The crossed braces between the pillars are left out so the crank can be seen. The cylinder is see-through so the piston can be seen.
