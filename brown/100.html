No. 100: Quick return crank

A round crank turns at a steady speed, and its pin slides in a long slot in a swinging arm. The arm pushes a ram down slowly and pulls it back up fast. A shaping machine cuts on the slow stroke and wastes as little time as possible on the way back.

How it works

The crank turns round at a steady speed. Its red pin slides along the slot and drags the arm from side to side. The short end of the arm moves the ram up and down through the link.

The pin spends most of each turn on the far side of the crank, away from the pivot. There it takes a long time to swing the arm. This is the slow cutting stroke. Only a short part of each turn is spent on the near side. There the arm swings quickly and the ram springs back. The crank never changes speed, only the arm does.

A longer crank makes the arm swing farther and the two strokes more unequal. Try Short, then Long, and watch how the speed readout changes between the two strokes.

In Brown's words

Quick return crank motion, applicable to shaping machines.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows only the crank, the slotted arm and the start of a rod. The sizes, the backing plate, the link, the ram, its rails and the block of metal were made up. The ram is drawn upright so the link pushes it straight. A real shaping machine lays it on its side. The shorter and longer cranks were added for comparison.
