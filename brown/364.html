No. 364: Rollers in oblique grooves

A small wheel with rollers on spokes drives a big drum whose shaft is at right angles to its own. The rollers catch on slanting ribs across the drum's rim. The drum turns in steps, with a short rest between them, while the small wheel turns steadily.

How it works

The small wheel turns, and each roller in turn swings up past the drum. Near the middle of its swing, the roller dips in among the ribs on the drum's rim. Then it swings out again, and the next roller comes along.

The ribs slant across the rim. As a roller climbs, it presses on the side of the rib next to it. A slanting rib can only get out of the way by moving sideways, so the drum turns a little. When the roller rolls off the top of the rib, the push stops and the drum rests until the next roller catches the next rib. The rollers turn as they rub along the ribs, so there is less wear.

Watch the drum readout jump between a speed and zero. Count the steps: there is one for every roller that passes, so one turn of the small wheel moves the drum by eight ribs.

In Brown's words

Intermittent rotary motion from continuous rotary motion about an axis at right angles. Small wheel on left is driver ; and the friction rollers on its radial studs work against the faces of oblique grooves or projections across the face of the larger wheel, and impart motion thereto.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The number of ribs, their slant and depth, all the sizes, the bearings, the post, the base and the bracket holding the drum shaft were made up. In a real machine something like a brake would hold the drum still between steps; here it simply stays where the last roller left it.
