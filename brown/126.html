No. 126: Bell-crank lever

A rope pulls straight down. A lever bent at a corner, called a bell crank, turns that pull into a pull sideways. This is how a pull can be sent round a corner, for example from a pedal to a brake far away.

How it works

The upper arm of the bell crank is tied to the rope that hangs from the pulley. The lower arm is tied to a second rope that runs sideways. The lever turns on a fixed pin at its corner.

When the hanging rope is pulled down, the upper arm goes down and the lever turns. The lower arm swings to the right and pulls the sideways rope. A long arm moves further than a short arm, so the two ropes do not move the same distance.

Try Short, then Long, and watch the readouts. A longer lower arm pulls the sideways rope further. The hanging rope moves the same distance as before.

In Brown's words

A bell-crank lever, used for changing the direction of any force.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The shaft, the posts behind the pulley and the lever pin, and the base were made up. The ropes are drawn thick and run on past the edge, as in the drawing. The short and long lower arms were added for comparison.
