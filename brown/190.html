No. 190: Screw clamp

Turning a handle makes a screw climb. The screw lifts one end of a holder, so the other end swings down and presses on a block of wood. A small turn of the handle holds the wood with a big force.

How it works

Watch the handle go round. The screw climbs a little with each turn, because its thread works like a ramp that winds round and round. It pushes up under the right end of the holder.

The holder tilts on a pin in the upright post, like a seesaw. When the right end goes up, the left end goes down and its round foot presses on the block. Once the foot touches the wood, the screw cannot climb any more and the handle stops. Turning back lowers the screw and the holder lifts off again.

Try Thin, then Thick. A thin block needs many more turns before it is held, and the holder tilts much further.

In Brown's words

A screw-clamp. On turning the handle the screw thrusts upward against the holder, which, operating as a lever, holds down the piece of wood or other material placed under it on the other side of its fulcrum.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows the bench, the post, the holder, the screw with its crank, and the block. The thread, the collar under the holder, the nut block in the bench, the round foot on the left end, the windows in the holder, the pin heads and the choice of block thickness were made up. The model starts clamped, as drawn, then loosens and tightens again. The holder rests on the collar by its own weight.
