No. 201: Irregular gear, arm and rod

A lopsided gear turns a small gear that rides on the end of a long swinging arm. As the lopsided gear turns, it pushes the small gear out and lets it back in, so the arm rocks. The rocking arm lifts and lowers a rod, while a belt carries the small gear's turning down to a big wheel.

How it works

The lopsided gear turns at a steady speed. Its shaft is near one edge, so its teeth are close to the shaft on one side and far away on the other. The small gear must stay in mesh, so it is pushed out and comes back once in every turn.

The small gear rides on the long arm, so the arm rocks to and fro. The short arm rocks with it and lifts and lowers the rod through the pin in its slot. The small gear also speeds up and slows down, because the far teeth move faster than the near ones. The belt passes that turning down to the big wheel, whose centre is the arm's pivot, so the belt stays tight.

Try Round, off centre, then Oval, centred. The shape of the gear sets the pattern: the round one rocks the arm once in a turn, the centred oval twice.

In Brown's words

A continual rotation of the pinion (obtained through the irregular shaped gear at the left) gives a variable vibrating movement to the horizontal arm, and a variable reciprocating movement to the rod, A.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown does not say what the big wheel drives or what turns the lopsided gear. The gear's exact outline, the numbers of teeth, the backing plate, the guide for the rod and all the sizes were made up. The small wheel behind the small gear is made smaller than drawn, so it clears the gear's shaft. The other two gear shapes were added for comparison.
