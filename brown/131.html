No. 131: Slotted arm and toothed sector

A turning disc has a pin that slides in a long slot. The slot is cut in an arm that rocks to and fro. The arm carries a part of a gear, called a sector. It pushes a straight bar with gear teeth, called a rack, from side to side.

How it works

The disc turns round and round. Its red pin slides along the slot and pushes the arm to the left, then to the right. The arm and the sector rock together, and the sector's teeth push the teeth of the rack.

The disc turns all the way round, but the arm only swings to and fro. So the rack does not turn. It slides one way, then back. The farther the pin sits from the middle of the disc, the wider the arm swings, and the longer the rack slides.

Try Near, then Far. Watch the red band on the rod, and see how far it travels each time.

In Brown's words

On rotating the disk carrying the crank-pin working in the slotted arm, reciprocating rectilinear motion is imparted to the rack at the bottom by the vibration of the toothed sector.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The backing plate, the base, the pivot pin, the two blocks that hold the rod, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The windows in the sector are left out. The choice of pin distance was added to show what it changes.
