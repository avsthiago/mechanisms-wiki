No. 417: Bent shaft and rocking rod

Turning a handle makes a slide move back and forth in a straight line. The end of the shaft is bent, and the bent end wobbles round in a cone. A rod hung on it passes that wobble down to the slide.

How it works

Turn the handle and watch the bent end of the shaft. It points down and to the left, then round to the front, then up, then to the back. The sleeve on it goes round with it, but it does not spin with the shaft.

When the bent end points down, the sleeve is low and to the right, and the rod stands nearly upright. Half a turn later the end points up, and the rod leans far over to the left. The rod's foot can only go along the slide, so the slide is pushed left and pulled back right once each turn.

The more the end is bent, the further the sleeve swings and the longer the slide's stroke. Try a little bend, then the bend as drawn, and compare the stroke.

In Brown's words

Continuous circular motion into a rectilinear reciprocating. The shaft, A, working in a fixed bearing, D, is bent on one end, and fitted to turn in a socket at the upper end of a rod, B, the lower end of which works in a socket in the slide, C. Dotted lines show the position of the rod, B, and slide, when the shaft has made half a revolution from the position shown in bold lines.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The rod's foot slides a little way in and out of its ball as it rocks, because a rod fixed at both ends could not follow the bent end all the way round; Brown's drawing does not show how deep the socket is. The guides for the slide, the base and the collars on the shaft were made up.
