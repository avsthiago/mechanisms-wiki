No. 480: Gasometer with a guide tube

A floating bell stores gas over water, rising as gas comes in and sinking as it goes out. A tube fixed to the bell slides over a standing tube in the tank. The standing tube keeps the bell straight and upright, so it cannot tilt or jam.

How it works

Gas goes into the bell through one pipe, so the bell rises. When gas leaves through the other pipe, the bell sinks. Its open rim always stays under the water, which seals the gas in.

The bell has no weights, so its own weight squeezes the gas. Look at the water inside the bell. It sits lower than the water outside, and the gap shows the squeeze. A narrow channel between the two tubes holds water at the outside level.

The tube on the bell slides on the standing tube, so the bell can only move straight up and down. Try a light bell, then a heavy one. The heavy bell squeezes the gas harder and pushes the water inside lower. Choose Closed to see the gasometer as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Another kind of gasometer. The vessel, A, has permanently secured within it a central tube, a, which slides on a fixed tube, b, in the center of the tank.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The tank is round and cut open at the front so you can see inside. The sizes, the gap between the two tubes and the three bell weights are made up. The water outside is kept at one level, and the gas dots only show how much gas is in the bell.
