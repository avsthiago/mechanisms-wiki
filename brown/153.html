No. 153: Studs, bar and bell crank

Studs on a turning disc push a bar to one side. A lever bent at a corner, called a bell crank, then pushes the bar back. So a steady turn becomes a push to and fro. Machines use it to move a slide.

How it works

The disc turns clockwise. A stud comes up on the left of the block under the bar. It pushes the block, and the bar slides to the right.

Half a turn later the other stud meets the knob on the lower arm of the bell crank. It knocks the knob up. The lever turns, and its upper arm pushes the red pin on the bar back to the left.

So each stud gives the bar one push right and one push left. Watch the red pin to see where the bar is. Press play and follow one stud all the way round.

In Brown's words

Circular motion into alternating rectilinear motion. The studs on the rotating disk strike the projection on the under side of the horizontal bar, moving it one direction. The return motion is given by means of the bell-crank or elbow-lever, one arm of which is operated upon by the next stud, and the other strikes the stud on the front of the horizontal bar.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown gives no sizes, so the sizes, the two studs, the back plate and the base were made up. A round knob was added behind the lower arm so the studs can pass behind the lever. The lever pushes the bar back only part of the way, so the bar settles between two end points after the first push. The model starts in the drawn position.
