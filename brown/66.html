No. 66: Jumping weight

A worm wheel turns slowly and steadily. A heavy ball on an arm is carried up with it, then falls over the top and swings down on its own. A slow, steady drive gives a sudden, quick move.

How it works

The worm turns the wheel slowly, and the wheel's shaft turns with it. A pin on that shaft pushes the edge of the hollow shaft. So the weight is lifted slowly up the right side.

Once the ball passes over the top, its own weight pulls it forward. It falls down the left side much faster than the pin can follow. The cut in the hollow shaft lets it fall half a turn, until the far edge of the cut stops it at the bottom. Then it hangs still until the pin comes round and lifts it again.

Slow the speed right down and watch the pin: after each fall it has to travel half a turn before it touches the hollow shaft again.

In Brown's words

A modification of 64; a weight, D, attached to an arm secured in the shaft of the worm-gear, being used instead of spring and cam.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown says the arm is fixed in the shaft of the worm wheel. Fixed there it could never fall ahead, so here it is fixed on the hollow shaft instead. The plate, the bearings, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The swing dies away because of a little made-up rubbing.
