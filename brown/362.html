No. 362: Traverse by an oblique groove

A pin rides in a slanting groove cut round a turning cylinder. As the cylinder turns, the groove pushes the pin sideways, first one way and then back. The pin carries a shaft and a drum with it, so they slide to and fro once for every turn.

How it works

Look at the groove on the cylinder. It does not go straight round. It wanders from one end of the cylinder to the other and back again. The pin is held in the groove, so it must follow it.

When the cylinder turns, the part of the groove under the pin moves sideways. The pin goes with it, and takes the upper shaft and the drum along. After one full turn the groove has gone to one end and come back, so the drum has slid out and back once.

Try the gentle slant, then the steep one. A steeper groove wanders further, so the drum slides further each turn.

In Brown's words

Alternating traverse of upper shaft and its drum, produced by pin on the end of the shaft working in oblique groove in the lower cylinder.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The groove is a closed loop round the cylinder, as Brown describes. The upper shaft is kept from turning so the pin always points down. The posts, base, crank and all sizes were added or made up.
