No. 389: Eccentric lifting jack

A jack for lifting heavy things a little at a time. Each swing of the handle pushes a toothed bar up by one tooth. A second hook, called the holding pawl, keeps the bar up while the handle swings back.

How it works

Swing the handle up and the disc lifts its ring and the curved arm. The tip of the arm is under a tooth, so the bar and its load go up too. Near the top, the holding pawl slips past a tooth.

Swing the handle down and the arm sinks. Now the bar would sink as well, but the holding pawl is under a tooth, so the bar rests on it. The tip of the arm slides down over the teeth and drops under the next one. Then the handle is ready to lift again.

The disc is close to the shaft, so a long swing of the handle moves the arm only a little. That is what makes the push strong. Try Short, then Long: a short swing lifts one tooth, a long swing lifts two.

In Brown's words

Lifting-jack operated by an eccentric, pawl, and ratchet. The upper pawl is a stop. :

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing, which shows the stand cut in half. The handle, the back plate that carries the shafts, the number of teeth and all sizes are made up. Gravity, or a small spring, keeps the tips of both pawls against the teeth. When the bar nears the top, both pawls are lifted clear and the bar is let down, so the lifting can start again.
