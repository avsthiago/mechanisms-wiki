No. 442: Pot wheel on the Eisach

A big wheel stands in a river and the current turns it. Pots on its rim dip into the water, fill, and are carried up. At the top they tip out into a trough. The river lifts a little of its own water.

How it works

The river pushes on the pots at the bottom of the wheel. The wheel turns, and the near side goes up. Each pot dips under the water, and its open end faces forward. It scoops water in as it moves.

The pot stays full while it climbs, because its open end points upwards. Near the top it turns over. Its open end points down and backward, and the water pours out into the trough.

Try Slow, then Fast. Count how many pots are emptied in the same time.

In Brown's words

Machine of ancient origin, still employed on the river Eisach, in the Tyrol, for raising water. A current keeping the wheel in motion, the pots on its periphery are successively immersed, filled, and emptied into a trough above the stream.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The posts, the stream bed, the water and the bank are made up. The spokes are drawn on one side only, so the trough can reach inside the rim. The number of pots, their shape and the wheel speeds are chosen to look right, not worked out from real flow.
