No. 365: Rod between skewed rollers

A round rod is gripped between two rollers that cross at a slant. When the rollers turn, the rod slides along and spins at the same time. Feeding a rod this way gives it a steady screw-like motion without cutting a thread on it.

How it works

Both rollers turn, in opposite directions. Where each one touches the rod, its surface moves square to its own axle. Because the axles are tilted, that movement is on a slant across the rod.

A slanting push has two parts. The up or down part slides the rod along. The sideways part rolls the rod round. The front roller pushes the front of the rod one way and the back roller pushes the back of it the other way, so both work together to spin it. The rod does not slip on either roller. So its surface moves just like the rollers' surfaces, and the red mark runs along a screw-shaped path.

The model turns the rollers back each time the rod reaches the end of its travel. Try Level, where the rod only slides. Then try Steep, where it turns fast and slides slowly.

In Brown's words

Cylindrical rod arranged between two rollers, the axes of which are oblique to each other. The rotation of the rollers produces both a longitudinal and a rotary motion of the rod.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows only the rollers and the rod. The tilting brackets, the posts and the feet were made up to hold them, and the sizes are made up too. Real rollers would need springs to squeeze the rod, and a drive to turn them. Here the rollers simply turn back at each end of the rod's travel so it never runs out.
