No. 294: Cylinder escapement

A watch needs something to let its wheels turn one small step at a time. Here a thin tube with half its wall cut away does the job. The teeth of a wheel slip through the tube one by one as it swings.

How it works

The tube is called the cylinder. Half of its wall is cut away where the teeth pass. A spring, not shown, tries to turn the escape wheel, but a tooth is stopped by the outside of the wall. The balance wheel swings the cylinder round until the cut edge passes the tooth. Then the tooth slips into the cylinder.

Each tooth is a wedge. As it slides in, its sloping back pushes on the cut edge and gives the balance a small shove. Then the point of the tooth stops against the inside of the wall. The balance swings back, the other cut edge passes the point, and the tooth slides out. It gives a second shove the other way as it goes. The next tooth then lands on the outside of the wall.

The balance spring pulls the balance back to the middle, and that sets the pace. A stiff spring pulls harder, so the swings are quicker and the wheel turns faster. Try Soft, then Stiff, and count the beats.

In Brown's words

A cylinder escapement. 294 shows the cylinder in perspective, and 295 shows part of the escape-wheel on a large scale, and represents the different positions taken by cylinder, A, B, during an oscillation. The pallets, a, b, c, on the wheel rest alternately on the inside and outside of cylinder. To the top of cylinder is attached the balance-wheel. The wheel pallets are beveled so as to keep up the impulse of balance by sliding against the beveled edge of cylinder.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing and description. Brown draws only the cylinder here. The escape wheel, the balance wheel, the balance spring and its post, the plate and all the sizes were made up. The bridge that holds the top of the balance in a real watch is left out, and so is the spring that drives the wheel. The escape wheel, balance wheel and balance spring are drawn faint so the cylinder shows clearly. Pick one to see it solid. The rubbing that keeps the swing steady is a simple guess.
