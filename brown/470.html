No. 470: Steam hammer

A heavy hammer hangs from a piston inside a steam cylinder. Steam let in under the piston lifts the hammer. When the steam is let out, the hammer falls on the hot iron and flattens it.

How it works

Watch the red valve on the front of the cylinder. When it moves up, steam rushes in under the piston. The steam pushes harder than the hammer weighs, so the hammer rises.

Then the valve moves down and the steam escapes. Nothing holds the hammer up now, so it falls freely, faster and faster, like a dropped stone. It hits the iron with all the speed it gained on the way down.

Try a light tap, then a full blow. A higher lift gives a faster fall and a harder blow. Watch how much thinner the iron gets each time. Choose Closed to see the cylinder as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Steam hammer. Cylinder fixed above and hammer attached to lower end of piston-rod. Steam being alternately admitted below piston and allowed to escape, raises and lets fall the hammer.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The cylinder is drawn see-through, and the steam box is shown as a simple box with one sliding block inside. In a real hammer a worker moves the handle; here the model moves it when the hammer lands and when it reaches the chosen height. The rods to the handle, the anvil block, the hot iron and all the sizes and pressures were made up. The blue dots show the steam: dark when fresh, pale as it leaves.
