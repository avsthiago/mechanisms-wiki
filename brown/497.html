No. 497: Fan blower

A fan spins inside a snail-shaped case. Air is drawn in through round holes in the middle and pushed out of the spout. Blowers like this fed air to forges and furnaces.

How it works

The blades spin inside a snail-shaped case. Air between the blades is carried round with them and flung outwards to the edge, the way mud is flung off a spinning wheel.

As the air is flung out, the middle is left short of air. Fresh air rushes in through the round openings in the sides to fill the gap. Watch the dots come in at the centre and spiral out.

The case gets wider as it goes round, so there is room for more and more air. It all gathers at the spout and is squeezed out. Try a faster speed and watch the air leave faster. Choose Closed to see the blower as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Fan-blower. The casing has circular openings in its sides through which, by the revolution of the shaft and attached fan-blades, air is drawn in at the center of the casing, to be forced out under pressure through the spout.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The front side of the casing is left off so you can see in, as in the drawing. The stand, the bearing and the sizes were made up. The air follows a simple rule, not a full model of the flow.
