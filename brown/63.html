No. 63: Jumping counter wheel

A disc turns slowly and steadily, but the star wheel beside it stands still, then jumps. Each jump moves it on by exactly one point. That makes it easy to count turns, which is why meters and turn counters use it.

How it works

The disc turns all the time. A pin comes up under the yellow drop and lifts its right side slowly. The pawl hanging from the drop rises too. It slides up over one point of the star and falls into the next gap.

Then the pin slips off the end of the drop. Nothing holds the drop up now, so the spring throws it down very fast. The pawl comes down with it and pushes the star round by one point. The star then waits, still, until the next pin arrives.

However slowly the disc turns, each jump is just as quick, so the count is easy to read. Try 1 pin, then 3. Count how many points the star moves for each turn of the disc.

In Brown's words

Jumping or intermittent rotary motion, used for meters and revolution-counters. The drop and attached pawl, carried by a spring at the left, are lifted by pins in the disk at the right. Pins escape first from pawl, which drops into next space of the star-wheel. When pin escapes from drop, spring throws down suddenly the drop, the pin on which strikes the pawl, which, by its action on star-wheel, rapidly gives it a portion of a revolution. This is repeated as each pin passes.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. In Brown's drawing the pins also lift the pawl clear of the star. Here the pawl simply slides up over the point instead. The drop is shaped more simply, and a coil spring stands in for Brown's flat spring. The stop pin above the spring, the plate, the posts and all the sizes were made up.
