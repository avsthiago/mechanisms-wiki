No. 387: Self-levelling wharf steps

A ladder joins a wharf to a floating boat. As the tide goes down, the boat sinks and the ladder slopes more and more. Its steps stay flat the whole time, so they are always safe to stand on.

How it works

At high tide the boat is level with the wharf and the ladder lies flat. As the water falls, the boat sinks and the far end of the ladder goes down. The steps stay flat all the same.

Each step turns on a pin at its back edge, in the lower bar. A rod hangs from the hand rail straight above that pin and holds the front edge. The rod, the step and the bit of post between the bars make a triangle that cannot change shape. The bars only slide that triangle along without turning it, because the posts on the boat stay upright like the posts on the wharf.

Try a small fall of tide, then a large one. The ladder tips further, but the red step stays flat.

In Brown's words

Self-adjusting step-ladder for wharfs at which there are rise and fall of tide. The steps are pivoted at one edge into wooden bars forming string-pieces, and their other edge is supported by rods suspended from bars forming hand-rails. The steps remain horizontal whatever position the ladder assumes.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing, which shows the ladder at high and at low tide. Here the tide moves between the two. The boat is allowed to drift in towards the wharf as the ladder tips, since the ladder cannot stretch. The width of the ladder, the pins, the wharf and all sizes are made up.
