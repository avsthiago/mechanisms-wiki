No. 403: Cyclograph of three rules

Three flat rules fastened together draw a circle arc without using its centre. That helps when the centre would fall far off the paper. Two pins and a pencil are all it needs.

How it works

Mark the two ends of the arc and its highest point. Lay the rules from the ends up to the top point and fasten them, with the brace across. Push a pin in at each end. Now slide the frame so both rules always touch their pins. The pencil in the corner draws the arc.

The corner of the rules never changes its angle. A rule from old geometry says that every point that sees the two pins at the same angle lies on one circle. So the corner can only move along that circle, and the pencil draws part of it.

Try a low arc, then a high one. A higher arc makes a sharper corner and a smaller circle.

In Brown's words

Cyclograph for describing circular arcs in drawings where the center is inaccessible. This is composed of three straight rules. The chord and versed sine being laid down, draw straight sloping lines from ends of former to top of latter, and to these lines lay two of the rules crossing at the apex. Fasten these rules together, and another rule across them to serve as a brace, and insert a pin or point at each end of chord to guide the apparatus, which, on being moved against these points, will describe the arc by means of pencil in the angle of the crossing edges of the sloping rules.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The paper, the size of the rules and the thin guide lines were added. The rules are drawn longer than Brown's so the pins stay on them for most of the arc; the last bit near each pin is not drawn. The pencil sits a little inside the corner so it clears the rules; the pins touch the rules at the same little distance, which keeps the drawn line a true circle.
