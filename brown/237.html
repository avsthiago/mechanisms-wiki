No. 237: Crown ratchet and pawl

A wheel on an upright shaft has sloping teeth standing up round its rim, like a crown. An arm above it swings to and fro and carries a pawl that hangs onto the teeth. Each swing one way pushes the wheel round a few teeth, and the swing back lets the pawl slide over them.

How it works

The arm lies across the top of the wheel and swings to and fro round the shaft. A small plate called a pawl hangs from it and rests in the gap between two teeth.

When the arm swings one way, the pawl pushes on the steep side of a tooth and carries the wheel round. When it swings back, the pawl meets the slope of the next tooth. It rides up, slides over the top and drops into the next gap. The wheel stays where it is. So the wheel only ever turns one way.

Try the Tiny swing. The pawl rises and falls in the same gap and the wheel does not move. Then try Medium and Wide, and count how many teeth the wheel moves on each swing.

In Brown's words

A reciprocating circular motion of the top arm makes its attached pawl produce an intermittent circular motion of the crown-ratchet or rag-wheel.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows the pawl as a curved plate fixed to the arm. Here it is a flat plate hinged to the arm, so that it can lift over the teeth. Its own weight keeps it down. The drum, the bearing, the base, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. A little rubbing in the bearing stops the wheel slipping back.
