No. 138: Cam under a rod

A flat plate with three points turns on its centre. An upright rod rests on its edge and slides up and down in two guides. The rod rises over each point and sinks into each dip, so one turn gives three lifts.

How it works

Watch the pointed foot of the rod. At the start it sits in a dip, where the edge of the cam is closest to the centre. As the cam turns, the edge swings out towards a point, and it pushes the rod up.

When the point passes under the rod, the rod is at its highest. Then the edge falls away again and the rod sinks down. The rise is quick near the points and slow at the dips, so the rod moves at a changing speed. It stops for an instant at the top and at the bottom.

Turn the cam through one full turn and count the lifts: three, one for each point. Try Low, As drawn and High, and watch the lift reading.

In Brown's words

On turning the cam at the bottom a variable alternating rectilinear motion is imparted to the rod resting on it.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The sizes, the curve of the edge, the pointed foot, the two guides, the post, the back plate and the base were made up. Brown draws the points evenly spaced, and so they are here. The faint disc turns with the cam. Brown does not say what keeps the rod down, so it is held only by its weight. The rod height is worked out exactly from the edge of the cam. The choice of point height was added for comparison.
