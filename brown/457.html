No. 457: Well sweep

A long pole rests on a post, with a heavy weight on its short end. The bucket hangs from the long end and is lowered into the well. The weight does part of the lifting, so raising a full bucket is easier.

How it works

The pole tips on the post like a seesaw. The long end carries the bucket. The short end carries a heavy weight. When one end goes down, the other goes up.

A full bucket weighs about twice as much as the weight. An empty bucket weighs less than the weight. So you pull down to sink the empty bucket. The weight then does half the work of lifting it back up.

Try No counterweight, then Half the load, then Full load. Watch the Hand reading when the bucket is full.

In Brown's words

Common mode of raising water from wells of inconsiderable depth. Counterbalance equals about one-half of weight to be raised, so that the bucket has to be pulled down when empty, and is assisted in elevating it when full by counterbalance.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The pole, post, well, rope and the weights of the bucket and counterweight are made up. The up and down swing is driven by a steady rhythm in place of a person. Water is tipped out at the top into a small trough.
