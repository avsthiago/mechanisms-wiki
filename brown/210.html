No. 210: Curved slotted arm and upright bar

Turning a shaft swings a hook-shaped arm with a curved slot. A pin on an upright bar rides in the slot, so the bar slides up and down. Its speed changes even though the shaft turns steadily.

How it works

The shaft turns the curved arm one way and then back, at a steady speed. The pin must stay in the slot, and the guides only let the bar go up and down. So the slot drags the pin, and the bar with it, up or down.

Where the slot runs straight across the top, it mostly slides past the pin. The bar hardly moves. Where the slot curves down the side, it is steep, so it lifts or lowers the pin quickly. The same steady turning gives the bar a changing speed.

Try Straight end, then Curved part, and compare the bar's speed in the readout. With Most of the slot you see the bar creep and then hurry in every swing.

In Brown's words

By turning the shaft carrying the curved slotted arm, a rectilinear motion of variable velocity is given to the vertical bar.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown does not show what turns the shaft, so the model turns it to and fro at a steady speed. The slot does not go all the way round, so the shaft cannot make full turns. The backing plate, the sizes and how far the arm swings were made up. The bar is made longer than drawn so it stays in both guides. The choice of slot part was added to show where the speed changes.
