No. 57: Belts driving two gears and a wandering pinion

One small pulley drives two belts. They turn a big ring and a gear inside it in opposite directions. A small gear between them spins and also travels round the centre, so one handle gives three different motions.

How it works

Turn the crank and both belts move. The plain belt turns the ring the same way as the crank. The crossed belt turns the inner gear the other way. The red pinion sits between them, with its teeth in both.

The ring pushes the pinion one way and the inner gear pushes it the other way. If one push is stronger, the pinion's arm is carried round with it. So the pinion spins on its pin and also wanders round the centre.

Try the small pulley, then Arm stands still, then the big pulley. A bigger pulley makes the inner gear turn more slowly. Watch the arm stop, then creep round the same way as the ring.

In Brown's words

The small pulley at the top being the driver, the large, internally-toothed gear and the concentric gear within will be driven in opposite directions by the bands, and at the same time will impart motion to the intermediate pinion at the bottom, both around its own center and also around the common center of the two concentric gears.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown does not show how the parts are held. The plate, the posts, the spokes behind the ring and the arm that carries the pinion were made up, as were the sizes and the number of teeth. The choice of pulley size on the inner gear was added to show how it steers the arm.
