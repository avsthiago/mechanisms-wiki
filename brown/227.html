No. 227: Chain and chain pulley

A chain runs over a wheel shaped like a star. The points of the star poke into the chain, so the chain cannot slip. Turning the wheel moves the chain down on one side and up on the other.

How it works

The chain has two kinds of link. Flat plates lie in the same plane as the wheel. Between them are rings turned on their side, at right angles to the plates.

Each ring leaves an open space in the chain. On top of the wheel, one point of the star sits in each ring, and the plates rest in the hollows between the points. So the chain cannot slip. When the wheel turns, the points push the rings and the whole chain moves.

The links lie round the wheel as straight pieces, not as a smooth curve. So the chain speeds up and slows down a little as each link comes off. Turn the speed down, follow the red link, and watch the Chain readout rise and fall.

In Brown's words

Represents a chain and chain pulley. The links being in different planes, spaces are left between them for the teeth of the pulley to enter.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows only the wheel and the two hanging ends of the chain. Here the chain is joined into a loop that hangs below the wheel, so it can go round and round. The stand, the shaft and the sizes were made up. The points were given straight sides so they clear the plates as the chain comes off.
