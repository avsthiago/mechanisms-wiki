No. 132: Press with slanting bars

Two slanting bars stand between a top disc and a bottom disc. When the top disc is turned, the bars lean upright and push the bottom disc down. A short twist of the lever gives a strong squeeze, so this was used in presses.

How it works

Swing the lever. It turns the top disc. The top ends of the bars are in holes in that disc, so they move round with it. The bottom ends are in the bottom disc, which cannot turn.

The bars cannot get longer or shorter. So when their top ends come round above their bottom ends, the bars have to stand more upright. That pushes the bottom disc down. The platen drops fast at first, then slower and slower, and the push gets stronger.

Try the full swing, then the half swing. Watch how far the platen goes down each time, and how the bars lean.

In Brown's words

This is a motion which has been used in presses to produce the necessary pressure upon the platen. Horizontal motion is given to the arm of the lever which turns the upper disk. Between the top and bottom disks are two bars which enter holes in the disks. These bars are in oblique positions, as shown in the drawing, when the press is not in operation; but when the top disk is made to rotate, the bars move toward perpendicular positions and force the lower disk down. The top disk must be firmly secured in a stationary position, except as to its revolution.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The frame, the columns, the floor, the block and the piece of metal, the sizes and the bar holes were made up. Brown draws the lever pushed sideways; here it swings round and back so the motion can repeat. Small guide lugs on the platen slide on the columns so it stays level. The top stalk on the beam is only for looks.
