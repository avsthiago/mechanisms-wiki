No. 460: Fairbairn's bailing scoop

A long hollow scoop is hinged on the low bank, with a deep bucket at its far end in a pit of water. A rocking beam on the high bank lifts it with a rod. The water runs back along the scoop and pours out at the hinge, into a channel a little higher than the pit.

How it works

The beam rocks up and down. When its left end is low, the bucket at the end of the scoop sits deep in the pit and fills.

As the beam rises, the rod lifts the scoop about its hinge. Once the scoop slopes down towards the hinge, the water runs back along it and pours out into the channel on the left. That channel is higher than the pit.

Try the rod in the Near notch, then the Far notch. Near the post the beam moves the rod only a little, so the scoop barely tips and pours a trickle. The far notch lifts it high and pours much more.

In Brown's words

Fairbairn's bailing-scoop, for elevating water short distances. The scoop is connected by pitman to end of a lever or of a beam of single-acting engine Distance of lift may be altered by placing end of rod in notches shown in figure.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The sizes, the beam's swing, the channel and the supports are made up. Brown shows the beam of an engine, so a steady rhythm rocks it here. The scoop is left open at the front so the water inside can be seen.
