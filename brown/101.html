No. 101: Swinging slotted bar

A long bar hangs from a pivot at the top and swings from side to side. A pin on a straight horizontal bar sits in a slot in the swinging bar. As the swinging bar moves, it pushes the horizontal bar to and fro in a straight line.

How it works

The top of the long bar is fixed to a pivot, so its lower part swings from side to side. The pin is part of the horizontal bar and has to stay in the slot. The guides let the horizontal bar move only left and right.

When the long bar tilts, the slot pushes the pin sideways. The pin also has to slide along the slot, because the slot gets farther from the pivot as it tilts. The bar moves a little faster when the long bar is tilted a lot. It is slowest when the long bar hangs straight down.

Try Small, then Large. With a small swing the bar moves almost evenly. With a large swing the speed readout changes more from the middle to the ends.

In Brown's words

Rectilinear motion of horizontal bar, by means of vibrating slotted bar hung from the top.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown does not show what moves the long bar, so the model swings it back and forth by its handle. The ceiling, the plate at the pivot, the hangers, the guides and all the sizes were made up. The horizontal bar is drawn round. The different swing sizes were added for comparison.
