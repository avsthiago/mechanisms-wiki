No. 345: Pendulum engine

Steam pushes a piston to and fro inside a cylinder, and a crank turns that push into a spin. Here the cylinder hangs from pegs at its top end and swings like a pendulum. The crank is below it, and the piston rod joins the crank directly.

How it works

Watch the cylinder. It hangs from its top end and swings a little to one side, then the other, once for every turn of the crank. It moves like the weight on a clock pendulum.

The rod is part of the piston, and it is joined to the crank pin. The pin goes round in a circle, so the rod always points at it. The cylinder has to swing to keep up. Because it swings with the rod, nothing pushes the piston sideways, and no guide is needed.

Try a short crank arm, then a long one. The long arm makes the piston travel further, and the cylinder swings more. Choose Closed to see the cylinder as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Inverted oscillating or pendulum engine. The cylinder has trunnions at its upper end and swings like a pendulum. The crank-shaft is below, and the piston-rod connected directly with crank.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The front half of the cylinder is cut away so the piston shows. The two fixed beams, the pegs, the shaft and all the sizes were made up. Steam pipes and valves are left out.
