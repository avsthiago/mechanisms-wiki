No. 494: Stone tongs

Two curved arms cross like scissors and are pulled shut by two links. The tongs close round a block of stone, and the bigger the stone, the wider they open. Lift on the rope and the stone comes up.

How it works

The tongs are lowered over the stone with their jaws open. The rope pulls up on the top joint of the two links. The links pull up on the upper ends of the two arms, and those ends move together. The arms turn on their middle pin, so the lower ends, the jaws, close on the stone.

Once the jaws touch the stone, they cannot close more. Pulling on, the rope lifts the tongs and the stone with them. A heavy stone pulls the tongs down harder. That pulls the links tighter, so the jaws squeeze harder too. The heavier the stone, the harder the tongs bite.

Put the stone down and the rope goes slack. The links drop, the arms swing wide and the jaws let go. Try Narrow, then Wide, and watch how far the jaws open and how high the rope has to go.

In Brown's words

Tongs for lifting stones, etc. The pull on the shackle which connects the two links causes the latter so to act on the upper arms of the tongs as to make their points press themselves against or into the stone. The greater the weight the harder the tongs bite.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The stone is a plain block. The ground, the rope up to the crane, the thickness of the arms and the way the two arms sit one in front of the other were made up. The tongs are held at one height while they close. Real tongs hang from the rope, and a rough stone would help them grip.
