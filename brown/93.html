No. 93: Crank in a slotted yoke

A crank turns a peg round in a circle. The peg slides along a long slot in a yoke. The yoke moves only up and down, and it carries a straight rod with it. No jointed connecting rod is needed.

How it works

The shaft turns, and the red peg goes round in a circle. The peg sits in the slot of the yoke. The slot is long and straight, and it lies across the way the rod moves.

When the peg goes up, it lifts the yoke. When it goes down, it lowers the yoke. When it moves left or right, it just slides along the slot and does not push. So the rod goes only up and down. It is fastest when the peg is level with the shaft, and it stops for a moment at the top and the bottom.

The rod travels twice as far as the peg is from the shaft. Try Near, then As drawn, and watch the travel change.

In Brown's words

Crank motion, with the crank-wrist working in a slotted yoke, thereby dispensing with the oscillating connecting-rod or pitman.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The guides, the plate behind the shaft, the base and all the sizes were made up. The disc is drawn behind the yoke so that the peg can be seen in the slot. The other peg distances were added for comparison.
