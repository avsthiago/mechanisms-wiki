No. 215: Watch stop, fourth kind

A stop that lets a watch spring be wound only so many turns, then blocks the key. A pin on a round plate turns a wavy star wheel one step per turn. One side of the star is left uncut, and that side is what stops the key.

How it works

Turn the key and the plate goes round with it. Once each turn the pin slides into a slot of the star. It turns the star by one slot, then slides out. The bite in the disc gives the points of the star room to swing past.

The rest of the time the round edge of the disc sits in a shallow hollow of the star and holds it still. One side of the star has no hollow. When that side comes round, it hits the edge of the disc just past the bite. Then the key cannot turn any further.

Let it run and watch the turns count up until the key stops. Then it turns back as the spring unwinds, and the same uncut side stops it from the other side. Count the turns between the two stops.

In Brown's words

Other modifications of the stop, the operations of which will be easily understood by a comparison with 212.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown does not say how this stop works. Here it is read as a pin that steps a star wheel, with the uncut side drawn as one of the star's bulging sides. The key, the plate that holds the shafts, the sizes and the depth of the hollows were made up. The bite and the slots were worked out so the pin moves the star exactly one slot and nothing rubs. The spring itself is left out, so the model just turns back to show unwinding.
