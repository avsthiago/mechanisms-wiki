No. 114: Part-toothed pinion and two racks

A small gear, called a pinion, with teeth on only part of its rim turns round and round. It pushes a frame one way, rests a moment, then pushes it back the other way. A steady turn becomes a push, rest, pull, rest.

How it works

The pinion turns clockwise all the time. First its teeth meet the top row. The top of a clockwise wheel moves right, so the frame is pushed right.

Then the last tooth leaves the top row and the frame stops. The smooth part of the rim passes by without touching anything. Half a turn later the first tooth meets the bottom row. The bottom of the wheel moves left, so the frame is pushed back left. Then it rests again.

Each tooth on the pinion moves the frame one tooth along. Try 5 teeth, then 7, and watch how far the frame travels.

In Brown's words

Uniform circular motion into reciprocating rectilinear motion, by means of mutilated pinion, which drives alternately the top and bottom rack.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The teeth cover less than half of the pinion's rim, so that one row lets go before the other row is reached; otherwise the frame would jam. The posts, the base, the plate behind, the end rods, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The choice of 5, 6 or 7 teeth was added for comparison.
