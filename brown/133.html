No. 133: Sector press

A hand crank turns a small gear. Its teeth drive a larger toothed wheel, called a sector, which swings about its shaft. A long rod links the sector to a flat block above, called the platen, and pushes it up and down. A press like this squeezes with great force.

How it works

Swing the crank. The small gear turns, and its teeth push the teeth of the sector. The sector swings about its shaft. The rod is pinned near the sector's edge, so it moves up and down as the sector swings.

The rod lifts the platen when the sector swings one way and lets it down when it swings back. The sector is much bigger than the pinion, so the crank turns many times for one swing of the sector. A long, easy swing of the handle gives a short, strong push.

Try Small, then Medium, then Large. The sector swings the same amount each time. Watch how far the crank has to turn.

In Brown's words

A simple press motion is given through the hand-crank on the pinion-shaft; the pinion communicating motion to the toothed sector, which acts upon the platen, by means of the rod which connects it therewith.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The frame, the back plate and the base, the sizes, the number of teeth and the length of the crank were made up. Brown draws no work under the platen, so none is shown. Brown shows the crank turning; here it swings back and forth so the sector can swing. The choice of pinion size was added to show what it changes.
