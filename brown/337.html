No. 337: Parallel motion with a short rod

The end of a rocking beam moves on a curve, but a piston rod must go straight up and down. A short hanging rod and a sideways radius rod pull the curve almost straight. The piston rod hangs from near the middle of the short rod.

How it works

The end of the beam swings on a curve. At the top and bottom of its swing it moves to the right. The radius rod swings on a curve too. At the ends of its swing it moves to the left.

The short rod joins the two ends. Its top is pulled right and its bottom is pulled left, by about the same amount. So a point near its middle hardly moves sideways at all. That is where the piston rod hangs, and it goes up and down in an almost straight line.

The line is only nearly straight. Look at the Off line reading. Try Small, then Large, and watch the dark path open into a thin figure of eight. Choose Closed to see the cylinder as it looks from outside.

In Brown's words

Parallel motion in which the radius rod is connected with the lower end of a short vibrating rod, the upper end of which is connected with the beam, and to the center of which the piston-rod is connected.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows only the beam end and the rods. The cylinder, the piston, the posts and bearings, the floor and all sizes were made up. The beam is rocked to and fro as if by a crank on its far end, which is left out. The piston rod is fixed a little above the middle of the short rod, at the point that gives the straightest line for these rod lengths.
