No. 60: Two speeds by belts

Two belts from two drums can turn a shaft at two speeds. One sliding bar moves both belts at once. Only one belt drives at a time, while the other runs on a loose pulley.

How it works

The top shaft carries a big drum and a small drum. Each drum has its own belt down to a pair of pulleys. In each pair the outer pulley is loose and the inner one is fixed to the lower shaft.

A belt moves its pulley's edge as fast as its drum's edge. The small drum drives a big pulley, so the lower shaft turns slower than the drums. The big drum drives a pulley of the same size, so the lower shaft keeps up with the drums.

To change speed, both belts move one pulley to the right. One belt comes off its fixed pulley as the other goes on, so they never fight. Try Slow, then Fast, and watch the lower shaft.

In Brown's words

For transmitting two speeds by means of belts. There are four pulleys on the lower shaft, the two outer ones being loose and the two inner ones fast. The band to the left is shown on its loose pulley, the one to the right on its fast one; a slow motion is consequently transmitted to lower shaft. When band to the right is moved on to its loose pulley, and left-hand one on to its fast pulley, a quicker motion is transmitted.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing and description. The sizes, the bearings and the frame are made up. Brown does not show how the belts are moved. Both move the same way to change speed, so here one bar with two forks moves them together.
