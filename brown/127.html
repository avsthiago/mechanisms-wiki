No. 127: Air-pump racks

A long handle rocks a toothed wheel back and forth. The wheel pushes two toothed bars, called racks, one on each side. One rack rises while the other sinks, so two pumps work in turn, one pushing air as the other draws it in.

How it works

Rock the handle and the wheel turns one way, then back. Its teeth push on the teeth of both racks at once. The rack on the right goes up while the rack on the left goes down. Then they swap.

Each rack is joined to a rod and a disc, called a piston, that fits a tube. When a piston goes down it pushes air out of its tube. When it goes up it draws air in. The two pumps always work in opposite turns, so a pump is always pushing air.

Try Short, then Long. Watch how far the discs slide inside the tubes. Choose Closed to see the pumps as they look from outside.

In Brown's words

Motion used in air-pumps. On vibrating the lever fixed on the same shaft with the spur-gear, reciprocating rectilinear motion is imparted to the racks on each side, which are attached to the pistons of two pumps, one rack always ascending while the other is descending.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown shows the wheel, the handle and the two racks. The plate and base that hold the shafts, the guide rails behind the racks, the rods, discs and tubes for the pumps, the sizes and the number of teeth were made up. The choice of how far the handle rocks was added to show what it changes.
