No. 437: Volute wheel

Water is fed into a spiral-shaped casing that curls round a wheel. It sweeps round the wheel and pushes on the little vanes all the way. Then it falls out through slanted buckets near the middle, which push the wheel a second time.

How it works

Water comes in along the channel at the top and turns clockwise round the wheel. The casing curls in closer and closer, so the water is always right beside the vanes.

The water pushes on the vanes and the wheel goes round the same way. Then the water slips inward and falls out through the slanted buckets. As it drops through them it gives the wheel a second push.

Try a low flow, then a high one. The wheel turns faster with more water.

In Brown's words

Volute wheel, having radial vanes, a, against which the water impinges and carries the wheel around. The scroll or volute casing, b, confines the water in such a manner that it acts against the vanes all around the wheel. By the addition of the inclined buckets, c, c, at the bottom, the water is made to act with additional force as it escapes through the openings of said buckets.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing, which is a view from above. The floor under the casing, the shaft, the thickness of the walls and the sizes of the vanes and buckets are made up. The wheel speed follows the flow by a simple rule.
