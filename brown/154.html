No. 154: Studs, bell crank and weight

A disc with studs turns round and round. Each stud pushes down on one end of a lever bent at a corner, called a bell crank. A weight on a rope pulls the lever back, so the weight rises and falls again and again.

How it works

The disc turns clockwise. Each stud comes round to the lower arm of the lever and pushes it down. The lever turns, and its upper arm pulls the rope. The rope runs over the pulley and lifts the weight.

The stud keeps pushing until it slides off the end of the arm. Then nothing holds the lever. The weight falls and pulls the lever back to where it started. It stops against a small pin. The next stud then comes round and pushes again.

Try 1 stud, then 2, then 3. With more studs the weight is lifted more often. Watch the readout count the pushes.

In Brown's words

Circular motion into alternating rectilinear motion, by the action of the studs on the rotary disk upon one end of the bell-crank, the other end of which has attached to it a weighted cord passing over a pulley.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The shaft, the stand for the disc, the upright for the lever, the post for the pulley, the base and the small stop pin that holds the lever up were made up. Brown does not say how far the lever turns. Here it turns as far as each stud pushes it. The weight falls freely, so it does not slow down before it stops.
