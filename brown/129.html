No. 129: Chinese windlass

A rope winds onto the thick part of a barrel while it unwinds from the thin part. A pulley with a hook hangs in the loop of rope between them. The hook rises only a little for each turn of the handle, so it can lift a very heavy load.

How it works

Turn the handle. The barrel turns and the rope winds onto the thick half. At the same moment it unwinds from the thin half. The pulley hangs in the loop between them, so it is pulled up.

The thick half takes in more rope than the thin half lets out. The loop gets shorter by the difference, and that shortening is shared by its two sides. So the hook rises by half the difference between the two lengths of rope for each turn.

A small rise for each turn means a small hand force can lift a big load. Try the thin half Almost as thick, then Much thinner, and watch the hook rise and the number of turns.

In Brown's words

Chinese windlass. This embraces the same principles as the micrometer screw 111. The movement of the pulley in every revolution of the windlass is equal to half the difference between the larger and smaller circumferences of the windlass barrel.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The two posts, the base, the crank and the sizes were made up. Brown shows no handle. The rope coils on the barrel stay the same as it turns; only the lengths of rope that hang down are worked out. The rope leans slightly from the barrel to the middle of the pulley. The turns shown are chosen so the hook stays clear of the barrel.
