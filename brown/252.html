No. 252: Two rollers moved by oblique slots

Two rollers sit side by side in a straight slot. A frame with two slanting slots slides up and down below them. Each slanting slot pushes its roller sideways, so the two rollers move apart and together by the same amount.

How it works

The frame moves straight up and down. The rollers can only move left and right, because their pins sit in the level slot. Each pin also sits in a slanting slot in the frame.

When the frame rises, the slanting slots lean in toward each other and push the pins apart. When the frame sinks, the slots pull the pins back together. Both slots lean the same amount, so both rollers move the same distance.

A slot that leans more pushes the rollers further for the same frame stroke. Try the three slot angles and watch how far apart the rollers get.

In Brown's words

A and B are two rollers which require to be equally moved to and fro in the slot, C. This is accomplished by moving the piece, D, with oblique slotted arms, up and down.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The slanting slots, the sliding rod and its guide, the stand and the thickness of the plates were made up. The roller pins stick out behind the frame so you can see them in the slots. The rollers are kept clear of each other at every setting.
