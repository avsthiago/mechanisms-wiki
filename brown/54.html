No. 54: Star wheel

A small gear turns steadily one way, yet the big wheel turns one way and then back again. The small gear works on the front of a ring of bars, then slips through a gap and works on the back. Mangles, machines that press wet washing between rollers, used this to roll the cloth back and forth.

How it works

Turn the crank and the small gear, called a pinion, turns with it. It lies across the ring with its teeth between the bars. On the front of the ring it pushes the bars sideways, so the wheel turns.

When the last bar reaches the pinion, the pinion swings round that bar into the gap and comes out behind the ring. Now its teeth push on the back of the bars. It still turns the same way, but the back of the bars moves the opposite way, so the wheel goes back. A minus sign in the readout means the other way. The pinion's shaft slides in the slot of the green plate to change sides.

Try Nearly all round, then Half way round. Watch how far the wheel turns before it goes back.

In Brown's words

Mangle or star-wheel, for producing an alternating rotary motion.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown draws the ring with no gap, but the pinion needs one to get from the front to the back, so a gap was cut and the outer rim split there. Brown also draws a second small gear on the left; the drawing does not show what it does, so it is left out. The crank, the slotted plate, the post behind the wheel, the base, the sizes and the number of bars were made up. The half ring was added to show what the length of the ring changes.
