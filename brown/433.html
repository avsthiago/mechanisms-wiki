No. 433: Horizontal overshot water-wheel

The wheel lies flat on an upright shaft. A jet of water from a sloping pipe hits its slanted blades and pushes them round. The shaft can turn a millstone directly, with no gears.

How it works

Water runs down a sloping pipe and shoots out as a jet. The jet hits the blades at the front of the wheel. Each blade is slanted, so the water pushes it sideways, along the circle.

As one blade moves away, the next one meets the jet. Used water drops off the ends of the blades to the floor. The wheel spins flat, and the shaft above it spins with it.

Try Low flow, then High flow. Watch the jet and the wheel speed.

In Brown's words

Horizontal overshot water-wheel.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. The beam, its two posts, the floor, the support under the pipe and the water are made up. The number and slant of the blades are chosen to look right. The water is drawn as drops, and the wheel speeds are chosen to look right, not worked out from real flow.
