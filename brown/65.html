No. 65: Tappet and studs with a stop lever

A wheel that turns all the time moves a second wheel on by one small step in each turn. Between steps the second wheel is locked, so it cannot drift. Counters and machines that feed work forward in steps use this.

How it works

The driving disc turns all the time. Once in each turn its arm sweeps over the stud wheel, catches a stud and pushes it down. That moves the stud wheel on by exactly one stud. Then the arm swings clear and the stud wheel stops.

While the stud wheel waits, the tooth of the stop lever sits between two studs. The lever cannot tip, because its other end is pressed against the rim of the driving disc. Just as the arm starts to push, a notch in the rim reaches the lever. A stud shoves the tooth down and the lever's other end rises into the notch. As the notch moves on, its sloping side pushes that end down again, and the tooth comes back up in front of the next stud.

So the stud wheel needs as many turns of the driver as it has studs to go round once. Try 8 studs, then 12, and count the turns for one trip of the red stud.

In Brown's words

The left-hand disk or wheel, C, is the driving-wheel, upon which is fixed the tappet, A. The other disk or wheel, D, has a series of equidistant studs projecting from its face. Every rotation of the tappet acting upon one of the studs in the wheel, D, causes the latter wheel to move the distance of one stud. In order that this may not be exceeded, a lever-like stop is arranged on a fixed center. This stop operates in a notch cut in wheel, C, and at the instant tappet, A, strikes a stud, said notch faces the lever. As wheel, D, rotates, the end between studs is thrust out, and the other extremity enters the notch; but immediately on the tappet leaving stud, the lever is again forced up in front of next stud, and is there held by periphery of C pressing on its other end.

This model is rebuilt from Brown's drawing. Brown does not give sizes, so the sizes of the discs, the arm, the notch and the lever, the shape of the tooth and the upright plate that holds it all were made up. The choice of 8 or 12 studs was added to show what it changes.
